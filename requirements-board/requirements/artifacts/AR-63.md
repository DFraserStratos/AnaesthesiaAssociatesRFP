How a procedure's money moves from our system into AA's trust accounting in Xero, read left to right, and how our system's ledger mirrors Xero. On the left, our system holds each procedure and raises its invoice first, with a linked receivable and payable in its own ledger, and only then submits it to Xero; on the right, Xero holds four accounts as Greg drew them on the whiteboard: the trust account (AA's one bank account), Sales, and the Debtors and Creditors control accounts, each with its sub-ledger (payers and Contract holders; each anaesthetist, plus the AA Holding account, a creditor that is never paid itself). Above Xero, outside AA, are the payers who pay in and the anaesthetists' own bank accounts the payouts land in. Every entry is marked as one of four kinds wherever it appears: a receivable (a payer owes AA), a payable (AA owes an anaesthetist or the AA Holding account), cash (money in the trust account) or a sale. The steps work through an ordinary $100 invoice for Dr Nick and a $200 prepayment for a procedure Dr Ben does, each starting in our system with nothing in Xero, then the balance check before every payment run: each sub-ledger equals its control account, and our ledger equals Xero. Still open: when the payable to the AA Holding account is created (drawn on payment, as on the whiteboard; the catalogue creates it with the prepayment invoice, OQ-80), what releases the transfer (OQ-125), the buyer-created tax invoice (OQ-29), the payment cycle (OQ-47), and who sends the invoice to the payer, which is for the build to decide.
