How a procedure's money moves from our system into AA's trust accounting in Xero, read left to right, and how our system's ledger mirrors Xero. On the left, our system holds each procedure and raises its invoice first, with a linked receivable and payable in its own ledger, and only then submits it to Xero. On the right, Xero holds the bank feed, where each receipt waits until it is matched, and four accounts drawn as T-accounts: the trust account (AA's one bank account), the Clearing account, and the Debtors and Creditors control accounts, each with its sub-ledger (payers and Contract holders; each anaesthetist, plus the AA Holding account, a creditor that is never paid itself). Every invoice and its bill post to Xero together through the Clearing account, so it always reads $0, and money left in it shows the two systems are out of step. Above Xero, outside AA, are the payers who pay in and the anaesthetists' own bank accounts the payouts land in. Every entry is marked as one of four kinds wherever it appears: a receivable (a payer owes AA; an invoice in Xero), a payable (AA owes an anaesthetist or the AA Holding account; a bill in Xero), cash (money in the trust account) or clearing. The steps work through an ordinary $100 invoice for Dr Nick and a $200 prepayment for a procedure Dr Ben does, each starting in our system with nothing in Xero, then the weekly payment run: first the balance check (Clearing reads $0, each sub-ledger equals its control account, our ledger equals Xero, and no bank lines are left unmatched), then one batch that pays each anaesthetist into his own bank account. Still open: when the bill to the AA Holding account posts (drawn with the invoice), how a posted bill is kept from being paid early, how Xero posts the move to Dr Ben and what releases it (OQ-125), the buyer-created tax invoice (OQ-29), the payment cycle (OQ-47), and who sends the invoice to the payer.
