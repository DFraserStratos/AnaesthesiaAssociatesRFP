The billing and invoice flow from Booking to settled payment, in four stages, revised 5 November to the flat Contract model. At booking setup the office gives each Procedure one Contract on the step rail, saying where the invoice goes and then which Contract (the RVG code is optional, the procedure is the text as received, and a Procedure with none shows "Needs an invoice destination"). A Contract is one priced item: its holder Party and whether it bills that holder or the payer on the Booking, a pricing kind (RVG at the anaesthetist's rate, at its own rate or less a discount, a fixed price in dated price ranges, or an open price typed on the Procedure), whether it is prepaid (offered under Payment type: Prepaid, never an RVG price), whether the anaesthetist may override an RVG price, its AA code, specialties and Active or Disabled, and an optional holder reference code, invoice description, default RVG code and funding tag. The engine checks whether a prepayment is required and outstanding, which never blocks the procedure. If it is, the prepayment invoice is generated when an admin approves it (an optional step AA can switch off): its pair is a receivable from the payer on the Booking and a payable to the AA Holding account, mirrored to Xero; it goes out with a prepayment letter, is paid and held in the AA Holding account (the money sits in AA's trust account), is reconciled, and the requirement is re-checked. A move to another anaesthetist changes nothing in the ledger. Cancelled before the procedure, a prepayment is credited if unpaid or refunded from the AA Holding account if paid, raised automatically and paid from the system (OQ-115). After the procedure the anaesthetist records the billing data (on an RVG price the RVG code, times and modifiers; on a fixed or open price an RVG code for reference only), may change the Contract until the List is AUTHORISED (which clears any typed price), and may override the price only where an RVG Contract allows it, never a fixed price (OQ-117) or a prepaid one (OQ-96), with no reason and flagged at review; they mark each Booking complete and submit the List. The office reviews and corrects it, the anaesthetist can still change it, and the last change stands. Authorising locks the whole List, takes the pricing snapshot (Contracts are edited in place, so it copies their settings and the price as they then were) and hands it to the Billing/Invoice Engine as one unit; after that, corrections are additional invoices or credits, never edits. The engine bills the holder Party or the payer, applies the multi-procedure rule to RVG prices, calculates each fee by its pricing kind and leaves prepaid Procedures off the final invoice, mixed Bookings included: nothing is deducted, and the final invoice covers only the Procedures that are not prepaid (OQ-116). Each prepaid Procedure's prepayment is instead released from the AA Holding account to whoever did the List by a journal transfer, not a payment, its trigger still open (OQ-125); once the prepayment invoice is raised the prepaid price is read-only, and any difference is a second invoice or credit raised by hand (OQ-96). The engine then creates a linked receivable and payable in the internal ledger, generates one invoice per billable party per Booking and sends the pair to Xero (ACCREC, draft ACCPAY). The engine sends the invoice by email or post to the holder Party's or the payer's address (shown so for explanation: whether our system or Xero sends invoices is for the Stratos dev team to decide at build); a payment detected in Xero is reconciled to the ledger and the payable is authorised for exactly the amount received, any remaining balance staying outstanding. The weekly payables run and the approval of a period's BCTIs are not yet confirmed (OQ-47).
