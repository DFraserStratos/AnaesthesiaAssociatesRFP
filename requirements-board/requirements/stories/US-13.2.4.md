The admin dashboard shows one reconciliation status that says plainly whether the books are square or not. It checks every account that should balance: the accounts that should read zero, each sub-ledger against its control account, the internal ledger against Xero, and bank lines still unmatched in Xero.

Beside it, the dashboard lists each item still to resolve, such as a receipt nobody has placed, with its amount and how long it has been outstanding.

This is so the office, and the director who approves a payment run, can see at a glance whether everything balances, and so nothing unresolved is left to grow from week to week.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Square.** Given every account that should balance does, and no bank line is unmatched, when an admin opens the dashboard, then the status says the books are square.
- **Out of balance.** Given any account that should balance does not, then the status says so and names the account and the difference.
- **Unresolved items.** Given a receipt that has not been matched, then it is listed with its amount and how long it has been outstanding.

## Technical discussion

Which accounts it checks follows the posting design Greg and Sam are drawing: in their proposal each invoice and its bill post through a clearing account that should always be zero, so money left in it is the first sign of trouble. Where a receipt nobody can place is parked, left open on Xero's bank reconciliation or moved to a holding account there, is not settled ([bulk remittance](US-10.2.4)).

## Notes

Greg, 10 November, with AA's accountant: "we want some fairly straightforward, very obvious controls on a dashboard that show that all of the accounts that should balance to zero are balancing to zero. So you've got this little reconciliation box that says, 'Yeah, we're good,' or 'No, we're not.'" It is on the dashboard of the system, not Xero's. On unresolved items: "why is it 3 weeks since this was carried in the reconciliation and why hasn't it been resolved?"; "it should come up in the dashboard as an alert that it's an item to be resolved." Who acts on them was debated: Greg would have whoever signs the payment run ask, "why am I signing this check with all of this unallocated funds sitting over here?"; Michael sees resolving them as management's job.

Our reading: which accounts the status checks (the sub-ledgers against their control accounts, our ledger against Xero, unmatched bank lines), and what it shows when out of balance.
