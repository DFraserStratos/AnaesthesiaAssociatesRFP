The prepaid amount is the fixed price on the [anaesthetist's own first-party Contract](US-04.2.14) line for the procedure. When the office sets up the Booking, it [selects that Contract](US-04.3.4) for the procedure, and the amount follows from it: nobody estimates it or works it out by hand.

It is always the full amount. There is no partial prepayment or deposit, and the system has no concept of "100% of the procedure": it is simply the prepaid amount.

This is because each anaesthetist must set their own prices; AA cannot set a common price for its members.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given a Booking that needs prepayment, when the office selects the anaesthetist's own first-party Contract for the procedure, then the prepaid amount is that Contract line's fixed price.
- Given a prepaid amount, then it is the whole amount: no part payment or deposit can be set.

## Notes

The directors' answer of 6 October ("Anaesthetist will create and manage their own fixed price contracts") replaces OQ-04's earlier answer, an estimate of base, time and two modifier units at the anaesthetist's unit value. The 7 October client meeting agreed: "every anaesthetist always has a defined amount: this is how much the prepayment is." OQ-38 (two contingency modifier units in the estimate) no longer arises.

OQ-76 answered: there are no partial prepayments for anaesthetics. Ben: "I don't know anyone doing it for anaesthetics at the moment." It can be added later if a need appears.

The office creates and maintains these Contracts, from the price list each anaesthetist supplies; anaesthetists do not edit Contracts in the app (OQ-91, answered 8 October). What happens when a prepaid procedure has no price in the Contract is OQ-92.
