The office sets a Procedure's prepaid amount by picking a prepaid Contract, normally an item on the [anaesthetist's own price list](US-04.2.14) or a surgeon's, or by typing an amount on Prepaid · Fixed price ([a prepaid amount from outside any price list](US-06.2.6)). The prepaid amount is the Procedure's price when its prepayment invoice is raised: the [price range](US-04.2.15) in force on the procedure date, or the typed amount.

This is because each anaesthetist must set their own prices; AA cannot set a common price for its members.

- The prepaid amount is always the full amount, never a percentage of the procedure: there is no partial prepayment or deposit.
- A prepaid Contract's prices are above zero.
- Once the prepayment invoice is raised the amount is read-only; a difference is a credit or an additional invoice.

## Acceptance criteria

- **From the price range.** Given the office picks a fixed-price prepaid Contract, when the prepayment invoice is raised, then the prepaid amount is the price of the range in force on the procedure date.
- **Full amount.** Given a prepaid amount, then it is the whole amount: no part payment or deposit can be set.
- **Above zero.** Given a prepaid Contract, when a price range of 0 or no price is saved, then it is refused.
- **Read-only.** Given the prepayment invoice is raised, then the prepaid amount cannot be changed.

## Notes

Our reading (contract model proposal A16): the amount is read-only once the prepayment invoice is raised.

The directors' answer of 6 October ("Anaesthetist will create and manage their own fixed price contracts") replaces OQ-04's earlier answer, an estimate of base, time and two modifier units at the anaesthetist's unit value; OQ-38 (two contingency modifier units) no longer arises. The 7 October client meeting agreed: "every anaesthetist always has a defined amount: this is how much the prepayment is."

OQ-76 answered: there are no partial prepayments for anaesthetics. Ben: "I don't know anyone doing it for anaesthetics at the moment." It can be added later if a need appears. OQ-92 answered: every prepaid price is above zero; Greg, 28 October: "a non-zero price is mandatory on a prepayment contract line." OQ-91 answered 8 October: the office creates these Contracts from the price list each anaesthetist supplies; anaesthetists do not edit Contracts in the app.

Donald, 16 October: "There needs to be some human in the loop that places [it] on the contract to then trigger the prepayment." Greg: "if a booking's made using that contract, then it does invoke a prepay procedure. But it's not the procedure to set it up, it's the procedure to bill the customer." Greg, 14 October: "technically it's an estimate, but it's the anaesthetist's price."

A new price range does not move a prepaid Procedure once its prepayment invoice is raised (Donald, 19 October). Ben, 27 October: "You would honour the money that's been prepaid, because that's the quote that the patient's been given" (OQ-104; a surgeon's non-prepaid fixed price and third-party price lists were not discussed).

Greg, 28 October: a prepaid Contract can be an anaesthetist's own price list or a surgeon's, identified in the Contract by practitioner ID, "because we can use this exact same mechanism for surgeon's... arrangements". A surgeon's arrangement is held against individual surgeons, not the rooms, and the office picks it from the Contract list filtered by practitioner ID (OQ-105, answered 28 October); one Contract can be linked to many surgeons (Donald, 29 October), which D4's linked practitioners keep.

Ben, 5 November: a prepaid amount need not come from a price list: "just make it two grand" ([a prepaid amount from outside any price list](US-06.2.6)).
