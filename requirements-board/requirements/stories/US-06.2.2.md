The prepaid amount is the fixed price for the procedure on the line of the prepaid Contract the office picks, normally the [anaesthetist's own price list](US-04.2.14) or a surgeon's price list; the Contract identifies whose list it is by the practitioner ID of the anaesthetist, or of each surgeon it is linked to (one Contract can cover several surgeons), and the office picks from the [Contracts filtered by those practitioner IDs](US-04.3.2). When the office sets up the Booking, it [selects that Contract](US-04.3.4) for the procedure, and the amount follows from it: nobody estimates it or works it out by hand. A prepay line must carry a price above zero: the price is mandatory.

It is always the full amount. There is no partial prepayment or deposit, and the system has no concept of "100% of the procedure": it is simply the prepaid amount.

This is because each anaesthetist must set their own prices; AA cannot set a common price for its members.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given a Booking that needs prepayment, when the office selects the prepaid Contract for the procedure, then the prepaid amount is that Contract line's fixed price.
- Given a prepaid amount, then it is the whole amount: no part payment or deposit can be set.
- Given a prepaid Contract line, when it is saved without a price or with a price of 0, then it is refused.

## Notes

The directors' answer of 6 October ("Anaesthetist will create and manage their own fixed price contracts") replaces OQ-04's earlier answer, an estimate of base, time and two modifier units at the anaesthetist's unit value. The 7 October client meeting agreed: "every anaesthetist always has a defined amount: this is how much the prepayment is." OQ-38 (two contingency modifier units in the estimate) no longer arises.

OQ-76 answered: there are no partial prepayments for anaesthetics. Ben: "I don't know anyone doing it for anaesthetics at the moment." It can be added later if a need appears.

The office creates and maintains these Contracts, from the price list each anaesthetist supplies; anaesthetists do not edit Contracts in the app (OQ-91, answered 8 October). OQ-92 answered: the line always has a price.

Greg (14 October): "technically it's an estimate, but it's the anaesthetist's price." A prepaid Procedure keeps the price on its issued prepayment invoice when the Contract's price changes before the procedure: a new Contract version does not relink it (Donald, 19 October). Ben confirmed this for a prepaid Procedure on 27 October: "You would honour the money that's been prepaid, because that's the quote that the patient's been given" (OQ-104; a surgeon's non-prepaid fixed price and third-party price lists were not discussed). Greg, 28 October: "a non-zero price is mandatory on a prepayment contract line." A surgeon's arrangement is held against individual surgeons, not the rooms, and the office picks it from the Contract list filtered by practitioner ID (OQ-105, answered 28 October); it is one Contract that can be linked to many surgeons (Donald, 29 October).
