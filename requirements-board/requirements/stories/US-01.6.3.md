An admin assigns a Draft List to an anaesthetist by choosing the anaesthetist (the AM or PM Slot is predefined on the draft list). The Draft List becomes an [active List](FT-07.1) in that Slot, keeping anything already recorded on it, and stops being shown as unassigned. The [availability finder](US-01.4.2) helps find who is free.

The office does not assign a Draft List onto an unavailable Slot: the Slot must first be made available, with the anaesthetist's agreement. An assignment to a [not-preferred pairing](US-01.3.5) shows a soft warning and can still go ahead.

This is so every Draft List ends up with an anaesthetist, without the office re-keying it.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Office assigns.** A Draft List is never put to anaesthetists as an offer; only the office assigns it.
- **Becomes active.** After assignment the item is an active List in the chosen Slot with exactly one surgeon and one hospital ([one surgeon, one hospital](US-01.3.1)).
- **Slot must be free.** A Slot holds one List; an occupied Slot is not offered without the admin resolving it.
- **Slot must be available.** Given an anaesthetist's Slot is marked unavailable, then the Draft List is not assigned to it until the Slot has been made available with the anaesthetist's agreement.
- **Audited.** The assignment is recorded in the audit trail (US-13.5.2).

## Notes

When an admin opens a Draft List they see everybody potentially available, the same way anaesthetists see available colleagues (Donald; Greg: "Absolutely. Great.").
