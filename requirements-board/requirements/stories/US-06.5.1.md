A prepayment is not paid to the anaesthetist. It is credited to the AA Holding account, an accounts payable prepay account, which the internal ledger treats like an anaesthetist's account: the other side of the [prepayment invoice's pair](US-06.3.1) is the holding account instead of an anaesthetist. Its balance is all the deposits received for procedures not yet done. The holding account sits within AA's trust accounting.

The holding account is not a bank account and not a Xero ledger account: it is a creditor in the payables sub-ledger, beside each anaesthetist, that receives prepayments and transfers them to the anaesthetist who did the procedure, but is never paid itself. The trust account is the bank account the money sits in.

Prepayment is two steps. First the money is collected into the holding account. Then, once the procedure is done and the office authorises the List ([nothing further after a prepaid procedure](US-06.4.1)), a second pair transfers the money (an accounting move, not a payment) from the holding account to whichever anaesthetist did the List.

A cancelled prepayment that was paid is refunded to the patient from the holding account ([cancellation of prepayment procedures](US-06.5.2)). Money leaves it only by a transfer or a refund the system raises, never by hand outside it. Payments are made in Xero, which the system tracks (OQ-111).

This is so refunds and prepayments are reconciled in one place, instead of loosely across AA's books and each anaesthetist's, and so a prepaid Booking that changes anaesthetist leaves nothing to amend.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given a paid prepayment, when the procedure has not happened, then nothing is paid to the anaesthetist.
- Given a prepayment invoice, then its pair is a receivable from that invoice's payer and a payable to the AA Holding account.
- Given the holding account, then its balance is the total of the prepayments received for procedures not yet done.
- Given the holding account, then no money leaves it except by a transfer or a refund the system raises.

## Technical discussion

How Xero posts the move from the holding account to the anaesthetist is still open. Journals cannot easily be raised through Xero's API, which is why the holding account is a creditor rather than a ledger account: a move between two creditors can be made by the system without hand-keyed journals.

Other ways to hold prepayments were weighed: a liability account in the general ledger (no breakdown of whose money it is, and another account to reconcile), a second AA bank account (separation, but a second account to control), and a prepaid sub-account for each anaesthetist. One holding creditor stands for now, to be seen working before it is settled.

In Xero a posted bill is an approved bill, so the holding account's bill would show in the bills to pay although it is never paid; how it is kept out of the payment run is still open. If its bill posts with the prepayment invoice, as the [trust account diagram](AR-63#holding-account) draws it so the clearing account stays at zero, then by our reading Xero's creditor balance includes prepayments invoiced but not yet paid, while this story's balance counts only those received.
