A prepayment is not paid to the anaesthetist. It is credited to the AA Holding account, an accounts payable prepay account, which the internal ledger treats like an anaesthetist's account: the other side of the [prepayment invoice's pair](US-06.3.1) is the holding account instead of an anaesthetist. Its balance is all the deposits received for procedures not yet done. The holding account sits within AA's trust accounting.

The holding account is not a bank account and not a Xero ledger account: it is a creditor in the payables sub-ledger, beside each anaesthetist, that receives prepayments and transfers them to the anaesthetist who did the procedure, but is never paid itself. The trust account is the bank account the money sits in.

Prepayment is two steps. First the money is collected into the holding account. Then, once the procedure is done and the office authorises the List ([nothing further after a prepaid procedure](US-06.4.1)), a second pair transfers the money (a journal transfer, an accounting move, not a payment) from the holding account to whichever anaesthetist did the List.

A cancelled prepayment that was paid is refunded to the patient from the holding account ([cancellation of prepayment procedures](US-06.5.2)), and payments out of it are made from the system, not by hand outside it.

This is so refunds and prepayments are reconciled in one place, instead of loosely across AA's books and each anaesthetist's, and so a prepaid Booking that changes anaesthetist leaves nothing to amend.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given a paid prepayment, when the procedure has not happened, then nothing is paid to the anaesthetist.
- Given a prepayment invoice, then its pair is a receivable from the payer named on the Booking and a payable to the AA Holding account.
- Given the holding account, then its balance is the total of the prepayments received for procedures not yet done.
- Given the holding account, then no payment is made out of it except by the system (a transfer or a refund).
