When a Procedure's Contract is a [fixed fee](US-04.2.4), the system uses the fixed fee as the total price for the procedure, not the BTM units.

Base, time and modifier units are not required on a fixed price; where the anaesthetist records them, they are kept for reference.

A fixed price never allows the anaesthetist's price override, whatever the Contract's flag says: the anaesthetist sees the price read-only, and to depart from it they [change the Contract](US-03.4.1) or ask the office. Only [the office's price change](US-05.4.2) can change a fixed price on a Procedure.

On a fixed-price Contract the system calculates nothing further. If the recorded BTM would come to more or less than the fixed price, no additional invoice or credit is raised automatically. Where the work was very different, the anaesthetist or the office may raise an [ad hoc invoice](US-08.6.3) or [credit note](US-08.6.5) by hand.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Fixed price used.** Given a Procedure on a fixed-price Contract line, when it is priced, then the price is the line's fixed price, and any recorded BTM units are kept for reference only.
- **No anaesthetist override.** Given a Procedure priced at a fixed price, then the anaesthetist cannot change its price, whatever the Contract's flag says.
- **Office change.** Given a Procedure priced at a fixed price, then the office can change its price, and the changed price is the price invoiced.
- **Nothing further.** Given recorded BTM units that would come to more or less than the fixed price, then no invoice or credit is raised automatically.

## Notes

Simplified 2026-10-02 from "prices it from the matched schedule line instead of RVG units, including any time band or add-on the line carries". [Contract lines](US-04.2.4) have no time band, add-on flag or quantity rule: add-ons and quantity rules were dropped on 7 October, and on 19 October, as far as Donald and Greg understand, there is no time band either (OQ-89). Time is only an optional recording by the anaesthetist.

Donald, 7 October: "the system should not try and calculate anything additional on a prepaid or on a fixed cost contract" (OQ-61). The RVG suggests that a "top up" on a fixed fee, using the RVG modifiers as a guide, is agreed with the Contract holder before the service.

OQ-117 answered (Donald, 29 October): "If something is a fixed fee, it doesn't allow a price override." This narrows the per-Contract flag (OQ-107, 19 October) for fixed prices: the anaesthetist's price override is never allowed on one, prepaid or not. On a prepaid Procedure any difference is a second invoice or credit raised by hand (OQ-96). Outside prepayment, a price the office changes is simply the price invoiced (Donald, 28 October).
