The ACCPAY to the anaesthetist is expected to stand as the buyer-created tax invoice (since renamed by IRD), carrying whatever wording and additional details that treatment requires. Whether the two are the same record, and what the wording must be, is pending OQ-29.

This is because invoices are proposed to be raised in the [anaesthetist's name with AA acting as agent](US-08.4.5); that GST agency treatment is also pending OQ-29.

## Notes

The buyer-created tax invoice is AA raising the anaesthetist's invoice to AA on their behalf so AA can pay out the money it holds for them ("money in, money out"), one per procedure (Notes 2026-10-01 #41). Every billable party has its own invoice, and each buyer-created tax invoice is the same value as its receivable (OQ-42, Notes 2026-10-01 #18). Generating them in the system saves AA a lot of manual work (Notes 2026-10-01 #41).

Whether the buyer-created tax invoice is the same record as the ACCPAY was left ambiguous in the meeting (Notes 2026-10-01 #41), and the wording it must carry waits on the accountant and the new IRD name (OQ-29).

IRD renamed the buyer-created tax invoice about 18 months ago; the new name was not given. The GST agency treatment is still with AA's accountant (OQ-29).

Volumes vary widely (rough figures): Ben about 50 to 70 a month, many about 20, some 3 to 10 (Notes 2026-10-01 #41).
