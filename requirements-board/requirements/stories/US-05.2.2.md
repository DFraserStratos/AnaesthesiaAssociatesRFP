Time units are tiered, not linear. For the first two hours of [anaesthetic time](US-03.3.3), the system charges 1 unit per 15 minutes or part thereof. From the start of the third hour, it charges 1 unit per 10 minutes or part thereof.

A part interval is always rounded up, always under the RVG tiers.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given 95 minutes of anaesthetic time, when time units are calculated, then the result is 7 time units (6 full 15-minute intervals plus 1 for the part interval).
- Given 125 minutes of anaesthetic time, when time units are calculated, then the result is 9 time units (8 for the first two hours plus 1 for the part of a 10-minute interval).

## Technical discussion

The tiers (interval lengths and where each starts) are defined as data, not hard coded (Greg: "Are we going to define those or are we going to hard code those?" Donald: "I guess probably define them.").

## Notes

Time units always come from the RVG rule, never from office tables or estimates (OQ-50). OQ-75 was answered by Donald and Greg: time is always rounded up and always uses the RVG tiered rules. The comparison with the NZSA RVG 2021 text itself was not done in the meeting.
