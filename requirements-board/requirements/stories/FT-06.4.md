After a prepaid procedure, the prepaid amount is the price by default, and the BTM units recorded on the day are for reference only. The system calculates nothing further and raises nothing automatically, in either direction:

- no additional invoice if the work was more than expected;
- no credit or refund if it was less;
- no threshold, however large the difference.

The same holds for every [fixed-price Contract](US-05.2.5). For a prepaid procedure this is the build position until Ben answers whether the prepaid price may be changed and whether part of it may be credited (OQ-96, OQ-97).

Where the work turns out very different, for example only half done or far longer than expected, the anaesthetist or the office may raise an ad hoc [additional invoice](US-08.6.3) or [credit note](US-08.6.5) by hand.

The anaesthetist is paid from the [prepay holding account](US-06.5.1), not by a new invoice to the patient. This is the second step of a prepayment: when the List goes to billing, a second pair moves the prepaid money from the holding account to whichever anaesthetist did the List. The trigger is the office authorising the List ([authorising the List](US-07.3.1)).

This is so the patient has a stable amount, and is never billed or credited without a person deciding to.

## Notes

OQ-61 and OQ-76 answered. Ben, 2026-10-06: "the system will not automatically invoice. Anaesthetists can / must create any ad-hoc invoices or credit notes as needed." Donald, 7 October: "the system should not try and calculate anything additional on a prepaid or on a fixed cost contract."

Not adopted: the pricing guide's proposal that the anaesthetist raises the price and "the difference is invoiced to the patient as an extra invoice", nor the idea floated on 7 October that the system works out the difference after the procedure with a later invoice to increase or decrease the final amount. This replaces the earlier rules that any positive balance is invoiced and that an overpayment is never refunded (OQ-03).

The v6 design differs (refund of a lowered prepaid price); see OQ-96.

OQ-80 answered 19 October: prepayment is two steps, and the second moves the money from the holding account to the anaesthetist "in place of another invoice to the patient". Greg named the trigger as the booking being "confirmed and submitted"; the catalogue hands a List to billing when the office authorises it, so that is used here, and the feature stays at Verify until AA confirms it. A prepaid price override combined with a move and the holding account is still open (OQ-96): Greg "might draw that out because it is a bit subtle".
