Admins can change a Procedure's price, rate or discount at any time, on any Contract, including a [fixed price](US-05.2.5): it is the only way to change a fixed price without changing the Contract, since a fixed price never allows the anaesthetist's price override (OQ-117). It is a change to the values on the Procedure like any other: no reason is needed, it is [audited](US-13.5.2) and the last change stands. The office normally touches a Booking last, at [review](FT-07.2), but it has no special authority over the anaesthetist.

The office's price change is not the anaesthetist's price override. The anaesthetist's override follows the Contract's flag and is never allowed on a fixed price; the office's change ignores the flag and works on any Contract.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Any Contract.** Given a Procedure on a fixed price, or on a Contract whose flag does not let the anaesthetist change its pricing, then the office can change its price, rate or discount, with no reason needed.
- **Audited.** Given the office changes a price, then the change is audited and the last change stands.

## Notes

OQ-16: the office can always change a price. On 14 October Greg suggested "locked" should read "fixed", and "price" means price, rate or discount ("price with a small p"). On 16 October Donald: the documents were "mostly documenting that, sequence-wise, probably the office was the last person to touch it, but they don't have any special authority"; Greg: "Yeah, that's changed that."

Whether the anaesthetist can change a Contract's price is a flag on each Contract, set by AA (OQ-107, answered 19 October); on a Contract that does not allow it, the anaesthetist asks the office, which can change any price.

Greg, 28 October, agreed: only AA admin staff can change any price on any Contract regardless of the Contract's rules, prepaid Contracts included. On a prepaid Procedure the office's change does not invoice or credit the difference by itself; any difference is a second invoice or credit raised by hand (OQ-96). Greg questioned "the only way to change a fixed price" while a fixed-price Contract might allow an override ("it's not universally true"). OQ-117 answered (Donald, 29 October): "If something is a fixed fee, it doesn't allow a price override", so the statement holds.
