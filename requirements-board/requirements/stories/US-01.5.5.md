An anaesthetist can never keep a List on a Slot they have marked unavailable. This holds for every reason, short-notice sickness included.

When an anaesthetist marks an AM or PM Slot unavailable, the system detects that it holds a List and makes them choose there and then:

- return it to the office, where it becomes a [Draft List](FT-01.6) with its hospital, surgeon and Bookings, shown among the office's Draft Lists with the nearest day first, so it does not disappear, or
- assign it to an available colleague, as when they [move their own List](US-01.4.3)

They are also asked what the vacated Slot becomes, free or unavailable, the same question as for any [List move](US-01.4.3).

This is so a List is never left with an anaesthetist who will not be there, and so the office never has to search for Lists stranded on someone who is away.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given a Slot holding a List, when the anaesthetist marks it unavailable, then they are offered "return to the office" or "assign to another anaesthetist".
- Given "return to the office", then the List keeps its hospital, surgeon and Bookings and becomes a Draft List.
- Given "assign", then the List moves into an available colleague's Slot.
- Given short-notice sickness marked on a Slot holding a List, then the same choice is forced; the anaesthetist cannot keep the List.
- Given either choice, then they are asked whether the vacated Slot becomes free or unavailable.

## Notes

Settled by OQ-64, and for short-notice sickness by OQ-81. Greg: the List "reverts to a draft list"; Donald: "return to office or assign to an available anaesthetist". Handling several Lists at once (a series of days off) was not discussed. Applying the [notifications](US-01.4.3) of moving their own List, and [no preference warning](US-01.4.5), to "assign" is an assumed reading.

Ben's practice, relayed by Greg: on the day, and always after hours, the anaesthetist usually finds a colleague themselves, often one doing a morning List at the same hospital; for the next day or later they hand it to the office.
