The anaesthetist can change the Contract on a Procedure, or choose one where the office has left none, until the List is AUTHORISED, choosing from the Contracts [already filtered](US-04.3.2) to that Booking. No explanation is needed; [every change is audited](US-02.5.5) and [flagged for office review](US-07.2.2).

This is so the anaesthetist can bill for what actually happened. The common case, which happens regularly: the hospital cannot get insurance pre-approval on the day, so the anaesthetist changes the Contract to [No contract · RVG](US-04.3.3) and the patient is billed. The anaesthetist also settles the Contracts the office could not, which is common, as the office often cannot tell which insurer pays or which RVG code applies.

Changing the Contract clears any price typed under the old one. Recorded RVG detail is kept, and the RVG code fills from the new Contract's default only when it is empty.

The anaesthetist cannot move a Procedure onto or off a prepaid Contract; only the office can.

## Acceptance criteria

- **None set yet.** Given a Procedure the office left with no Contract, when the anaesthetist chooses one, then it saves and the Procedure no longer needs an invoice destination.
- **No reason needed.** Given a List not yet authorised, when the anaesthetist changes a Procedure's Contract, then it saves without an explanation.
- **Reset.** Given a changed Contract, then a typed price is cleared and the recorded RVG detail is kept.
- **Audited and flagged.** Every change is audited and flagged at office review.
- **Pre-approval falls through.** Given a Procedure on an insurer's Contract, when the anaesthetist changes it to No contract · RVG, then [the Procedure's payer](US-11.2.2) (the patient, or someone else who pays) is billed.
- **Prepaid.** Given a Procedure on a prepaid Contract, then the anaesthetist cannot change its Contract; and when they change any other Procedure's Contract, no prepaid Contract is offered.
