An anaesthetist who wants fixed prices for their own work, for example prepaid cosmetic procedures, has their own Contract: a price list with a line and a fixed price for each procedure they price this way, for example Dr B. Smith, Face lift, $3,200. The anaesthetist tells the office which procedures and prices they want (for example from a spreadsheet of 25 procedures), and the office sets it up.

These Contracts:

- are offered only on that anaesthetist's own Bookings
- are created and kept by the office, from the price list the anaesthetist supplies
- have a holder that is not billed, so they bill [the payer on the Booking](US-11.2.2)
- never let the anaesthetist override a price: their lines are fixed prices, and a fixed price never allows the anaesthetist's price override (OQ-117), prepay list or not (OQ-96); to depart from one the anaesthetist changes the Contract or asks the office, which can still change any price ([office price change](US-05.4.2))
- when one is the anaesthetist's prepay list, are flagged as prepaid, which starts the [prepayment](FT-06.2) when the office picks it; its lines must carry a price above zero

Lines are fixed prices and need no RVG reference, though one can be given as a default (agreed in principle; Ben has not yet been asked).

This is where a [prepaid amount](US-06.2.2) comes from.

Each anaesthetist sets their own prices, because AA cannot set a common price for its members. The anaesthetist decides the prices and supplies their price list, and any change to it, to the office; the office enters it. Anaesthetists do not create or edit Contracts in the app.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given Dr B. Smith's own Contract with a Face lift line at $3,200, when one of Dr Smith's Bookings for a face-lift is set up, then the Contract is offered and prices it at $3,200.
- Given another anaesthetist's Booking, then Dr Smith's own Contract is not offered.
- Given the Contract is chosen, when the Procedure is billed, then the payer named on the Booking is invoiced.
- Given the Contract is chosen, then the anaesthetist cannot change the line's fixed price; they change the Contract or ask the office, which can.
- Given an anaesthetist in the app, then they cannot create or edit a Contract, their own included.
- Given a prepay list, when the office saves a line with no price or a zero price, then it is refused.

## Notes

OQ-91 answered (Donald, 8 October): "First party contracts will always be created by the administrative team ... it is the anesthetists who would supply those price lists to the admin team to set up within the system." The directors' "Anaesthetist will create and manage their own fixed price contracts" is read as the anaesthetist deciding the prices.

OQ-92 answered: a prepay line always carries a price.

Perhaps a templated letter goes to anaesthetists to gather their price lists, to be tested (Greg, 14 October).

A prepaid Procedure keeps the price on its issued prepayment invoice when the Contract's price changes before the procedure: a new Contract version does not relink it (Donald, 19 October). Ben confirmed this for a prepaid Procedure on 27 October (OQ-104).

Greg, 28 October: an anaesthetist's own price list holds "fixed prices, but they're editable. So when he does his timesheet, he can change it." Overtaken by OQ-117 (Donald, 29 October): "If something is a fixed fee, it doesn't allow a price override." Donald confirmed the same day that this covers every fixed price, the anaesthetist's own price list included: to charge differently on a Procedure priced from it, the anaesthetist switches Contract or asks the office, which can still change any price (Typed decision 2026-10-29 · Donald (product owner)).

Greg, 28 October: a prepaid Contract must be set up as not editable, "otherwise you're going to break the system". Ben, by phone the same day, wants a rare difference on the day raised by hand as a second invoice. A non-zero price is mandatory on a prepayment Contract line.

Greg, 28 October: a surgeon may hold a price list of the same kind, not prepaid but otherwise identical, which any anaesthetist working with that surgeon prices from. Both are identified by practitioner ID in the Contract. A surgeon's arrangement is held against individual surgeons, not the rooms (OQ-105, answered), and is one Contract that can be linked to many surgeons (Donald, 29 October).
