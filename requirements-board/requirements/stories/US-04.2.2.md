A Contract line can set how its procedures are priced:

- **a fixed price**: then BTM units are not needed, and are kept for reference if recorded
- **a rate**: a [unit rate](US-05.2.6) used instead of the anaesthetist's own
- **a discount**: a [percentage](US-05.4.3) off the calculated price
- **base or modifier units** of its own

With none of these, or on a Contract with no lines, the price is BTM units x [the anaesthetist's own unit value](US-12.1.1).

Each Contract carries a flag, set by AA, that says whether the anaesthetist may change its pricing (price, rate or discount). It is a flag on each Contract, not a global rule. Typically it allows this on Contracts priced from RVG units. A fixed price never allows the anaesthetist's price override, whatever the flag says ([fixed fee](US-05.2.5), OQ-117): the flag governs the price, rate and discount only on Procedures not priced at a fixed price. Where the flag does not allow it, the anaesthetist cannot change the price: they [change the Contract](US-03.4.1), which may change who is billed, or ask the office. The office can [change any price](US-05.4.2), and the last change stands.

A Contract can also name references its holder needs on its invoices, such as an insurer member number or the holder's own reference (for example SXAP), which go on the invoice.

This is so that every Procedure that selects a given Contract is billed the same way.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Fixed price.** Given a line with a fixed price, when the Procedure is priced, then the price is the line's price, and any recorded BTM units are kept for reference only.
- **Fixed rate.** Given a line with a fixed rate, when the Procedure is priced, then the price is BTM units x the Contract's rate instead of the anaesthetist's own.
- **Fixed discount.** Given a line with a fixed discount, when the Procedure is priced, then the price is BTM units x the anaesthetist's own unit value less the discount, shown where the price is changed, and editable only where the Contract lets the anaesthetist change its pricing.
- **Units.** Given a line with base or modifier units, then they replace the RVG code's figures under that Contract; otherwise the RVG code's figures apply.
- **Default.** Given a line that sets none of these, or No contract (RVG), then the price is BTM units x the anaesthetist's own unit value.
- **Editable setting.** Given a Contract that lets the anaesthetist change its pricing, they can change the price, rate or discount, with no reason needed. Given one that does not, the price is read-only and they cannot apply a discount of their own.
- **Fixed price never overridden.** Given a line with a fixed price on a Contract that lets the anaesthetist change its pricing, then the anaesthetist still cannot change that price.
- **Holder references.** Given a Contract whose holder needs a reference such as an insurer member number, when the invoice is raised, then the reference is printed on it. The office supplies it, not the anaesthetist ([insurer and approval number](US-11.4.3)).

## Technical discussion

Proposed in our RFP response: insurer terms tend to be schedule-based (a maximum payable per procedure) rather than a discount applied to a counterparty, so the question is the shape of the rate lookup. Stratos leans toward extending how the Contract model resolves rates rather than building a parallel structure alongside it. AA to advise how likely direct insurer billing is to grow (see OQ-20).

In the technical design v6 the setting is a flag on the Contract ("is_editable"), typically false for hospital fixed-price Contracts and true for those using RVG values. Contracts state only what differs from an RVG-style calculation, the detail left to the developers ([contract behaviour](AR-29#contract-behaviour)).

## Notes

Merges the former US-04.2.3 (Base unit override) and US-04.2.6 (Adjustment and override permissions), both now Retired. Base units sit on the RVG group, and a Contract line can state its own. US-04.2.6's office override permission is not carried over: the office can always change the price, whatever the Contract says (OQ-16).

Greg floated, as an idea only, making Contracts children of a master code so that a rare change to an RVG unit value routes to every Contract under it (Donald: "it could have multiple parents"). Not adopted.

The billing flow draft's note also lists billable party, invoice email and pre-paid amount as defined by the Contract. Who is billed follows from the Contract's holder ([who is billed](US-04.2.1)); the payer and their email are on the Booking; the prepaid amount comes from the line of a Contract flagged as prepaid, such as an anaesthetist's [own price list](US-04.2.14).

Whether the anaesthetist may depart from a Contract's price is settled as the per-Contract flag above, not a global rule (OQ-107, answered 19 October): a Contract with a price can be marked as not allowing a change, which makes it fixed, or allow it and trust the anaesthetist. Greg found the definition of fixed "a little rigid, but we can worry about that another day". A prepaid Contract is never editable: it cannot also allow a price override (Greg, 28 October; OQ-96). OQ-117 answered (Donald, 29 October) narrows the flag for every fixed price: "If something is a fixed fee, it doesn't allow a price override." The office's price change is not affected.
