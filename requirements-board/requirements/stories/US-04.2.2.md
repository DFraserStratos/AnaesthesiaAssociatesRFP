A Contract line can set how its procedure is priced:

- **a fixed price** for the procedure: the BTM units the anaesthetist records are then kept for reference only
- **a fixed rate:** a [contract unit rate](US-05.2.6) used instead of the anaesthetist's own
- **a fixed discount:** a [pre-applied percentage](US-05.4.3) off the anaesthetist's calculated price
- **base or modifier units** that differ from the procedure's

With none of these, the price is BTM units x [the anaesthetist's own unit value](US-12.1.1).

The anaesthetist can change the price or apply a discount, with a reason, on No contract (RVG) and on their own first-party Contracts. They never can on a third-party Contract: there the price is shown but cannot be edited, and they cannot apply a discount of their own (a fixed discount the Contract sets is shown, locked). To depart from a third-party price the anaesthetist changes the Contract, which also changes who is billed. The office can still [override any price](US-05.4.2) at review.

A Contract can also name any references its holder needs on its invoices, such as an insurer member number or claim reference. The Booking then asks for them.

This is so that every Procedure that selects a given Contract is billed the same way.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Fixed price.** Given a line with a fixed price, when the Procedure is priced, then the price is the line's price, and the recorded BTM units are kept for reference only.
- **Fixed rate.** Given a line with a fixed rate, when the Procedure is priced, then the price is BTM units x the Contract's rate instead of the anaesthetist's own.
- **Fixed discount.** Given a line with a fixed discount, when the Procedure is priced, then the price is BTM units x the anaesthetist's own unit value less the discount, shown where the price override is and locked.
- **Units.** Given a line with base or modifier units, then they replace the procedure's figures under that Contract; otherwise the procedure's, or its RVG group's, figures apply.
- **Default.** Given a line that sets none of these, or No contract (RVG), then the price is BTM units x the anaesthetist's own unit value.
- **Anaesthetist adjustment.** Given No contract (RVG) or the anaesthetist's own first-party Contract, the anaesthetist can type a price or apply a percentage discount, with a reason. Given a third-party Contract, the price is read-only and the anaesthetist cannot apply a discount; any fixed discount the Contract sets is shown but locked.
- **Holder references.** Given a Contract whose holder needs a reference such as an insurer member number, when a Booking uses it, then the Booking asks for that reference.

## Technical discussion

Proposed in our RFP response: insurer terms tend to be schedule-based (a maximum payable per procedure) rather than a discount applied to a counterparty, so the question is the shape of the rate lookup. Stratos leans toward extending how the Contract model resolves rates rather than building a parallel structure alongside it. AA to advise how likely direct insurer billing is to grow (see OQ-20).

## Notes

The guide's worked examples ([AR-28](AR-28)):

- an insured patient on an insurer's fixed-price Contract: the agreed price, which the anaesthetist cannot change; the insurer is billed
- a face-lift with a surgeon who has a fixed-fee arrangement: the surgeon's third-party Contract and its agreed price; the patient is billed
- the same face-lift with no arrangement: No contract (RVG), BTM x the anaesthetist's own rate; the patient is billed
- pre-approval falls through on the day: the anaesthetist changes to No contract (RVG), and the patient is billed
- an anaesthetist who charges a flat price: No contract (RVG), and they type their own price
- a colleague's family member at a reduced rate: No contract (RVG), with a 50% discount, or $0 for a no-charge invoice

The draft technical design derives whether the price can be adjusted from the holder's party type (adjustable on a first-party Contract and on No contract (RVG)); where that setting lives is open in the design ([AR-29 p6](AR-29#p6), [open points](AR-29#p11)). It is a draft and may change before the catch-up build.

Merges the former US-04.2.3 (Base unit override) and US-04.2.6 (Adjustment and override permissions), both now Retired. Base units now sit on the RVG group, with a procedure-level figure and a Contract line able to override them. US-04.2.6's office override permission is not carried over: the office can always override whatever the Contract says (OQ-16).

Greg floated, as an idea only, making Contracts children of a master code so that a rare change to an RVG unit value routes to every Contract under it (Donald: "it could have multiple parents"). Not adopted.

The billing flow draft's note also lists billable party, invoice email and pre-paid amount as defined by the Contract. Who is billed follows from the Contract's holder ([who is billed](US-04.2.1)); the payer and their email are on the Booking; the prepaid amount comes from the anaesthetist's own fixed-price Contract ([their own price list](US-04.2.14)).
