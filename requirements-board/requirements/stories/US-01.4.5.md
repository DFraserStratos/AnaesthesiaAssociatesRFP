When an anaesthetist [moves](US-01.4.3) one of their own Lists to a colleague, the app reveals nothing about any [not-preferred pairing](US-13.6.3) between the colleague and the List's surgeon:

- the anaesthetist moving the List sees no warning
- the colleague who receives it sees nothing either
- the app simply shows which colleagues are available

If the surgeon complains, the office can explain that the move was the anaesthetist's own, and that the system neither reveals nor blocks preferences when one anaesthetist hands a List to another.

This is because cover is often arranged at 6am when no office staff are around, a surgeon would rather have the List covered than not, and the anaesthetist and surgeon will usually talk anyway. Preferences stay private to the office in both directions. The office still sees them when it [assigns or moves a List](US-01.3.5).

## Acceptance criteria

- **No warning.** Given a colleague has a not-preferred pairing with the List's surgeon (either way round), when an anaesthetist moves their List to that colleague, then no warning or prompt is shown and the move goes ahead as any other.
- **Nothing revealed.** Neither anaesthetist can see, in the move or anywhere else in their app, that a not-preferred pairing exists.
- **Availability only.** The colleagues offered for the move are shown by availability alone, not separated or ordered by preferences.

## Notes

Ben: "If the system is just showing which anaesthetists are available that morning in green, I don't think you need that alert. There'll need to be a phone call anyway." A yellow warning was floated and dropped. This replaces the earlier soft warning and the recommendation of a short prompt without the reason.

The office is notified of the move through the [shared notification pool](FT-13.8) (OQ-65).
