The [master procedure list](US-05.1.6) holds standard single procedures. A combination is just a [fixed-fee Contract](US-05.2.5) under the procedure, not a separate procedure. For example, the Merivale Plastic Surgery set fee schedule (a surgeon's rooms' third-party Contract, self-funding, billed to the patient) prices "Face lift" at $3,565, "Facelift plus 1 add on (eg bleph)" at $3,910 and "Facelift plus 2 add ons (eg bleph & fat grafting)" at $4,255. All three are fixed-fee Contracts under Face Lift, and the latter two are combinations. A combination of several procedures, for example an abdominoplasty, breast lift and liposuction, is one Contract sitting against each of its parent procedures, so picking any of them offers it.

When a billable party wants the combined price broken into parts, the admin raises [additional invoices](US-08.6.4).

This keeps the master procedure list clean while still pricing combined work the way it is agreed.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given a combination Contract set against three procedures, when any of them is picked, then the Contract is offered.
- Given the Merivale schedule, then "Facelift plus 1 add on" is a fixed-fee Contract under Face Lift priced at $3,910.

## Notes

Once a combination Contract is chosen, the Booking records only the Contract, not its component procedures (a trade-off raised in the meeting). Greg's "operation" container (a level holding several procedures tied to one Contract) was not adopted; the simpler model is to be tried in the next prototype. Contracts are expected to number in the thousands; they are kept navigable by the picker's procedure and holder filters and code search (OQ-66). A split after invoicing is a credit note against the original combined invoice, then new additional invoices ([split a combined Procedure](US-08.6.4), OQ-72).

On 7 October Greg accepted the combination model as written after first doubting it: "all you're trying to do is associate that contract with that procedure and you're done"; "That's fine." The picker shows both the single and the combination Contracts, since the combined price can be lower than the parts. He still finds a combination under several procedures "a bit problematic", to see how it looks later.
