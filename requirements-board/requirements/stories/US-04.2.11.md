The office sets up a combination of procedures as a [fixed-price](US-05.2.5) Contract of its own, offered alongside the single procedures. For example, Merivale Plastic Surgery's set fees, held by the surgeon group's [Party](US-04.1.5), linked to its surgeons and billing the payer, include the separate Contracts:

- "Face lift"
- "Facelift plus 1 add on (eg bleph)"
- "Facelift plus 2 add ons (eg bleph & fat grafting)"

This is so combined work is priced the way it is agreed, often lower than the parts, with no extra structure.

Each combination stands alone and may carry a [default RVG code](US-04.2.18), for example H3.

Fee extras in a schedule (a GA add-on fee, "per 15 minutes over 120", "per area") are not Contracts: they are billed as [events](US-03.7.2) or [additional invoices](US-08.6.3) on the Procedure, with the amount typed. Separately priced procedures a schedule calls add-ons (MIGS add-on to cataract, anterior vitrectomy) stay Contracts.

When a billable party wants the combined price broken into parts, the admin raises [additional invoices](US-08.6.4).

## Acceptance criteria

- **Combination.** Given the Merivale Contracts, then "Facelift plus 1 add on" is a Contract of its own at its fixed price.
- **Found by RVG code.** Given that Contract carries the default RVG code H3, when the user searches by H3, then the Contract is found.
- **Fee extra.** Given a GA add-on fee on a schedule, then it is billed as an event or additional invoice, never picked as a Contract.
