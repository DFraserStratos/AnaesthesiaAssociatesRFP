Admins apply the Contract to each Procedure at booking setup, when the Booking is created or matched, ahead of the day of the procedure. The office's main job here is to identify who pays: it picks the [contract holder](US-04.1.5), then the Contract, then the line where the Contract has lines. Setting the Contract is mandatory: a Booking without one stays on the admin's list until it is sorted. Picking a Contract flagged as prepaid starts the [prepayment](FT-06.2).

The RVG code is not the office's job: Ben and Nick do not want staff assigning base units. The office may enter one if they know it; otherwise they leave it for the anaesthetist (Greg: "if you can find it, fine; if you don't know, don't worry").

This is different from the RFP's original design, where the billing route and its Contract were only resolved by the engine once [the List was authorised](US-07.3.1). Setting the Contract at booking setup instead means office staff can see and check it well before the procedure happens.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given a Procedure with no Contract, then its Booking stays on the admin's list until a Contract is set.

## Notes

Today the office sets the Contract from the hospital's daily sheets. In the future state the hospital defines it through its inbound data: see [hospital data sets the Contract](US-04.3.6) (OQ-22).

OQ-99 answered: the office may leave the RVG code or line blank for the anaesthetist; the Contract is what must be set.
