A Contract is a structure in the system that sets the rules for how a procedure on a Booking is paid for, and who pays. It is not necessarily a legal contract between AA and anyone.

AA is the party in the centre. Everyone else who has an arrangement with AA is a contract holder:

- an insurer, a hospital or a surgeon's rooms (a third party)
- an anaesthetist, for their own price list (a first party)

AA manages Contracts as master data, and every Procedure selects exactly one. Picking it is the whole pricing decision: this replaces the RFP's separate step of resolving a billing route and then looking up a governing Contract. There can be as many Contracts as AA needs (OQ-67).

The Contract decides who is billed:

- its holder, when the holder pays AA itself, such as an insurer [accepting direct claims](US-11.4.1)
- otherwise the [payer named on the Booking](US-11.2.2): the patient by default, or a parent or guardian

When nobody has a special arrangement, the procedure is priced on "No contract (RVG)". It is offered first for every procedure, and the system stores it as a Contract like any other.

This is so every Procedure knows, from one choice, how it is priced, what rules apply and who gets the invoice.

## Technical discussion

Proposed in our RFP response: whether the hospital route is final or can fall back to the Billable Party (for example on dispute or non-payment) is treated as a commercial and relationship decision before a technical one. Re-routing an issued invoice also affects the matching ACCPAY and the anaesthetist's outstanding balance view, so Stratos wants to understand what AA does today before proposing what the system should do.

Note: the response still frames this as the hospital billing route; in this catalogue the Contract replaces separate route resolution. AA has since answered the fallback question (OQ-19): if a hospital or other holder disputes an invoice because of an error on AA's side, it is fixed by [credit note and re-issue](US-08.6.2); otherwise it is treated as an outstanding invoice. No fallback to the patient is defined.
