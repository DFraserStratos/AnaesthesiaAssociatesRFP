The anaesthetist can type their own price on a Procedure where its Contract allows it. Whether it does is a setting AA sets on each Contract, and it exists only on a Contract priced from RVG units. The RVG code, times and modifiers stay recorded and shown beside the typed price, and a price of $0 gives a no-charge invoice.

This is so an anaesthetist, as an independent practitioner AA acts for, can set their own price where AA allows it, while a price agreed with a hospital or surgeon stands.

Where the price is not theirs to type:

- **An RVG Contract that does not allow the override**: the price shows read-only.
- **A fixed price**, prepaid or not: never open to the anaesthetist's override (OQ-117, OQ-96).
- **No contract · Fixed price**: the anaesthetist types the amount ([open-price fallbacks](US-04.3.9)); on any other fallback the amount is the office's.

To depart from a price they cannot change, the anaesthetist [changes the Contract](US-03.4.1), which may also change who is billed, or asks the office, which can [change any price](US-05.4.2).

No reason is needed: a typed price is audited and flagged at office review, with the computed price beside it. The only explanation the system asks for is on a claimed [modifier](US-03.3.4), because insurers ask for it.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Own pricing.** Given an RVG Contract that allows the override, then they can type a price and the RVG detail is kept.
- **Not allowed.** Given an RVG Contract that does not allow the override, then the price shows read-only.
- **Fixed price.** Given a Procedure on a fixed-price Contract, prepaid or not, then the price shows read-only.
- **Contract discount.** Given an [RVG Contract with a discount](US-05.4.3), then the discount shows as applied and the anaesthetist cannot change it.
- **No reason.** Given an entered price, then it saves without a reason and is flagged at office review.
