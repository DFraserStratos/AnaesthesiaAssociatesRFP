Admins maintain the insurer master list, each with an accepts direct claims flag.

Where an insurer accepts direct claims, it holds one or more insurer-held [third-party Contracts](US-04.1.1) governing how procedures billed to it are priced.

## Technical discussion

AA confirmed (OQ-20) that an insurer needs no rate table of its own. Special rates, for example for nib, are held as insurer Contracts in the existing Contract model, not a parallel rate structure.

- Insurer terms tend to be schedule-based (a maximum payable per procedure) rather than a discount applied to a counterparty. A Contract that resolves rates differently becomes a rule within the existing model, proven by automated tests, and is resolved during Scoping and Design.
- Cover split between an insurer and the patient is not a Contract setting: on the Booking, the user [splits the Procedure's fee between parties](US-08.2.3) (OQ-23; split basis per OQ-68).

## Notes

Many insurance jobs are plain RVG, paid by the insurer, rather than a fixed fee (Greg).

Most insurer and hospital Contracts are plain RVG Contracts with no special prices, billed to their holder. Southern Cross, for example, holds several, which act almost as identifiers for its billing: "there's nothing underneath them... it falls through to the default values" (Greg, 7 October, after Ben stressed that most Contracts are still RVG). How such a Contract is offered for every procedure without a line per procedure is OQ-98.

The Booking often shows the patient's insurance status (for example SXAP), and the hospital often confirms it. That indication guides which Contract is chosen, but the Contract decides who is billed: an insured patient who pays and claims the money back themselves is billed under No contract (RVG). Whether and where the indication is stored is OQ-93.
