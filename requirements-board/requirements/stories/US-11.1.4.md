The NHI is mandatory: every patient record has one, and there is no blank NHI (Donald and Greg, 16 and 19 October). A Booking cannot be saved without an NHI.

A booking that arrives without one, from a feed or a surgeon's PDF, is not a Booking yet. It waits at intake, on the [unmatched queue](US-02.1.4), until the office adds the NHI, and then it is matched and becomes a Booking. The earlier idea of a Booking going ahead with the NHI pending and authorising blocked is dropped.

This is because Bookings arrive from feeds and theatre lists that sometimes omit the NHI, and "you don't want things rejected out of feeds" (Greg): the intake row is kept, not lost. A procedure never goes ahead without an NHI in the room (Donald), so the NHI exists even when the office does not have it yet. The [intake rows waiting for an NHI](US-11.1.5) are the office's list to chase.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given a Booking being created or edited, when it has no NHI, then it cannot be saved.
- Given an incoming booking with no NHI, then it waits on the unmatched queue and no Booking or patient record is made from it.
- Given the office adds the NHI to a waiting row, then it can be matched and becomes a Booking.

## Notes

On 7 October Vanessa said "make NHI mandatory"; the room added that this makes the NHI the patient identifier throughout the system. In the public health sector a patient without one is assigned a temporary NHI, and even a self-funding patient from overseas has one if they are in the hospital's records. On 19 October Greg: "It's mandatory now, so that's that" (OQ-49); Donald then settled the incoming case as above.

What happens to a duplicate patient record when an NHI is found later no longer arises, because no patient record is made without an NHI.
