Contracts come in two kinds, alongside the one default:

- **Third-party Contracts:** an agreement with an insurer, a hospital or a surgeon's rooms. Usually a fixed price per procedure, agreed for a period (often a year). The anaesthetist cannot change the price.
- **The anaesthetist's own first-party Contracts:** [their own price list](US-04.2.14), for example for prepaid cosmetic work.
- **No contract (RVG):** [standard RVG pricing](US-04.3.3) at the anaesthetist's own unit rate, offered for every procedure.

Some Contracts set a [fixed rate](US-05.2.6) or a [fixed discount](US-05.4.3) instead of a fixed price.

There are no formal categories beyond these. The [Contract picker](US-04.3.2) is one long list grouped by contract holder, with headings within it, and No contract (RVG) first. Most insurer and hospital Contracts, for example Southern Cross's several Contracts, are plain RVG Contracts with no special prices, billed to their holder.

This keeps Contracts as simple as the business is: who holds the arrangement and what it sets, rather than a fixed set of categories to choose from.

## Notes

[ACC arrangements](US-05.5.1) are hospital or other contract-holder Contracts with ACC pricing, not a separate kind (RFP).

There is still no Pre-paid category. The prepaid amount now comes from the anaesthetist's own first-party Contract (OQ-25, OQ-04).

Whether a holder's plain RVG Contracts are offered for every procedure is OQ-98.
