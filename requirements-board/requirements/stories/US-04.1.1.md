There are no kinds or categories of Contract. Each Contract is set up from a few settings:

- whether its holder is billed
- whether the anaesthetist can change its pricing: a flag on each Contract, set by AA, not a global rule (on a prepaid Procedure, whether they can is OQ-96)
- whether it is prepaid
- whether it belongs to one anaesthetist or surgeon

Five arrangements cover most work:

| Arrangement | Who pays | Price based on | Anaesthetist can change price | Prepaid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [No contract (RVG)](US-04.3.3) | the payer on the Booking (the patient, or a guardian) | RVG units x the anaesthetist's own unit value | yes | no |
| A hospital's RVG Contract | the hospital | RVG units | yes | no |
| A hospital's fixed-price Contract | the hospital | the Contract's price or rate | no | no |
| [An anaesthetist's prepay price list](US-04.2.14) | the payer on the Booking | the anaesthetist's own price | yes (on a prepaid Procedure, OQ-96) | yes |
| A surgeon's fixed-fee arrangement | the payer on the Booking | the surgeon's fixed fee | no | no |

Other combinations can be set up for specific billing needs, for example a Contract at standard RVG pricing less a [fixed discount](US-05.4.3), or one with a [fixed rate](US-05.2.6). A Contract stays one unit: a hospital can hold several Contracts for the same procedure, one billed to ACC and one to an insurer, each a Contract of its own. Surgeons' arrangements are Contracts like any other.

The Contract picker is [the filtered Contract list](US-04.3.2). Most insurer and hospital Contracts, for example Southern Cross's several Contracts, are plain RVG Contracts with no special prices, billed to their holder.

This keeps Contracts as simple as the business is: who holds the arrangement and what it sets, chosen by settings, not categories.

## Notes

[ACC arrangements](US-05.5.1) are hospital or other contract-holder Contracts with ACC pricing, not a separate kind (RFP).

Prepaid is a setting on a Contract, not a category (OQ-25).

A hospital's plain RVG Contract has no lines (OQ-98, answered).

Greg dropped "first party" and "third party" from his documents ("they're not truly third and first party circumstances in all cases").
