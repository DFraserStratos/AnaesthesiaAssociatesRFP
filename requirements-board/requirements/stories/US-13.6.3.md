Admin staff keep a private record of who does not want to work with whom, in both directions:

- a surgeon who will not work with a particular anaesthetist
- an anaesthetist who will not work with a particular surgeon

Each entry is one surgeon and one anaesthetist, and says which side asked. It is kept on both the [surgeon's profile](US-13.6.2) and the [anaesthetist's profile](US-12.1.4). Recommendation: allow an optional reason.

Only admin staff see and maintain the record. Anaesthetists never see it, and neither side learns of the other's entry.

The record informs admin when they assign or move Lists: a soft warning, never a block (see [the assignment warning](US-01.3.5)). There is no warning when an anaesthetist hands their own List on; the system then reveals nothing to either party (see [no preference warning](US-01.4.5)).

This is so the system holds the pairings the office keeps in its head today, and can inform admin's matching without the people involved learning of an entry.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Record a pairing, either direction.** An admin can add an entry from the surgeon's or the anaesthetist's profile, saying which side does not want to work with the other; the entry shows on both profiles.
- **Remove a pairing.** An admin can end an entry; the history of who changed it is kept (see US-13.5.2).
- **Admin only.** Given an anaesthetist signed in to the mobile or web app, when they view their own profile, a List or a surgeon, then no entry is shown, in either direction.
- **Warns admin, never blocks.** Given an admin assigning or moving a List, when they choose an anaesthetist, then anaesthetists with an entry against the List's surgeon are shown separately from the others, with a soft warning, and can still be chosen.
- **No warning on an anaesthetist's own hand-over.** Given an anaesthetist hands their own List to a colleague who has an entry against the List's surgeon, then neither of them sees any warning.

## Notes

This needs the surgeon master list rather than free-text surgeon names. Positive preferences, who someone does want to work with, are [preferred pairings](US-13.6.4).

The name 'blacklist' should change. Greg's name for it was the 'naughty list' and the catalogue called it the blacklist; on 7 October Donald said "I would probably stop calling this a blacklist and find a better term for it". No new name was chosen; 'not-preferred pairings' is a working description until AA picks one (OQ-102).

How it was settled (OQ-43): on 2026-10-01 Greg pictured anaesthetists keeping their own list on their own screen, and on 2026-10-02 he wanted the design to allow both directions ("We'll call it two lists"). On 7 October the room settled it: "Definitely don't want either side to know", and "it has to be private: the admin team can see and update it, but the anaesthetists shouldn't see it". Surgeons do ring the office to say they will not work with someone. On an anaesthetist's own hand-over, Ben: "if it was last-minute sick cover, the surgeon would rather have the list covered than not covered"; a yellow warning was floated and dropped. This replaces the earlier ideas of showing the record on the anaesthetist's own screen and of a short prompt to the anaesthetist.

Greg suggested the not-preferred information could also feed the order in which suggested anaesthetists are presented (Notes 2026-10-07 · AA client meeting #20).
