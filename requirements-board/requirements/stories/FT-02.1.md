The matching screen (Intake) is the front door for every List and change that reaches AA from the surgeons' rooms and the hospitals. Arrivals come in on their own, and the office decides what each one becomes: a new List, or an update merged into a List AA already holds.

This is so one checking step the office controls stands between every outside source and the schedule, and the office can trust what has come in and how it was taken in.

## Notes

Today AA staff take bookings from the hospitals' daily sheets and downloads and match them to Lists and Bookings on a matching screen. That matching screen imports the files from St George's and Southern Cross through an integration; everything from the other providers is entered by hand.

Greg, from Vanessa, says the hospital sends one updated theatre list a day, for the next day's bookings, and does not send repeated updates; the v6 executive summary describes it as a PDF and as a direct data integration (format still OQ-13). It can carry a few things the surgeon's rooms do not know.

Greg and Donald agreed the first version has no automated decisions: downloads from "probably just the same two hospitals" land in the matching and creating screen, where staff see the patient details and what is missing, then save a [Draft List](US-01.6.1) or assign an anaesthetist. Automated approvals come once the matching is trusted. More hospital feeds and automatic matching are [Future Work](FT-14.6).

OQ-108 answered 4 November (Donald, for the build; not yet seen by Ben or Vanessa): both intake sources are built in the first release. OQ-109's answer of 18 November (no automatic syncing in the first release) was replaced on 26 November (Donald, for the build; not yet seen by AA): the screen "should be acting as a front door to all lists that are sent by PDFs, and also all hospital integrations", with no import button, and "We never feed back to other systems, we only ever bring stuff in." Ben named Forte as one of the two integrated hospitals on 27 October; the catalogue keeps St George's and Southern Cross.
