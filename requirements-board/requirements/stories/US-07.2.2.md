The admin reviews each Booking on a submitted List, covering:

- its Procedures and the Contract selected for each one, each shown as [the text as received, then the Contract, then the RVG code](US-03.1.9)
- whether a Procedure needs an insurer's Contract, and the insurance status that came with the Booking
- [the payer named on the Booking](US-11.2.2), who is invoiced when the Contract bills the patient: the patient by default, which the office or anaesthetist can change to a parent or guardian
- the insurer and insurance approval number on the Booking ([insurer and approval number](US-11.4.3)); a code such as SXAP says the hospital is arranging the cover and is the billable party, not that the cover is approved
- other references
- addresses and invoice email addresses
- any Procedure the anaesthetist [flagged for the office to check](US-03.6.3)
- anything missed or filled in incorrectly, such as an operation not completed properly

The admin then approves the Contract selections made earlier, or corrects them. The review raises two warnings, neither a block:

- a patient under 18 who is the payer on the Booking for a Procedure billed to that payer (see [warn when a child is the payer on the Booking](US-11.2.4))
- a Booking that says an insurer will pay, but with no insurer's Contract chosen for any of its Procedures (still marked to be confirmed in the design); pre-approval often falls through on the day, when the patient pays and the anaesthetist changes the Contract to No contract (RVG)

This is so mistakes in the anaesthetist's Contract choice and the billing details are caught before the List is authorised. AA acts as agent for independent anaesthetists, so the system accepts the anaesthetist's choice and relies on this review.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given a Booking whose insurance status says an insurer will pay, when no Procedure on it has an insurer's Contract, then the office review shows a warning that the admin can clear, and the List can still be authorised.
- Given a Booking with a Procedure whose Contract bills the patient, when the admin reviews it, then [the payer named on the Booking](US-11.2.2) is shown and can be changed.

## Notes

Approval happens here, at the SUBMITTED review after the anaesthetist submits, not before the procedure (OQ-26; Q&A #4).

The Booking may show the patient's insurance status (for example SXAP), which the anaesthetist sees when choosing, while each Procedure's Contract decides who is billed, so one Booking can have Procedures billed to different parties (OQ-55; Notes 2026-10-07 · Pricing model documents #11). Greg: "the booking does hold the funding source" (Notes 2026-10-07 · AA meeting with Greg #6). OQ-93 is partly answered: the Booking holds the insurer and the approval number; whether insurance narrows the Contract list is still open. The insured-but-no-insurer-Contract warning is the draft design's "Warning at office review, not a block, because pre-approval regularly falls through on the day" (Notes 2026-10-07 · Pricing model documents #34). The order for who pays (the insurer named on the hospital's theatre list, the hospital applying to the insurer as agent) is still to be drawn out with Vanessa (Notes 2026-10-01 #29 #55). The under-18 check is a mild warning that the admin can clear, not a block (OQ-54; Notes 2026-10-01 #28 #47).

Greg (19 October): the Booking holds the insurer and the insurance approval number. A blank insurer is fine if the patient is paying; otherwise the insurer must be specified, and the office checks at review that the approval is there (the hospital will usually already have done the work, so the office only needs its own record to have it too). The anaesthetist can still change the List until it is authorised, so the office sees their latest changes at review.
