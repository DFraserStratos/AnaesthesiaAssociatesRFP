Admins keep AA's list of broad surgical specialties, with no subspecialties: general surgery, not upper GI. [Surgeons](US-13.6.6) and [Contracts](US-04.2.16) take their specialties from it. Every change is audited.

This is so one shared list narrows the Contracts offered for each surgeon.

Until AA's specialist spreadsheet gives the list, it starts as Ophthalmology, General surgery, Plastic surgery, ENT, Gynaecology, Orthopaedics, Urology, Gastroenterology and Cardiothoracic surgery. Anaesthetists have no specialty.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Broad only.** Given the list, then each specialty is one broad name with no subspecialty under it: general surgery, never upper GI.
- **Shared.** Given a specialty is renamed, then every surgeon and Contract that holds it shows the new name.

## Notes

Ben, 5 November: "We can be quite generic; rather than saying upper-GI surgeon or something, we can just say general surgery", and, on keeping to the top level, "The broad specialties."

Anaesthetists' pain work does not bill through AA (Vanessa, 5 November: "Doesn't, it kind of doesn't"), so no anaesthetist specialty is needed.

Donald's D3, 5 November: "AA keeps a list of broad specialties, with no subspecialties." Adding Urology, Gastroenterology and Cardiothoracic surgery to D3's six is our reading of the contract files (SX Robotic, Ruru, STG Heart) in the contract model proposal (A11), for AA to confirm.
