Where a Booking is insured, the office records on it the insurer and the insurance approval number, the reference the insurer gives when it accepts the claim. Most of the time the office knows the hospital, surgeon, insurer and approval number from the booking.

If a Booking says it is insured but has no approval number, the office rings the surgeon's rooms. A code such as SXAP from the rooms or the hospital says the hospital is arranging the cover and is the billable party, not that the cover is approved. Recording these is the office's job; it is not required of the anaesthetist.

The approval number is held on the Booking; the v6 design also holds the insurer's name on the booking procedure (Greg's proposal, not yet confirmed). Whether insurance narrows the Contract list is open (OQ-93). The Contract still decides who is billed.

This is because being insured is not the same as having the claim approved, and the approval number is the missing piece the holder needs.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given an insured Booking, the office can record the insurer and the approval number.
- Given a Booking that says it is insured but has no approval number, then the office rings the surgeon's rooms for it.
- Given a Booking, the anaesthetist can mark it complete without an approval number.
