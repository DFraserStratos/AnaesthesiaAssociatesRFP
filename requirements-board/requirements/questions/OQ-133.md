When the office adds a backdated price range to a fixed-price Contract, every Procedure on it that is not yet authorised, not edited and not prepaid re-prices to the range in force on its date. When a setting other than a fixed price changes from a date (for example Unisports' rate from 1 January 2027), the office [makes a new Contract](US-04.1.2), and Procedures dated after the change stay on the old one until someone moves them.

Does the office want a list of the Procedures affected? For a new price range, those whose price changed; for a new Contract, those dated after the change that are still on the old one.

What hangs on it: whether the Contract screen shows such a list, or the office relies on its review to catch them.

1. **No list.** The office's review catches them.
2. **A list in both cases,** shown when a price range is added or a new Contract is made.
3. **A list only for the new-Contract case,** where nothing moves by itself.

No recommendation: until it is answered, the build shows no list (the proposal leaves it out of the model, AR-58 section 10). Raised by Donald from section 11.2, point 1, of the [contract model proposal](AR-58) (5 November); not discussed with AA.

## Answer

Answered 18 November (Donald, for the build; not yet discussed with AA). Option 3, extended with two warnings.

**A dated change to a setting other than the fixed price.** The office still makes a new Contract for it ([dated change](US-04.1.2)). When it does, the app warns that Procedures dated on or after the change are still on the old Contract and lists them, so the office can move each one. The same warning shows if the office disables the old Contract while such Procedures are still on it. Nothing moves by itself: the office decides each one.

**A backdated price range.** No list of the Procedures that re-priced, since they follow the [range in force](US-04.2.15) by themselves. But when the new range covers authorised Procedures, which keep their old price, the app warns how many kept it and lists them, so the office can decide whether to act.

Donald, on the new-Contract case: "I would expect the app to float a warning to the user about it... the right way to fix this is creating a new contract with new settings."
