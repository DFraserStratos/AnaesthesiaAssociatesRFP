Contract prices carry an [effective-from date](US-04.2.10), and hospitals and funders usually give an effective date, sometimes with a few weeks' notice. When a Procedure is priced, which date picks the price: the date of the procedure, the date the Booking was made, or the date the invoice is raised?

Recommendation: the date of the procedure, because that is when the service was given; the Contract version used is then locked at AUTHORISED as already agreed (US-04.3.5).

Meeting 2026-10-01: Greg: "I'd go with the procedure" date. A price change between booking and procedure then only affects prepayments. To propose to AA.

## Answer

The date of the procedure. At the 7 October client meeting, Donald: "the price used for the invoice is the price effective on the date the procedure actually takes place, correct?" The room: "Correct." Donald: "Okay, so that's question 48." Greg, the same morning: "that's settled. It's the date of the procedure", the same idea as the patient's age on the day.

Greg and Donald also agreed that Contract pricing must carry an effective date; whether the Contract is dated and its lines move with it (Greg's view) or the lines carry their own dates is left to Greg's model. The guide has a price review create a new dated version of the contract, with bookings priced under the old version keeping the old price; the draft technical design has the resolver use the procedure date and reject a contract not valid on it. (See "Notes 2026-10-07 · AA meeting with Greg #1 #39 #40", "Notes 2026-10-07 · AA client meeting #6", "Notes 2026-10-07 · Pricing model documents #18 #28")

The meeting adds: every Contract change creates a new version from a date the office chooses, never in the past ("You can't back date a price upgrade because it might stuff up your existing invoicing"), and the procedure date decides which version prices a procedure, even if it is invoiced later. (See "Notes 2026-10-16 · AA meeting with Greg #16", "Notes 2026-10-16 · Pricing model documents v6 #42")

Update 2026-11-05 (Donald, for the build; not yet confirmed by AA or Greg): overtakes the answers above in part. The date of the procedure still decides the price, but there are no Contract versions, and a price can now be backdated. Donald: a fixed-price Contract has price ranges, each with "a start date, which may be in the past, and a price; it ends the day before the next one starts, so ranges never overlap or leave a gap. The range in force on the procedure date gives the price." This replaces the 16 October rule that a change takes effect from a date "never in the past". No other setting is dated: a rate or discount is edited in place, and "Changing a setting from a date means a new Contract." A Procedure's price follows the range in force on its date until something holds it. Donald: "a prepaid Procedure keeps its prepayment invoice's price, an edited value keeps the edit, and AUTHORISED freezes everything." That meets Greg's concern for existing invoicing: a backdated range never touches an authorised or invoiced Procedure, and AA raises an additional invoice by hand if it wants the difference (AR-58 section 7.2). Still open: whether the office wants a list of the Procedures a backdated range or a dated change affects.
