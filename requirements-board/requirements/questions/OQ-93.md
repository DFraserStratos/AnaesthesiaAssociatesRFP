## Answer

Partly answered: the insurer and the approval number are stored on the Booking ([US-11.4.3](US-11.4.3)), from the source data, and the office picks up the approval reference. Still open: whether insurance narrows the Contract list, and whether Greg's "approval number" is the design's insurance acceptance reference (both answered on 23 October: see the 2026-10-23 update below).

- Greg, 16 October, after talking with Ben and Vanessa: "there's a big difference between being insured and having your insurance claim approved." The rooms usually settle the insurance before the booking reaches the List, so the missing piece is "the approval number": "I've added the approval number to the booking data so that there's a place to put it." If a booking says insured with no reference, Vanessa rings the surgeon's rooms. See Notes 2026-10-16 · AA meeting with Greg #1.
- The v6 design holds the insurer's name and an insurance acceptance reference on each booking procedure, set by the office or the anaesthetist. See Notes 2026-10-16 · Pricing model documents v6 #5.
- A code such as SXAP tells the office the hospital will arrange Southern Cross cover and is the billable party ("So you can set up the contract that way"), not whether cover is approved. See Notes 2026-10-14 · AA meeting with Greg #3.
- Whether the insurer has approved the charges is a gap in the information chain, followed up by Vanessa with the hospital; the more common failure is the wrong party being billed, which needs a session with Vanessa. See Notes 2026-10-14 · AA meeting with Greg #14 #16.
- The approval reference is the office's job to pick up, not a requirement on the anaesthetist when completing the Booking. See Notes 2026-10-16 · AA meeting with Greg #57.
- Still open: whether it narrows the Contract list (answered 23 October: it does not). It is left open; v6 no longer lists "the insurer" among the context that filters Contracts (hospital, surgeon or rooms, anaesthetist). See Notes 2026-10-16 · Pricing model documents v6 #5 #31.
- A patient-billed Contract on a Booking that notes insurance raises a warning at office review, not a block; the design still marks this "TBC" (dropped 23 October). See Notes 2026-10-16 · Pricing model documents v6 #5 #39.

Still open: whether the "approval number" Greg named is the same field as the design's insurance acceptance reference; it is not stated (answered 23 October: it is).

Update 2026-10-19: Greg, at the office review: the Booking is "meant to have the insurer and the insurance approval number... If the insurance is blank, that's probably okay if the patient's paying. Otherwise, you need to specify the insurer, and the office at this point would be checking that the approval was there, but the hospital will have already done the work for them." Parts 1 to 3 were not discussed. See "Notes 2026-10-19 · AA meeting with Greg #12".

Update 2026-10-23: parts 1 to 3 are answered (Donald and Greg). (1) The insurance indication does not narrow the [Contract list](US-04.3.2). Donald: "does the insurance indication narrow down the contract list? No." (2) No warning when a Booking notes insurance but a patient-billed Contract is chosen: "we don't have to try and do any matching or magic algorithm with upstream and our contract system", perhaps later "once we start building it". The only warning is an insurer with no approval number. Greg: "if the insurance field is filled in and the approval is blank, then that's a warning condition"; any other, "Not that I think we can detect." It stands on its own, an alert to the office or the anaesthetist. This answers where the warning shows, which the 2026-10-28 update calls unsaid: to the office, and to the anaesthetist when they look at the Booking themselves (Donald). (3) Greg's approval number is the design's insurance acceptance reference: "Apparently yes." This drops the warning at office review for a Booking that names an insurer but has no insurer Contract (16 October, v6), and the recommendation's soft warning with it. On 28 October the missing-approval warning was agreed again (Greg: "It'll be a warning"; Donald: "We've already talked about that"), and for an insured patient under a patient-billed Contract Greg said "We bill the patient, end of story" (Notes 2026-10-28 · AA meeting with Greg #4 #40). Still open is the 28 October matter: whether the anaesthetist sees the number itself. See "Notes 2026-10-23 · AA meeting with Greg #1 #14 #15"

Update 2026-10-28: the approval number stays, and its absence is a warning. Donald: "I don't think we need insurance approval numbers, do we?" Greg: "the insurer field tells us to start with whether you have any insurance at all. The approval number says whether the claim's been accepted"; "sometimes the hospital asks for the approval number on the invoice so they can match them up"; "It'll be a warning. It should be a warning." Where it is held: "it's just fields on the booking." Greg would drop the line that recording it is the office's job and not the anaesthetist's, because "the anaesthetist may well change that on the day of the procedure... This might be blank and they might find out on the day." The rooms should send it with the list, "but it doesn't happen as much as it should"; it shows on the matching screen and the office can edit it there. New, to ask AA: does the anaesthetist see the number itself, or only that it is approved? Donald would show it to the office only; Greg: "the anaesthetist will want to see it... when he gets to the hospital." Where the warning shows (the office review, the anaesthetist's view, or both) was not said. Parts 1 and 3 were not discussed. See "Notes 2026-10-28 · AA meeting with Greg #4 #41 #42"

Update 2026-11-05 (Donald, for the build; not yet confirmed by AA or Greg): the answers stand, and the step rail gains a suggestion. Insurance on the Booking still never removes a Contract from those offered, but the Booking's insurer or funder now marks one path "Suggested". Donald: "The booking's funder only suggests a path." It points to the funding tag that names that insurer or funder (Southern Cross Health Society for SXAP), or to the insurer's own Contracts under Special (for example NIB), and the office can still choose any path (AR-58 sections 2.6 and 7.2). Donald names "an insurer or funder" among the kinds of Party; reading insurers (NIB, Southern Cross Health Society) as their own kind, apart from funders (ACC, Health NZ, NZDF, Veterans Affairs), is our reading, and with it the missing approval number warns only when the Booking's funder is an insurer, so an ACC or Health NZ Booking does not warn.
