Greg: "the booking does hold the funding source. That's where it's meant to be defined." The guide: the surgeon's booking often shows the patient's insurance status (for example SXAP), which guides which Contract is chosen, but the Contract decides who is billed; when the Booking says an insurer will pay but no insurer Contract is chosen, the office is warned before billing. The draft design leaves where the indication is stored open, and OQ-55's answer said neither the Patient nor the Booking holds it.

Do we store an insurance or funding indication on the Booking (or Procedure), from what source, and does it narrow the [Contract list](US-04.3.2) or only drive the [review warning](US-07.2.2)? The guide's question 7 (is a warning the right response) is also unanswered.

Recommendation: Store a simple insurance or funding indication on the Booking, taken from the source data and editable by the office; raise a soft warning at office review when it says an insurer pays but no insurer Contract is chosen. Whether it also suggests the [insurer's](US-11.4.1) Contracts is still open (see the answer). It never decides who is billed.

## Answer

Partly answered: the insurer and the approval number are stored on the Booking ([US-11.4.3](US-11.4.3)), from the source data, and the office picks up the approval reference. Still open: whether insurance narrows the Contract list, and whether Greg's "approval number" is the design's insurance acceptance reference.

- Greg, 16 October, after talking with Ben and Vanessa: "there's a big difference between being insured and having your insurance claim approved." The rooms usually settle the insurance before the booking reaches the List, so the missing piece is "the approval number": "I've added the approval number to the booking data so that there's a place to put it." If a booking says insured with no reference, Vanessa rings the surgeon's rooms. See Notes 2026-10-16 · AA meeting with Greg #1.
- The v6 design holds the insurer's name and an insurance acceptance reference on each booking procedure, set by the office or the anaesthetist. See Notes 2026-10-16 · Pricing model documents v6 #5.
- A code such as SXAP tells the office the hospital will arrange Southern Cross cover and is the billable party ("So you can set up the contract that way"), not whether cover is approved. See Notes 2026-10-14 · AA meeting with Greg #3.
- Whether the insurer has approved the charges is a gap in the information chain, followed up by Vanessa with the hospital; the more common failure is the wrong party being billed, which needs a session with Vanessa. See Notes 2026-10-14 · AA meeting with Greg #14 #16.
- The approval reference is the office's job to pick up, not a requirement on the anaesthetist when completing the Booking. See Notes 2026-10-16 · AA meeting with Greg #57.
- Still open: whether it narrows the Contract list. It is left open; v6 no longer lists "the insurer" among the context that filters Contracts (hospital, surgeon or rooms, anaesthetist). See Notes 2026-10-16 · Pricing model documents v6 #5 #31.
- A patient-billed Contract on a Booking that notes insurance raises a warning at office review, not a block; the design still marks this "TBC". See Notes 2026-10-16 · Pricing model documents v6 #5 #39.

Still open: whether the "approval number" Greg named is the same field as the design's insurance acceptance reference; it is not stated.
