OQ-58's answer raises the prepayment invoice "only if the patient is the billable party"; in the meeting Donald said "we say patient as a generic term", meaning the person paying for the patient (for example a guardian). Does a prepayment apply when the billable party is a guardian, and when it is an insurer or hospital?

Recommendation: any person paying for the patient, never an organisation.

## Answer

That's right, it's any person paying for the patient, never an organisation.

The meeting adds: Greg: "prepayments are always patient-direct... there's no hospital involved in this process." (See Notes 2026-10-02 · AA meeting with Greg #14)

The meeting adds: in the v6 design the typical prepay Contract (an anaesthetist's price list) has a holder who is not billed, so the payer on the Booking (the patient or a nominated person) is who prepays. (See "Notes 2026-10-16 · Pricing model documents v6 #30")

The meeting adds, and revises the answer above (28 October): "never an organisation" is dropped. In practice the one who prepays is a person, the payer on the Booking, but the system does not forbid it: any Contract holder's Contract can be configured as prepaid. Greg: "I can't think of a case where the converse is true, but the configuration engine lets you do it"; "we built an engine that you can configure all these switches in... it's flexible and permissive... if the DHB had a contract, part of that contract may be that the procedures are prepaid. Can't imagine this." "Just take that sentence out." (See "Notes 2026-10-28 · AA meeting with Greg #6")
