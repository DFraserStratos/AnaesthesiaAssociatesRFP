[Disabling a Contract](US-04.1.2) hides it from the step rail. A Procedure already on it keeps it and bills as normal (our reading, AR-58 A14).

Should a Disabled Contract on a Procedure not yet authorised raise a flag at [office review](US-07.2.2), so the office can decide whether to move it?

What hangs on it: whether disabling a Contract AA no longer uses can quietly bill Bookings already set up on it.

1. **No flag.** The Procedure bills as normal.
2. **A review flag, never a block.** The office sees it at review and decides.

No recommendation from the evidence. Raised from section 11.2, point 4, of the [contract model proposal](AR-58).

## Answer

Answered 18 November (Donald, for the build; not yet discussed with AA). Option 2: a review flag, never a block.

At [office review](US-07.2.2), a Procedure not yet authorised whose Contract is Disabled is flagged, so the office decides whether to keep it or move it to another Contract. The office approves or corrects it as it does any other review flag. This is the same check as the existing flag for a Contract that is no longer [offered on the List](US-04.3.2) (as after [a move](US-04.3.11)): a Disabled Contract is offered on no List.

Only Procedures dated on or after the day the Contract was disabled are flagged. A Procedure dated before then was done while the Contract was in use and bills as normal without a flag. The day it was disabled comes from the [Contract audit](US-04.1.3); a Contract still has no dates of its own.

This sits alongside the OQ-133 answer, which warns the office when it disables an old Contract after a dated change while Procedures dated after the change are still on it. Together: a warning when the Contract is disabled, then a flag at review for each Procedure still on it.
