Cosmetic combinations (for example abdominoplasty, breast lift and liposuction) are generally prepaid or fixed price and billed as one combined operation. They are split only when a billable party asks, for example Southern Cross asking for three invoices, and the component prices may not add up to the bundle price because the bundle can carry a discount. Should the system model a combined procedure as (a) a bundle master procedure with a fixed price, (b) several Procedures in one Booking priced by a Contract, or (c) one Procedure that is only split into components on invoices? When it is [split](US-08.6.4), is the original combined invoice credited?

Recommendation: (a) for the bundle, with the split done by additional invoices (component prices independent of the bundle price). This shapes how the multi-procedure rule and additional invoices interact.

## Answer

Answered in Oct 1st Transcript

In short, we will filter / group the procedure list by body headings.
Then you can pick a contract based on the procedure

A combo procedure exists as a contract against each of the relevant parent procedures

From the transcript:

- The master procedure list is a clean list of standard single procedures. A combination is a Contract, not a procedure, and one combination Contract sits against each of its parent procedures.
- The user picks a procedure (grouped and filtered by RVG body headings), then a Contract from those for that procedure, filtered by the hospital, which is always known.
- Greg's alternative, an "operation" level holding several procedures under one Contract with time and modifiers on the operation, was not adopted. The simpler model goes into the next prototype to see how it feels.
- The nesting is: a day holds slots or Lists, a List holds Bookings, a Booking holds procedures, a procedure holds a Contract, and the Contract may be split.
- When a billable party wants a combined price broken into parts, additional invoices are raised against the procedure. Once a combination is chosen, history shows only the Contract.
- Not settled: whether the original combined invoice is credited when it is split, and how thousands of Contracts are kept navigable.
