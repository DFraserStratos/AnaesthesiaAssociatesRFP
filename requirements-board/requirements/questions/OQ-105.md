**Already settled:** a surgeon's arrangement is a Contract like any other (Greg, 16 October).

**Still to answer, for Donald:**

1. **Is a surgeon's fixed-price arrangement held by the surgeon, or by their rooms?** That decides whether it is offered on every Booking with that surgeon, or with any surgeon of those rooms. The v6 design can name the surgeon on a Contract but calls surgeon fixed-price contracts "not fully resolved".
2. **Where it is offered, does the office pick it by hand, or does the system suggest it?**

Recommendation: hold it as a Contract of the surgeon's rooms, linked to the surgeons it covers, offered on their Bookings after the hospital's Contracts, picked by the office.

Some surgeons' rooms have fixed prices with AA for regular procedures, "to give their patients price certainty" (for example Merivale Plastics' price list). Greg (14 October): "this discussion has shown a flaw in my document already... how does the system know that contract is relevant to this booking"; "I haven't accommodated that... in the data model... It's just a lookup hole." Donald: "Isn't that just a fixed price contract of the surgeon?"

The v6 design lets a contract holder name a practitioner, which "can also be used to indicate when the contract is with a surgeon", so its Contracts are only selectable on that surgeon's Bookings, but it also says surgeon fixed-price contracts are "not fully resolved".

Update 2026-10-28: both readings were aired again; today's Contracts are held against individual surgeons. Greg: a surgeon "may have an arrangement with AA... So anytime that surgeon does that procedure, whichever anaesthetist is working on it will... be doing that price"; the patient always pays AA. On rooms, Greg: "there's not a requirement to be able to hold these contracts by surgeon's rooms because it's not currently done, but it probably is a better generic model." Donald: "There's no rooms... it's all against individual surgeons", then restated a model close to the recommendation: "you could create a contract for a surgeon's room and you just name it whatever you want to name it, but then you apply it to individual surgeons" ("It's a better model" is said in between, and it is unclear which model it means). Greg's only objection was that filtering Contracts by practitioner gets "a bit uglier" with rooms. On part 2, Greg: the v6 design filters the Contracts at booking setup by "the practitioner ID of the surgeon and... the practitioner ID of the anaesthetist", so the office picks from a filtered list; the prepaid price list story should name "the anaesthetist or surgeon's own price list", identified by practitioner ID. Not settled: Greg still sees rooms as the better generic model ("we might build it into the theoretical model"). See "Notes 2026-10-28 · AA meeting with Greg #5 #46"

## Answer

Held against the individual surgeon, not the rooms (Donald, 28 October; Greg did not object to it as a requirement). A surgeon's fixed-price arrangement is a Contract that belongs to the surgeon, identified by practitioner ID, so it is offered on that surgeon's Bookings whichever anaesthetist does the work. Greg: "anytime that surgeon does that procedure, whichever anaesthetist is working on it will... be doing that price." Donald: "There's no rooms... it's all against individual surgeons." A Contract may be named for a rooms ("you just name it whatever you want to name it"), but it is applied to individual surgeons.

The office picks it at booking setup from the Contract list, which is filtered by "the practitioner ID of the surgeon and... the practitioner ID of the anaesthetist" (Greg).

Greg agreed only that holding Contracts by surgeon's rooms is not a current requirement, "because it's not currently done"; he still prefers rooms as "probably... a better generic model" and said "we might build it into the theoretical model". See "Notes 2026-10-28 · AA meeting with Greg #5 #46"

Update 2026-10-29: one Contract, linked to one or more surgeons (Donald, product owner). The decisions to hold the arrangement against individual surgeons and to link one Contract to many are Donald's, on 28 and 29 October. A surgeon's fixed-price arrangement is one Contract that can be linked to many surgeons, for example the surgeons of one rooms; there is not one Contract per surgeon. The office picks it from the [Contract list](US-04.3.2) filtered by practitioner ID, and a Contract linked to several surgeons is offered on a Booking with any of them. This settles what the answer left open: a Contract may be named for a rooms but is applied to individual surgeons, and Greg said one-to-many makes the selection "a bit uglier". (See Typed decision 2026-10-29 · Donald (product owner))
