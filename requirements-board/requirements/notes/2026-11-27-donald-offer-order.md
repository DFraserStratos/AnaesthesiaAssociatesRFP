- **Covered:** what the four admin-only priority tiers are called in the app, and where the short and
  long forms of the names are used.
- **Standing:** Donald's decision, made for the build. Items it changes go to Verify, not Agreed.
- **Inputs gathered here:** Donald's typed messages in the session, and the options Claude set out
  (offer order, priority words, first call, Vanessa's metals, role names, numbers with a reframed

## Donald's decisions

1. **"Tier 1 to Tier 4" is not understood at a glance.** Donald: "I think our naming of tier 1, 2, 3,
   and 4 might not be instantly recognizable or understandable. There's been points when I've been
   testing the app and I'm not sure whether tier 4 is good or tier 1 is good." Bears on OQ-102,
   whose interim answer was the plain labels Tier 1 to Tier 4 until AA picks names.
2. **The tiers are an offer order.** Donald: "I think I like your offer order as updated language
   here." The setting is called the **offer order**, and its four values, highest first, are
   **Offer first**, **Offer second**, **Offer third** and **Standard** (the default). The names say
   what the setting does: who the office offers extra or cover work to first. "Standard" is neutral,
   so the default says nothing hurtful if it is ever seen. What each value holds is unchanged:
   directors, particularly helpful shareholders, other shareholders, then non-shareholders. The
   pairing names, "Preferred" and "Not preferred", are unchanged. Bears on US-01.3.6, US-01.3.5,
   US-13.6.4, US-12.1.4, US-01.4.2, US-01.4.5, US-01.4.7 and OQ-102.
3. **A short form where a heading gives the context.** Donald: "When we're showing them in a list or
   a table, rather than saying offer first, maybe we shorten it down to first, second, third,
   standard." Accepted with Claude's refinement ("Yes, please go ahead"): the short form First,
   Second, Third, Standard is used wherever an "Offer order" heading or label sits beside it (the
   field on the anaesthetist's card, a table column, a filter); the long form is used where the name
   stands alone, such as the pill on a row of the office's anaesthetist pickers. Bears on US-01.3.6
   and US-12.1.4.
