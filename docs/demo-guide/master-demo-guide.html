The prototype in one page

A fully interactive React front end over a mock in-browser backend that demonstrates every major feature of the RFP's candidate architecture. It is a demo prototype, not production software: all data is fictional and every external system is simulated.

The 30-second explanation. Anaesthesia Associates coordinates bookings and billing for about 85 independent anaesthetists. The system maintains a rolling AM/PM schedule, keeps patient Bookings current, lets anaesthetists capture BTM billing data, lets the office check and authorise completed Lists, then calculates and issues invoices. Xero handles receivables and banking, while the Billing Engine stays the system that translates financial events back into each anaesthetist's view.

The mental model

Schedule
  → Day
    → Anaesthetist
      → AM List and PM List
        → Booking (one patient appointment)
          → Procedure(s)
            → Billing line(s)
  • Every active anaesthetist has two sessions a day, AM and PM, from their start date, over a rolling horizon (a setting, four months today). Every session starts Free.
  • The anaesthetist's calendar is the session's status; the office puts a List (hospital, surgeon, Bookings) into a session, or a recurring booking does, and the List then shows in its place. The app says session, never "slot".
  • A List is not an employment shift, and a Booking is not a shift application.
  • The anaesthetist completes Bookings and submits the whole List; the office checks and authorises it; authorisation locks the Bookings and hands the List to the Billing Engine.

The lifecycle

ACTIVE → SUBMITTED → AUTHORISED → Billing run completed
  • ACTIVE: anaesthetist and office may update; a hospital row changes a Booking only when the office applies it on the matching screen.
  • SUBMITTED: the office reviews and corrects; the anaesthetist sees it less prominent ("Done · unbilled") and can still change it until it is authorised. The last change stands, and Review shows "Changed since submitted".
  • AUTHORISED: Bookings are locked, and each Procedure's Contract and price are locked onto it ("Contract locked at authorise"); the whole List hands to billing. The List leaves the anaesthetist's main view at once, its invoices show in their balances at once, and search finds it.
  • Billing-run completion stamps billedAt for the office's billing monitor; it no longer decides what the anaesthetist sees. There is no RETURNED state.
  • A List's state is DRAFT (a Draft List, no anaesthetist yet), ACTIVE, SUBMITTED or AUTHORISED; a session's availability is separate.

0 · Create and maintain the schedule canvas

The system ensures every active anaesthetist has two Lists a day across the rolling horizon; the anaesthetist's calendar (days off, repeating days off and Public sessions) sets each session's status directly, nothing reconciled; the seeded public sessions are Public with the note CPH, each anaesthetist with a weekly one having a weekly Public series; recurring bookings then project their Lists, empty, after the calendar (a closed session is not painted; Phase 31 makes it a Draft List), and a change reapplies at once through "Apply to canvas now"; hospital holidays flag Lists; conflicts surface without deleting the List and clear when the clash goes or the office clears them. Ready

In Admin, the office also maintains surgeons (each holding one or more specialties, a practice name and their contacts; the HPI CPN is filled in by admin staff, unique when held), surgeon groups, hospital contact emails, AA's list of specialties, its funding tags (SXAP, HNZ, ACC, in the order they are offered), and its private pairing preferences and anaesthetist priority tiers. The system keeps no surgeons' rooms: a surgeon holds a practice name, one personal contact and one or more office contacts (practice manager, PA or shared inbox: name, email, phone, blanks allowed). The update email to a surgeon goes to the List's sender when the List came from a rooms' PDF, otherwise to the first office contact with an email, with the others offered; never every contact and never the personal contact. Anaesthetists never see preferences or tiers, and an anaesthetist handing on their own List gets no warning.

Triggers: a recurring booking, a surgeon's call, hospital bookings arriving (the office imports the hospital's file, St George's and Southern Cross), the rooms' emailed PDF list (uploaded with the address it came from), or a missing Booking found on the day. Handoff: the Booking appears on her List with the hospital's wording. Manual fallbacks are first-class: phone advice (office) and mobile/web manual Booking; hand entry keeps the procedure as typed or told. Each Booking holds its procedure text as received from each channel; the hospital's later text replaces the surgeon's rooms' once the office applies its row, and the original stays in the history. A request with no anaesthetist yet becomes a Draft List, which can take Bookings before it is assigned. Every change writes lastModifiedBy/At and append-only audit. Ready

4 · Capture BTM and complete a Booking

What a Procedure asks for follows its Contract's pricing kind: on an RVG price the RVG code, start and finish times and the modifiers step are required (None apply answers the step); on a fixed or open price, no times, units or modifiers, and the RVG code is optional, for reference. Mark complete checks exactly three things: a Contract, the RVG detail on an RVG price, and a price (not Needs a price); what only the office can fix goes to it with Alert the office. Per Procedure on an RVG price: pick an RVG code by typing (on a ranged group the starting figure can be changed; a value outside the guide raises an office warning, never a block), record start and finish, and the system computes tiered Time units (1/15 min for the first two hours, then 1/10 min), plus the modifiers, at the anaesthetist's own value per unit. She sees what the price comes to as she records: a quiet Price card in the capture ("4 units x $26.50 = $106.00") and the Booking total above Mark complete (the phone's dock strip, the web rail panel), rising as she claims a modifier or adds time, with no prompt. She may add a fixed billing line, and type a price where the Contract allows it, no reason needed; elsewhere the figures are read-only ("Set by {Contract}. When every active Booking is complete, the List is submittable. Ready

5 · Submit the whole List and office review

The anaesthetist marks the List complete and submits; it becomes SUBMITTED, stays on her main view less prominent, and she can still change it until the office authorises it (Review shows "Changed since submitted"; a reopened Booking must be completed before authorise). The office reviews the full set (each Procedure's text, Contract with who is invoiced and RVG code, the payer, the insurer or funder and approval number, alerts, edits since submit, BTM, and flags that never block: a Contract change, a typed price beside the computed one, a No charge pill on a $0.00 price, a Contract not offered on this List, Needs an invoice destination, Not marked completed), approves or corrects each Contract change (authorising approves any still waiting), corrects office-owned data, optionally logs a phone note, and authorises. Authorising writes each Booking's lock: each Procedure's Contract and its settings, the range in force, units, rate and discount, the price, who is billed and who is paid (the List's anaesthetist, fixed then). One list-authorised event hands the whole List, with its locks, to the Billing Engine; a $0.00 Booking raises a $0.00 "No charge" invoice, with no payable and no BCTI. Review is a human sanity check, not an automatic gate. Ready

6 · Calculate and issue invoices

The Billing Engine reads only each Booking's lock, written at authorise: it never looks up a Contract, payer, rate per unit or RVG group afresh and never swaps in a default, so a Contract edit after authorise changes nothing. Each Procedure's locked Contract decides who is billed: its holder Party when it bills its holder, otherwise the Procedure's payer (the patient, or whoever Paid by names; Procedures sharing a payer share one invoice); the Booking's insurer or funder never decides it. It takes each price from the lock, applies the multi-procedure rule to RVG-priced Procedures, bills each party its share where a Procedure's fee is split on the Booking (the Split button in its Billing card's Paid by, typed by the office or the anaesthetist: $ or %, one party taking the rest), and makes one invoice per billable party per Booking (one per share where split). Each invoice is in the name of the payee locked at authorise, with their GST number, and AA as agent (provisional, OQ-29), and prints from the lock (the Locked record card; Regenerate from locked data rebuilds it identically); the layout comes from the Contract (the holder's when it bills its holder, otherwise the patient's) and GST is added at the foot. The run sends or queues each invoice by the Party's delivery: emailed, or To post for a payer with only a postal address (provisional, OQ-122); a payer with neither raises No invoice address until the office types one. The office resends only. RFP ambiguity: two invoices arise where a Procedure's fee is split on the Booking. The List left the anaesthetist's main view at authorise; its invoices show in her balances at once, and search finds it afterwards. Ready

7 · Collect money and disburse to the anaesthetist

The Billing Engine creates a Xero pair: ACCREC (what the payer owes AA) and a DRAFT ACCPAY (the full amount AA owes the anaesthetist, equal to the ACCREC; AA's own fee is invoiced separately, never deducted). Both carry the InvoiceNumber and Reference. A webhook (with a daily reconciliation poll as safety net) marks payment; the payable authorises for exactly the amount received, including a partial; the payables run disburses. Paid into AA and disbursed to the anaesthetist are separate states. A prepayment is the exception: its payable credits the AA Holding account and is never paid itself; the anaesthetist is paid only after the transfer at authorisation (workflow 8, Pre-payment). The apps read the Billing Engine's own ledger, AA's system of record, never Xero directly: every invoice is a ledger pair (a receivable from the payer, a payable to the anaesthetist) created with the invoice, payments are recorded in the ledger first, the payables run pays from the payable legs, and Xero mirrors both. The office checks on Admin → Ledger that receipts held equal payables due plus the AA Holding account, and follows up any anaesthetist on its out-of-balance list (catch-up Phase 36). Ready

7a · Monthly AA fee invoicing

The office maintains AA fee settings (several fixed items and a charge per BCTI, all ex GST; a change applies from the next run). At month end, Run monthly fee invoices counts each anaesthetist's BCTIs (one per receivable invoice, once, against the anaesthetist who did the procedure, in the month it is paid in full; a prepayment's payable is not counted, provisional, OQ-29) and raises one AA-FEE invoice to each active anaesthetist, with GST at the foot and a Xero ACCREC against their existing contact (no ACCPAY). The anaesthetist pays it into AA's own account; it is never deducted from a payment to them, and Admin and web Accounts → AA fees show it paid or unpaid. The fixed schedule, the paid-only count and BCTI granularity are with AA's accountant. Ready

8 · Failures, late additions and compliance

Pre-payment (catch-up Phase 27): a Procedure on a prepaid Contract, picked by the office, is invoiced to its payer in full, one prepayment invoice per payer; the office approves and sends it on Admin Invoices (a switchable step), crediting the AA Holding account, and an unpaid one is a warning that turns strong as the date nears, never a block (AA's decision, OQ-57); it is left off the final invoice. The AA Holding account (catch-up Phase 27a) is a payables creditor kept like an anaesthetist's account: not a bank account (the money sits in the trust bank account), not a Xero ledger account, never paid itself; money leaves it only by a transfer or a refund the system raises. Two steps: the payer's money is collected into it (nothing is paid to the anaesthetist; its card on the Billing monitor shows the balance and a Received entry), then transferred automatically to the anaesthetist who did the List when the office authorises it (provisional, OQ-125), with no office step, no new invoice and no GST movement; the next payables run pays them, and the payment itself is made in Xero (OQ-111). Money paid after the transfer goes straight to the anaesthetist's payable. Two payers mean two invoices, each with its own payable to the AA Holding account and its own transfer. A move changes nothing: nothing is repointed, and the transfer pays whoever did the List. A prepayment's payable is not counted as a BCTI (provisional, OQ-29). Warnings: one routine; a small triangle on the Booking, the warning at the top of the Booking on opening, the Day view outline, and the office's To-do card with Clear; no confirm step at submit. Catch-up Phase 21 adds two mild rules: a child as the payer, and an insurer named with no approval number (never ACC or Health NZ). Profile and bank details (catch-up Phase 26): each anaesthetist's profile holds their rate per unit, phone, email, registration number, HPI CPN and bank account. The anaesthetist sees it on the More tab and the web Profile page and edits only their own rate per unit; contact details, HPI CPN and bank details are held by the office, read-only with a lock, the bank number masked (Dr Souter's 99-0101-•••••21-00). The office keeps every profile on the Admin anaesthetist record and in Master data, Anaesthetists; the HPI CPN and bank details are mandatory. Bank details are display-only master data: the payment is made in Xero. Post-op: a linked addendum Booking with its own cycle; the original stays immutable. Billing failure: per Booking, and a List never fails as a whole (OQ-05): the run prices only from the lock, so what can still fail is outside it (here the Xero hand-off); the failed Booking is held back while the rest of the List bills, and the office retries it once from the Billing monitor without editing any Contract or other master. Row the office cannot match: an unmatched row, or one with no NHI, waits in the unmatched queue; a row with no NHI places a stub on its List, never a Booking, never counted and never blocking submit or authorise, and adding the NHI puts the Booking in the stub's place, while rejecting the row removes it (our reading); a duplicate row is skipped; a later update waits for approval, matched by date, surgeon, hospital and session; a Booking moved to another day arrives as a cancellation plus a new Booking; a row for a submitted List can still be applied by the office; a row for an authorised List cannot be applied. Future scope: automatic hospital sync and automated hospital changes are Future Work, and an HL7 v2 message that fails retries then dead-letters for manual intervention. Compliance: everything is audited, both NHI formats validate, and no NHI or other personal information enters Xero, patient names included (confirmed with AA). Ready

Cheat sheet

The rules most likely to be questioned, and the phrasing that keeps the demo faithful to the RFP.

Fee calculation

Fee = (Base + Time + Modifier units) × anaesthetist's own dollar value per unit
  • The anaesthetist Booking shows what the price comes to as she records, on every Contract: a quiet Price card in the capture ("4 units x $26.50 = $106.00") and the Booking total above Mark complete (the phone's dock strip, the web rail panel), rising as she claims a modifier or adds time, with no prompt. She types a price only where an RVG Contract allows it (No contract · RVG, Forte Health RVG), or the amount on No contract · Fixed price; no reason is asked. Elsewhere the figures are read-only: "Set by {Contract}. To use a different price, change the Contract or alert the office." Admin Bookings keep the full billing view.
  • Aria Skin and Laser Clinic is RVG at the Contract's rate, $26.50 a unit, which the anaesthetist cannot change: Dr Fitzgerald's Wed 15 Jul Aria Booking is 22 units x $26.50 = $583.00 (A5 base 8 + time 14, 08:30 to 11:30). An added billing line is a fixed amount only.
  • A price typed at $0.00 keeps the RVG detail recorded ("This gives a no-charge invoice."). Review shows a No charge pill, and authorising raises a $0.00 "No charge" invoice: no payable, no BCTI.
  • Each Contract has one of five pricing kinds (RVG at the anaesthetist's rate, RVG at the Contract's rate, RVG less the Contract's discount, a fixed price from its range in force, or an open price typed on the Procedure). A Procedure's price follows its Contract (the range in force, the rate or discount as they stand) until a typed price, a prepayment invoice or AUTHORISED holds it; else "Needs a price". The office can type any price until AUTHORISED, a prepaid one only until its invoice is raised; no reason. Admin → Booking → Change price shows the computed price beside the field.
  • Reset → Confirm reset in the shared top bar restores the pristine data and clock from any app while keeping the current app.
  • Closing things: Escape closes only the top layer (a sheet over a drawer closes first, then the drawer) and returns focus to what opened it. Every web and Admin dialog has a corner X (Close) at its top right. A phone sheet has no X: its handle or a tap on the scrim closes it. The Demo actions pill, live clock, Reset popup and app switcher also close on Escape or an outside click.
  • Time: 1 unit per 15 min for the first two hours, then 1 unit per 10 min.
  • Each anaesthetist has an independent value per unit.
  • The anaesthetist picks the RVG code by typing; it is optional, and the system does not infer it.
  • A ranged group starts at its starting figure; any value is accepted, and one outside the guide raises a warning for the office after the procedure, never a block.
  • Base units sit on the RVG group alone; a Contract states none and may only name a default RVG code. Choosing a Contract fills an empty RVG code from its default; a Procedure that has one keeps it.
  • P1 (non-supine positioning) is included, pre-applied at 0 (removable), on every Neurosurgery and Spine code, so it cannot be added twice; the age modifier is pre-applied from the date of birth, removable.
  • Every other modifier, ASA included, is claimed with a short reason; modifiers stack with no limit, and the same modifier is never added twice.
  • A part interval always rounds up (confirmed with AA); the tiers are held as data (the RVG time rule under Master data, RVG groups, read only). The anaesthetist enters start and finish only.
  • The form follows the Contract's pricing kind: RVG code, start and finish and the modifiers step (None apply is an answer) on an RVG price; none of it on a fixed or open price, the code only for reference. Mark complete checks a Contract, the RVG detail on an RVG price, and a price (not Needs a price); never the payer's contact, the approval number or out-of-range base units.
  • Review flags never block: Changed by Dr X: was the old Contract (Approve or Correct), Price typed: $X (computed $Y), Contract not offered on this List, Needs an invoice destination, Not marked completed, Email or postal address needed, manual B/T/M notes. A Disabled Contract raises Contract disabled on <day> for a Procedure not yet authorised and dated on or after that day (never a block; Make active clears it). The one blocker is a Procedure that still Needs a price: Authorise for billing stays disabled and names it, with an Open link, until a price is typed. Approve all N Contract changes; authorising approves any still waiting. No move resets a Contract or typed price.

Patient insurance distinction

nib is billed only through nib's own Contract. A patient with insurance who pays AA is on No contract · RVG (or a price list) and claims back themselves (the AIA patient). Not the same workflow. The Booking names its insurer or funder (a Party) and holds the approval number, editable by the office and on the day by the anaesthetist, who sees the number; a missing number is the only insurance warning, for an insurer only (NIB, Southern Cross Health Society), shown to the office and the anaesthetist. The funder never decides who is billed or narrows the Contracts; it marks one path Suggested.

The payer on each Procedure

Each Contract decides who is billed: its holder, or the Procedure's payer. The payer is on each Procedure's Paid by line, the patient from the patient record unless Change names someone else (a parent, guardian, benefactor or family trust); the office and the anaesthetist change it until authorised (bottom sheet on the phone, dialog on web). Changing one Procedure's payer offers the change to the Booking's other Procedures with the same payer, each ticked (untick to keep); a Procedure added later copies the payer the others share, else the patient; with no Contract the line reads "Payer: not billed yet". There is no Booking-level Payer card; the insurer or funder and approval number stay on the Booking (empty state "None"). Procedures sharing a payer share one invoice, and one prepayment invoice per payer. A billed payer needs an email or an NZ Post postal address (post alone is enough); Review flags one with neither, never a block.

Invoices

Contracts

Every Contract is one priced item held by a Party (a hospital, insurer, funder, surgeon or surgeon group, an anaesthetist, or AA). It bills its holder or the payer on the Booking (a prepaid Contract always the payer); it has a pricing kind, an optional override on RVG kinds, prepaid or not, and is Active or Disabled. It is edited in place with an audit, never deleted; a change from a date is a new Contract, except a fixed price, which changes through price ranges. Each priced item is a Contract of its own, with a holder reference code, an invoice description and a default RVG code; the range in force on the procedure date gives a fixed price, and a new range, even backdated, re-prices everything not yet authorised, prepaid or priced by hand, while an authorised Booking and its invoices never change (they keep their lock): the ranges then list each authorised Procedure that kept its old price. Disabling a Contract, or making a new one that replaces it from a date, lists the not-yet-authorised Procedures still on it, each with Open; nothing moves, and Review flags each one dated on or after the disable day (Contract disabled on <day>). A combination such as Merivale's Facelift plus 1 add on is its own Contract; fee extras are events. An anaesthetist's price list is one prepaid Contract per item, kept by the office, every price above zero (Dr Souter's). No Contract prices an additional procedure by its own rule. No contract · RVG, held by AA, is always there; every Contract has AA's own code. A Contract carries none or more specialties (none means every specialty) and at most one funding tag (SXAP, HNZ, ACC); with who it bills, the tag decides where it is offered (Patient, the hospital and tag, or Special), shown live in the editor. The system keeps one Fixed price Contract, marked System, for every place an invoice can go; tagging a hospital's first Contract with a new tag makes its one at once, and only its name, AA code and invoice description change. Tip: find a Contract by typing its name, AA code, holder reference code, a word of its invoice description or its default RVG code ("CAT", "H3"); filter by holder or funding tag.

Authorising a List locks each Procedure's Contract onto it: the Contract's settings, the price range in force, the units, rate and discount, the price, who is billed (with the patient and payer details as they stood), the split and who is paid. The run prices from the lock alone, with no fallback: nothing is swapped for a default or another Contract, and a later Contract edit, Disable, backdated range, unit-value or RVG group change never reaches an authorised Booking, while an ACTIVE Booking still follows its Contract. The invoice keeps its recipe: it prints from the lock (the Locked record card), and Regenerate from locked data rebuilds it and says identical; the recipe, not a replay of the data as at a date. Which fields are frozen is Donald's proposal (AR-58 2.8), to confirm with AA (US-15.0.3). An invoiced Booking changes only by a credit note and rebill (a later build).

Contract history

A Contract is edited in place, every change audited with its values before and after, who and when; nothing is deleted (a Contract is Disabled, never removed). There are no versions, no staged or submitted price review and no upload. The Contract panel's History tab lists each change (for example "Contract updated · Kirsty W. · office", Pricing: RVG at $23.00 per unit → RVG at $24.00 per unit), then Procedures locked against this Contract, each linked to its Booking and invoice. A seeded Contract reads "No changes yet" until it is edited. Parties and RVG groups have their own History too.

The money model

Payer → ACCREC → AA account → ACCPAY (full amount) → Anaesthetist
  • ACCREC is money AA is collecting; ACCPAY is the full amount AA owes the anaesthetist, equal to the ACCREC. AA's own fee is never deducted from it (it is invoiced to the anaesthetist separately).
  • Paid into AA and disbursed to the anaesthetist are separate states.
  • A prepayment's ACCPAY credits the AA Holding account instead, and is never paid itself; the money is transferred to the anaesthetist when the List is authorised (card 8 below).
  • A partial payment releases the payable for exactly the amount received; the rest stays outstanding.
  • Both Xero records carry the InvoiceNumber (the ACCPAY's ends -P) and the case Reference.
  • AA's monthly fee: Month end: fee settings + BCTIs paid that month → AA-FEE invoice (ACCREC) → Anaesthetist pays AA's own account; never netted. Sample: $350.00 + $150.00 fixed, $5.00 per BCTI; "Seed a month of BCTIs" then the run gives Dr Rutherford $700.00 before GST ($805.00 with GST).
  • Caveat: the fixed schedule, the paid-only count and BCTI granularity are with AA's accountant; amounts are held ex GST, with GST at the foot of the fee invoice.
  • The apps read the Billing Engine's own ledger, AA's system of record, never Xero directly. Xero mirrors the ledger for receivables and banking.
  • Bank details are master data: the office holds them on each anaesthetist's record (mandatory, with the HPI CPN), the anaesthetist sees them masked and read-only, and the payment itself is made in Xero. The app never pays from them.
  • Outstanding balances are flat individual payable rows, not a Booking-level rollup and not amounts the anaesthetist personally owes.

Internal ledger (catch-up Phase 36)

Receipts held = payables due + the AA Holding account  →  In balance
  • Pairs: every invoice is a pair in the Billing Engine's own ledger, created with the invoice (before and whatever happens at the Xero handoff): a receivable from whoever pays and a payable to the anaesthetist (the invoice number, and the same number with -P). A prepayment's payable is to the AA Holding account; authorising the List adds a transfer row to whoever did it. An AA fee invoice is a pair with no payable, outside the balance. Xero mirrors the pairs; a pair not yet in Xero is counted ("Not yet in Xero").
  • The equation: receipts held less payables due and the AA Holding account is $0.00 when in balance. RFP ambiguities and discovery decisions

Where the RFP is open or internally inconsistent, the prototype picks a reading and labels it. Present these as design proposals to confirm with AA, not settled facts.

1 · NHI in Xero (settled)

One section allowed NHI as a searchable Xero cross-reference; Appendix 2 said NHI never leaves the practice system. Confirmed with AA: no NHI or other personal information in Xero, patient names included.

2 · List disappearance trigger (answered)

A List leaves the anaesthetist's main view when the office authorises it (OQ-31), not at submit, not when billed, not on payment. Its invoices show in their balances at once; older work is found by search on patient name, NHI or date, with no calendar archive.

3 · Billing failure isolation (answered)

The RFP asked whether one failed Booking blocks the whole List. Answered (OQ-05): failure is per Booking and a List never fails as a whole; a failed Booking (a split Booking's invoices with it) is held back while the rest bills. The run prices only from the lock, so what can still fail is outside it, such as the Xero hand-off; the office retries once from the Billing monitor, and the fix never edits a master.

4 · Monitoring location

Admin Web vs a separate Billing Engine screen is left open. The prototype places monitoring in Admin Web.

5 · List reassignment mechanics

Settled by US-01.4.1: the List moves into the colleague's free session with its id, Bookings and history, and nothing about billing is recalculated. Answered (D60, OQ-84): whoever moves a List, the anaesthetist or the office, chooses what the vacated session becomes, Free or Unavailable, on every path; Free is chosen first, and when the move comes from marking the session unavailable, that status is chosen first. Answered (D7, OQ-39): an anaesthetist moves her own List at once, back to the office (a Draft List) or into a colleague's free session; nobody accepts and the office does not confirm. Answered (D15, OQ-65): every such move posts to one shared notification pool in the Admin App, separate from the to-do list. Answered (D58, OQ-79): notices are active or resolved; an admin ticks one (the same tick as a to-do's Clear) and it is hidden for the whole office, Show resolved brings it back with who and when, and nothing expires; an anaesthetist's Alert the office joins the pool (whether an alerted Booking may still be submitted is open; the prototype allows it). Answered (OQ-102's 19 October note, D54): the office's pickers order free colleagues by tier, then preferred pairing; the anaesthetist apps stay by availability alone. Answered (D44, OQ-43): no warning on the anaesthetist's own move; she sees colleagues by availability alone. Answered (D61, OQ-85; D54, OQ-113): she can also move a single Booking, to a colleague free in that session, found by typing a name or from the free list in name order; it always becomes a new List in their session, never added to an existing List (the rule for now), and telling the office is her option. Answered (D38, OQ-80): the payable follows the Booking; a prepaid Booking's payable stays the AA Holding account, nothing is recalculated or repointed, and the transfer when the List is authorised pays whoever did it. Whoever submits a List did every procedure on it.

6 · Hospital-closure conflicts (unavailability is never one)

Answered (OQ-09): a soft warning. Nothing blocks the office; the List is accepted and flagged. A conflict is a List at a closed hospital. An anaesthetist's unavailability, short-notice sickness included, is never a conflict (D59, OQ-81 part 3 answered): whoever marks a booked session unavailable, the office included, returns the List to the office or hands it to a colleague there and then. The List changes colour (amber, with its kind's bar and a reason line) and shows on the Conflicts screen across every date. It clears when the clash goes (the closure moved or deleted, or the List's hospital changed) or when the office clears it, with an optional note and "Flag again". The office never assigns or moves a List onto an unavailable session: it is made available first, with the anaesthetist's agreement (US-01.6.3). Not conflicts: a recurring booking landing on an unavailable session becomes a Draft List (Phase 31), and anyone who marks a booked session unavailable is asked first whether the List returns to the office or goes to a colleague (US-01.5.5).

7 · Multi-procedure invoice count

Grouping text says same-counterparty Procedures share an invoice; the RFP's "Split Billing" section says two invoices, for multiple procedures in one episode and for a partly covered procedure. Invoices group by billable party, one per billable party per Booking, so a multi-procedure Booking with one payer is one invoice and one BCTI; a Split on the Booking gives one invoice per share.

8 · Pre-payment

The RFP requires payment before the procedure. The trigger is Patient › Payment type: Prepaid, picked by the office, per Procedure, invoiced to the Procedure's payer: one prepayment invoice per payer, each payer locked once theirs is raised. The amount is the Procedure's price when the invoice is raised, in full and read-only after, or an amount the office types on No contract · Fixed price at the top of the Prepaid list. Approve and send is a switchable step (Admin Invoices, Prepayment approval, on by default); the pair credits the AA Holding account, not the anaesthetist. The AA Holding account is a payables creditor kept like an anaesthetist's account: not a bank account (the money sits in the trust bank account), not a Xero ledger account, and never paid itself; money leaves it only by a transfer or a refund the system raises (the refund is Phase 41). Admin → Billing monitor shows its card (fresh reset: $1,380.00, Priya Nair, AA-2026-0001). Two steps: the payer's money is collected into the AA Holding account (nothing is paid to the anaesthetist; Run payables pays nothing), then transferred automatically when the office authorises the List, with no office step. The transfer is not a payment: no new invoice, no GST movement; the Xero simulator shows it as a manual journal (MJ-0001 for Nair), and how it posts in Xero is still to be confirmed. The next payables run pays the anaesthetist; the payment itself is made in Xero (OQ-111). The trigger is provisional (OQ-125: Greg said "confirmed and submitted"). Money paid after the transfer goes straight to the anaesthetist's payable; a part-paid prepayment moves in full and is released as money arrives. Two payers mean two invoices, two payables to the AA Holding account and two transfers. A prepayment's payable is not counted as a BCTI (provisional, OQ-29). AA decided not to block (OQ-57): the warning escalates as the date nears (mild, then strong from two days before; D5). After the procedure it is left off the final invoice with nothing deducted, and a move to a colleague changes nothing in the books: nothing is repointed, the transfer pays whoever did the List, and the old anaesthetist never sees it. Demo seed: Annette Riley (awaiting approval, $1,200.00 plus GST) and Priya Nair (the mixed case, paid, AA-2026-0001).

Warnings: one routine, a To-do card and a Pre-payments card in Admin, a triangle on Bookings, the warning at the top of the Booking. Never a block, and no confirm step.

9 · Concurrency

Several sources can change a Booking: the office, the anaesthetist and, later, the hospital feeds. The office saves explicitly, and every save is one change set named for who made it (Switch office user shows two named users in one History). Handling two sources editing at once is Future Work in the catalogue, with the automated integrations (US-02.5.6); the prototype applies each save in order, each audited, and tells an open screen when someone else has saved.

10 · Modifier values

The modifier table is the NZSA RVG 2021 table as printed (30 codes, Admin → Master data → Modifiers); values that are not plain numbers ("2 + Time", "4-6") are entered at capture. Only the age modifier and a modifier the base units include are pre-applied, both removable; nothing else is pre-filled. The age bands are settled (OQ-95 answered, D29). Modifiers stack with no limit; AA's own (Vanessa's list) are not loaded yet, and the office can add one.

11 · Open vs genuinely overdue balances

The billing monitor's "Prior balance" intake flag reads any open (unpaid) prior episode. The RFP asks whether to distinguish open from genuinely overdue; invoices carry a raised date but no due date, so the prototype flags any open balance and holds the distinction as a discovery question.

12 · ACC pre-op flat-fee codes

ACC pre-operative assessment has its own flat-fee code set (CS250, CS260, CS70), separate from the general BTM structure. The prototype captures these as ancillary fixed-amount lines with the code in the description; the code set and rates are a confirmation item with AA billing. ACC itself is not a billing route or a kind of Contract: ACC work bills through a holder's Contract carrying the ACC funding tag, priced like any other.

13 · Specialties and funding tags

The starting specialties are the catalogue's nine plus Neurosurgery, Vascular surgery and Oral and maxillofacial surgery; subspecialties fold in (Colorectal, Breast, Upper GI and HPB under General surgery, Fertility under Gynaecology) until AA's spreadsheet replaces the list. A surgeon may hold more than one specialty (Ben said it is "not a thing"; for AA to confirm), and a Contract is offered when it shares any one. Each funding tag may name its funder. A specialty or tag in use cannot be removed.

15 · Surgeon contacts and practice (no surgeons' rooms)

Answered for the build (OQ-127 and OQ-126; Donald, not yet seen by AA): no surgeons' rooms are kept; each surgeon holds a practice name, one personal contact and one or more office contacts, and a surgeon at two practices holds an office contact for each. Superseded (OQ-128): a surgeon belonging to more than one room, as there are no rooms. Answered for the build (OQ-129): the HPI CPN is filled in by admin staff; a surgeon can be saved and booked without one ("No HPI CPN yet"). Open (OQ-143): the default is that it never becomes required.

Likely evaluator questions

Who actually uses mobile versus web?

The same anaesthetist. Mobile is the in-theatre/on-the-move interface; web is the wider planning and accounts view.

Who creates the shifts?

The system creates AM and PM sessions on the rolling canvas. Recurring bookings supply recurring defaults, painted after the anaesthetist's own calendar. "Apply to canvas now" repopulates the schedule ahead after a change, without touching booked Lists. Office staff reserve and amend Lists and handle exceptions. "Shift" is not the RFP term.

What about cases after six?

The office opens an out-of-hours session after PM for the anaesthetist, even the same evening, and puts a List in it: a new one, or a request already waiting as a Draft List for that evening. It is a session like AM and PM, one a day, 18:00 to 21:00 by default: the anaesthetist can mark it available or not, the finders offer it while it is free, and its Bookings are captured, submitted and billed like any List (S2 Beat 3e).

What about public work?

Public hospital sessions are a status on the session, with an optional note such as CPH, set by the anaesthetist on their calendar or by the office from the Day view (Change status). It is never a kind of List and is never billed. Public is not open for bookings or cover; changing it back to Free reopens the session. What else the office needs to know about public sessions is a discovery point (OQ-139).

Can an anaesthetist edit after submission?

Yes, until the office authorises the List. A submitted List stays on the anaesthetist's main view, less prominent, and they can still change it; every change is audited and Review shows "Changed since submitted". The last change stands, and a Booking reopened after submit must be completed again before the office can authorise. Authorised Bookings are immutable.

What happens when the office finds a mistake?

Office staff phone for clarification and make the correction. There is no Returned state; the anaesthetist can also correct it themselves until the office authorises it.

Why is Xero not doing all the billing?

AA has agency-specific billing rules and needs Procedure-level calculation, grouping and anaesthetist-facing views. Xero is the AR and banking add-on.

Does the anaesthetist get paid before the procedure?

No. A prepayment is held in the AA Holding account until the office authorises the List, then transferred to the anaesthetist who did it automatically, with no office step, and paid by the next payables run. The payment itself is made in Xero (OQ-111). Authorisation as the trigger is provisional (OQ-125).

How do late hospital changes work?

Hospital rows land on the matching screen with their wording exactly as received. A later update is matched to its Booking by date, surgeon, hospital and session and waits for the office to approve it; the office can apply rows up to AUTHORISED (a row for an authorised List cannot be applied). Nothing applies itself, and the hospital never sets the Contract. Applying hospital changes automatically comes after the first release (US-02.5.1 to US-02.5.4).

What if the patient has no NHI?

The RFP is internally inconsistent (unique identifier vs "where available"). The prototype uses a hidden internal ID as the invariant and allows a provisional NHI-pending patient.

A hospital row with no NHI waits at intake on Admin, Intake, Matching; no patient or Booking is made. So the anaesthetist still sees the case, a stub stands on the List in its place: a dashed amber row, "Waiting at intake: no NHI · The office is resolving this", with the time, name and wording as received. A stub is never a Booking: it is never counted ("n of n complete · 1 waiting at intake"), and it never blocks submit or authorise. When the office adds the NHI on Matching, the Booking takes the stub's place; rejecting the row removes the stub. On Reset, Matching already holds Mere Tipene's row (Southern Cross, Dr Souter's Tue 4 Aug PM List): type the NHI ZQA1024 and press Add NHI. The Southern Cross import's Tama Henare takes ZQA1008. How a stub reads, and that the Booking takes its place, are our readings, to confirm with AA.

Who do updates go to?

The List's sender when the List came from a rooms' PDF (the address it arrived from, shown as Replies go to on the List and its Bookings); otherwise the surgeon's first office contact with an email, the others offered. A re-sent PDF from a newer address, once applied, makes that address the sender, the earlier one kept in History. A cover change goes to the hospital. The office drafts the email when it matters, from the Booking, the List drawer or the pool notification, picks the changes and a template, and sends it from its own mail client: nothing is sent automatically in the first release (OQ-82).

Is the demo using real patient or integration data?

No. All names and data are fictional. The hospital files and the rooms' PDF lists are sample files, their procedure wording illustrative (AI-made, not AA's); NHI, email, Xero and payment events are simulated in-browser. Nothing syncs with a hospital: the office imports each file.
