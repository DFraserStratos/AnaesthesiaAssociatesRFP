The prototype in one page

A fully interactive React front end over a mock in-browser backend that demonstrates every major feature of the RFP's candidate architecture. It is a demo prototype, not production software: all data is fictional and every external system is simulated.

The 30-second explanation. Anaesthesia Associates coordinates bookings and billing for about 85 independent anaesthetists. The system maintains a rolling AM/PM schedule, keeps patient Bookings current, lets anaesthetists capture BTM billing data, lets the office check and authorise completed Lists, then calculates and issues invoices. Xero handles receivables and banking, while the Billing Engine stays the system that translates financial events back into each anaesthetist's view.

The mental model

Schedule
  → Day
    → Anaesthetist
      → AM List and PM List
        → Booking (one patient appointment)
          → Procedure(s)
            → Billing line(s)
  • Every active anaesthetist has two sessions a day, AM and PM, from their start date, over a rolling horizon (a setting, four months today). Every session starts Free.
  • The anaesthetist's calendar is the session's status; the office puts a List (hospital, surgeon, Bookings) into a session, or a recurring booking does, and the List then shows in its place. The app says session, never "slot".
  • A List is not an employment shift, and a Booking is not a shift application.
  • The anaesthetist completes Bookings and submits the whole List; the office checks and authorises it; authorisation locks the Bookings and hands the List to the Billing Engine.

The lifecycle

ACTIVE → SUBMITTED → AUTHORISED → Billing run completed
  • ACTIVE: anaesthetist, office and integrations may update.
  • SUBMITTED: the office reviews and corrects; the anaesthetist sees it less prominent ("Done · unbilled") and can still change it until it is authorised. The last change stands, and Review shows "Changed since submitted".
  • AUTHORISED: Bookings are locked; the whole List hands to billing. The List leaves the anaesthetist's main view at once, its invoices show in their balances at once, and search finds it.
  • Billing-run completion stamps billedAt for the office's billing monitor; it no longer decides what the anaesthetist sees. There is no RETURNED state.
  • A List's state is DRAFT (a Draft List, no anaesthetist yet), ACTIVE, SUBMITTED or AUTHORISED; a session's availability is separate.

Who uses each app

Three user-facing apps, two personas. Mobile and web are two contexts for the same anaesthetist role, not two jobs. The Admin app covers several office responsibilities under one persona.

Primary persona 1

Dr Melanie Souter · anaesthetist (mobile-first)

MS · ID 34821. Works independently across hospitals and surgeons; mobile during the day. Captures accurate Base, Time and Modifier data with minimal typing, recording only what each Procedure's Contract price needs, completes Bookings, and submits the whole List.

May: edit own Bookings on ACTIVE Lists, submit when every uncancelled Booking is complete, still change a submitted List of her own until the office authorises it, see colleagues' session availability (not their patient details), move one of her own Lists to a free colleague or back to the office, hand one Booking to a colleague who is free that session (a new List of theirs), alert the office about a Booking or List with a note, and split a Procedure's fee on her own Booking (Split in its Billing card's Paid by) until the List is authorised. The workflows

0 · Create and maintain the schedule canvas

The system ensures every active anaesthetist has two Lists a day across the rolling horizon; the anaesthetist's calendar (days off and repeating days off) sets each session's status directly, nothing reconciled; recurring bookings then project their Lists, empty, after the calendar (a closed session is not painted; Phase 31 makes it a Draft List), and a change reapplies at once through "Apply to canvas now"; hospital holidays flag Lists; conflicts surface without deleting the List and clear when the clash goes or the office clears them. Ready

In Admin, the office also maintains surgeons (each keyed on their HPI CPN and holding one or more specialties), surgeons' rooms, surgeon groups, hospital contact emails, AA's list of specialties, its funding tags (SXAP, HNZ, ACC, in the order they are offered), and its private pairing preferences and anaesthetist priority tiers. Anaesthetists never see preferences or tiers, and an anaesthetist handing on their own List gets no warning.

1 · Reserve a List and populate patient Bookings

Triggers: a recurring booking, a surgeon's call, a hospital message, an emailed PDF, or a missing Booking found on the day. Manual fallbacks are first-class: phone advice (office), mobile/web manual Booking, and PDF review-and-ingest (deduped by NHI); hand entry keeps the procedure as typed or told. Each Booking holds its procedure text as received from each channel; the hospital's replaces the surgeon's rooms' and the original stays in the history. A request with no anaesthetist yet becomes a Draft List, which can take Bookings before it is assigned. Every change writes lastModifiedBy/At and append-only audit. Ready

2 · Plan the day and manage late changes

The office reads the status-coloured grid, moves one Booking for a routine reschedule, edits hospital, surgeon or actual times, soft-cancels with a reason (Bookings stay visible, excluded from billing), and adds internal day notes. The Draft Lists rail card and band show unassigned Lists soonest first, assigned from a grouped, tier-ordered picker, and every List block shows its booking count. A recurring booking that lands on a day the anaesthetist has already blocked out becomes a Draft List for the office. Ready

3 · Find cover: the anaesthetist's own move, or the office's reassignment

The anaesthetist moves one of her Lists to a free colleague or returns it to the office, at once, also when she marks a booked session off (the app asks where the List goes first). She sees colleagues by availability alone, with no preference warning, and the move posts to the office's shared notifications; nothing about billing or prepayment is recalculated. When nobody has arranged cover, the office views free anaesthetists (Find available, or the Reassign list picker: in priority-tier order, with any not-preferred pairing grouped apart and warned, never blocked) and reassigns the whole List: it moves into the colleague's free session with its Bookings and history, keeping its id. Whoever moves a List, the anaesthetist or the office, chooses what the vacated session becomes, Free or Unavailable (OQ-84, answered). A sickness the office records (Change status on the Day view) never leaves the List in place: the same "Where does this List go?" step returns it or hands it on, and Conflicts covers hospital closures only. A colleague marked unavailable is never picked: the session is made available first. An anaesthetist unsure about a Booking or List uses Alert the office with a note; it stays in the office's notifications until ticked resolved. Nothing about billing is recalculated. Ready

An emergency or late case after the PM session: Kirsty opens an out-of-hours session from the anaesthetist's row on the Day view (any day from today, the same evening included; one a day; 18:00 to 21:00 by default) and puts a List in it, a new one or a Draft List waiting for that evening. From then on it is a session like AM and PM: the anaesthetist marks it available or not, the finders offer it while it is free, and its List moves, returns and bills like any other. An empty one can be closed (US-01.1.6). Ready

For one case rather than the whole List, she uses Move to a colleague on the Booking: a colleague free that session, found by typing a name or from the free list in name order, takes it as a new List in their session, never added to an existing List; a colleague on leave is offered only once they set the session available. Telling the office is a tick she may leave off; the colleague sees "Moved to you" either way. It is also the doer rule: whoever submits a List did every procedure on it, so a Booking a colleague does moves to a new List of theirs before the procedure, and its payable follows. A prepaid Booking's payable stays the AA Holding account: the new anaesthetist claims the prepaid amount and nothing is recalculated (US-01.4.7, US-01.4.6, catch-up Phase 32a). Ready

4 · Capture BTM and complete a Booking

What a Procedure asks for follows its Contract's pricing kind: on an RVG price the RVG code, start and finish times and the modifiers step are required (None apply answers the step); on a fixed or open price, no times, units or modifiers, and the RVG code is optional, for reference. Mark complete checks exactly three things: a Contract, the RVG detail on an RVG price, and a price (not Needs a price); what only the office can fix goes to it with Alert the office. Per Procedure on an RVG price: pick an RVG code by typing (on a ranged group the starting figure can be changed; a value outside the guide raises an office warning, never a block), record start and finish, and the system computes tiered Time units (1/15 min for the first two hours, then 1/10 min), plus the modifiers, at the anaesthetist's own value per unit. The age modifier, and P1 on Neurosurgery and Spine codes (included in their base units, at 0), are pre-applied and removable; any other modifier, ASA included, is claimed with a short reason, with no stacking limit; the same modifier is never added twice. An additional Procedure is time-only. When every active Booking is complete, the List is submittable. Ready

5 · Submit the whole List and office review

The anaesthetist marks the List complete and submits; it becomes SUBMITTED, stays on her main view less prominent, and she can still change it until the office authorises it (Review shows "Changed since submitted"; a reopened Booking must be completed before authorise). The office reviews the full set (each Procedure's text, Contract with who is invoiced and RVG code, the payer, the insurer or funder and approval number, alerts, edits since submit, BTM, and flags that never block: a Contract change, a typed price beside the computed one, a Contract not offered on this List, Needs a price, Needs an invoice destination, Not marked completed), approves or corrects each Contract change (authorising approves any still waiting), corrects office-owned data, optionally logs a phone note, and authorises. One list-authorised event hands the whole List to the Billing Engine. Review is a human sanity check, not an automatic gate. Ready

6 · Calculate and issue invoices

The Billing Engine reads each Procedure's one Contract, which decides who is billed: its holder Party when it bills its holder, otherwise the payer named on the Booking (the patient, or whoever the Different payer tick names); the Booking's insurer or funder never decides it. It calculates from the captured snapshot, enforces time-only additional Procedures, bills each party its share where a Procedure's fee is split on the Booking (the Split button in its Billing card's Paid by, typed by the office or the anaesthetist: $ or %, one party taking the rest), and makes one invoice per billable party per Booking (one per share where split). Each invoice is in the anaesthetist's name with their GST number and AA as agent (provisional, OQ-29); the layout comes from the Contract (the holder's when it bills its holder, otherwise the patient's) and GST is added at the foot. The run sends or queues each invoice by the Party's delivery: emailed, or To post for a payer with only a postal address (provisional, OQ-122); a payer with neither raises No invoice address until the office types one. The office resends only. RFP ambiguity: two invoices arise where a Procedure's fee is split on the Booking. The List left the anaesthetist's main view at authorise; its invoices show in her balances at once, and search finds it afterwards. Ready

7 · Collect money and disburse to the anaesthetist

The Billing Engine creates a Xero pair: ACCREC (what the payer owes AA) and a DRAFT ACCPAY (the full amount AA owes the anaesthetist, equal to the ACCREC; AA's own fee is invoiced separately, never deducted). Both carry the InvoiceNumber and Reference. A webhook (with a daily reconciliation poll as safety net) marks payment; the payable authorises for exactly the amount received, including a partial; the payables run disburses. Paid into AA and disbursed to the anaesthetist are separate states. The apps read the Billing Engine's mirror, never Xero directly. Ready

7a · Monthly AA fee invoicing

The office maintains AA fee settings (several fixed items and a charge per BCTI, all ex GST; a change applies from the next run). At month end, Run monthly fee invoices counts each anaesthetist's BCTIs (one per receivable invoice, once, against the anaesthetist who did the procedure, in the month it is paid in full) and raises one AA-FEE invoice to each active anaesthetist, with GST at the foot and a Xero ACCREC against their existing contact (no ACCPAY). The anaesthetist pays it into AA's own account; it is never deducted from a payment to them, and Admin and web Accounts → AA fees show it paid or unpaid. The fixed schedule, the paid-only count and BCTI granularity are with AA's accountant. Ready

8 · Failures, late additions and compliance

Pre-payment: an unpaid prepayment is a clear warning in both apps, never a block (AA's decision, OQ-57). Warnings: one routine; a small triangle on the Booking, the warning at the top of the Booking on opening, the Day view outline, and the office's To-do card with Clear; no confirm step at submit. Catch-up Phase 21 adds two mild rules: a child as the payer, and an insurer named with no approval number (never ACC or Health NZ). Post-op: a linked addendum Booking with its own cycle; the original stays immutable. Billing failure: the failed Booking is isolated, others invoice, then resolve-and-retry. Integration failure: a malformed message retries then dead-letters for manual intervention; a duplicate is an idempotent no-op; a message for a SUBMITTED/AUTHORISED target is parked. Compliance: everything is audited, both NHI formats validate, and no NHI or other personal information enters Xero, patient names included (confirmed with AA). Ready

Cheat sheet

The rules most likely to be questioned, and the phrasing that keeps the demo faithful to the RFP.

Fee calculation

Fee = (Base + Time + Modifier units) × anaesthetist's own dollar value per unit
  • The anaesthetist Booking shows no units or fee, only Mark complete; Admin Bookings keep the full billing view.
  • Reset → Confirm reset in the shared top bar restores the pristine data and clock from any app while keeping the current app.
  • Closing things: Escape closes only the top layer (a sheet over a drawer closes first, then the drawer) and returns focus to what opened it. Every web and Admin dialog has a corner X (Close) at its top right. A phone sheet has no X: its handle or a tap on the scrim closes it. The Demo actions pill, live clock, Reset popup and app switcher also close on Escape or an outside click.
  • Time: 1 unit per 15 min for the first two hours, then 1 unit per 10 min.
  • Each anaesthetist has an independent value per unit.
  • The anaesthetist picks the RVG code by typing; it is optional, and the system does not infer it.
  • A ranged group starts at its starting figure; any value is accepted, and one outside the guide raises a warning for the office after the procedure, never a block.
  • Base units sit on the RVG group alone; a Contract states none and may only name a default RVG code. Choosing a Contract fills an empty RVG code from its default; a Procedure that has one keeps it.
  • P1 (non-supine positioning) is included, pre-applied at 0 (removable), on every Neurosurgery and Spine code, so it cannot be added twice; the age modifier is pre-applied from the date of birth, removable.
  • Every other modifier, ASA included, is claimed with a short reason; modifiers stack with no limit, and the same modifier is never added twice.
  • A part interval always rounds up (confirmed with AA); the tiers are held as data (the RVG time rule under Master data, RVG groups, read only). The anaesthetist enters start and finish only.
  • The form follows the Contract's pricing kind: RVG code, start and finish and the modifiers step (None apply is an answer) on an RVG price; none of it on a fixed or open price, the code only for reference. Mark complete checks a Contract, the RVG detail on an RVG price, and a price (not Needs a price); never the payer's contact, the approval number or out-of-range base units.
  • Review flags never block: Changed by Dr X: was the old Contract (Approve or Correct), Price typed: $X (computed $Y), Contract not offered on this List, Needs a price, Needs an invoice destination, Not marked completed, Email or postal address needed, manual B/T/M notes. Approve all N Contract changes; authorising approves any still waiting. No move resets a Contract or typed price.

One Contract per Procedure, on the step rail

  1. Where does the invoice go? Patient, the List's hospital, or Special
  2. Then Payment type (Standard, Prepaid), the hospital's funding tag, or the holder
  3. The Contract, typed to search, under No contract (No contract · RVG, No contract · Fixed price) first

The office sets it at setup; the anaesthetist sees the same rail until the List is authorised, never onto or off prepaid.

Patient insurance distinction

nib is billed only through nib's own Contract. A patient with insurance who pays AA is on No contract · RVG (or a price list) and claims back themselves (the AIA patient). Not the same workflow. The Booking names its insurer or funder (a Party) and holds the approval number, editable by the office and on the day by the anaesthetist, who sees the number; a missing number is the only insurance warning, for an insurer only (NIB, Southern Cross Health Society), shown to the office and the anaesthetist. The funder never decides who is billed or narrows the Contracts; it marks one path Suggested.

The payer on the Booking

Each Contract decides who is billed: its holder, or the payer on the Booking. The payer is the patient, from the patient record, unless the Different payer tick names someone else (a parent, guardian, benefactor or family trust); the office and the anaesthetist change it until authorised. A billed payer needs an email or an NZ Post postal address (post alone is enough); Review flags one with neither, never a block.

Procedure text and the invoice description

The latest plain procedure text received, exactly as sent: the hospital's replaces the surgeon's rooms' (no switching), the original in the Booking's history; hand entry kept as typed; nobody edits it. Each Booking shows it in a small Procedure card; the Contract is in the Billing card and the RVG code in its card below. The invoice description shows on the invoice only: the procedure text, or a filled-in Contract invoice description on top; never the RVG code; the office's wording always wins. No attachments. Split is in the Billing card's Paid by line (on the phone, its fourth row) on each Procedure of a Booking, for the office or the anaesthetist. Invoices

Issued in the anaesthetist's name as supplier with their GST number (blank prints "GST number not held"; the number is on the Admin anaesthetist record), with AA as agent (AA GST number 102-345-678; the wording is Provisional (OQ-29)). Layout follows the Contract: the holder's when it bills its holder (invoice descriptions, the BTM breakdown, a cross-match strip, the holder reference code as Your reference), otherwise the patient's; Master data, Contracts shows Layout and Delivery read-only. Prices are GST exclusive; GST at the foot. Delivery is the Party's (email or post): the run emails each invoice or marks it To post (Provisional (OQ-122)); no address raises No invoice address, fixed by the office. The rail's Delivery card has the stamp, Resend and Print; there is no Email invoice button. One buyer-created tax invoice per invoice on each ACCPAY.

Contracts

Every Contract is one priced item held by a Party (a hospital, insurer, funder, surgeon or surgeon group, an anaesthetist, or AA). It bills its holder or the payer on the Booking (a prepaid Contract always the payer); it has a pricing kind, an optional override on RVG kinds, prepaid or not, and is Active or Disabled. It is edited in place with an audit, never deleted; a change from a date is a new Contract, except a fixed price, which changes through price ranges. Each priced item is a Contract of its own, with a holder reference code, an invoice description and a default RVG code; the range in force on the procedure date gives a fixed price, and a new range, even backdated, re-prices everything not yet authorised, prepaid or priced by hand, while invoices already raised never change. A combination such as Merivale's Facelift plus 1 add on is its own Contract; fee extras are events. An anaesthetist's price list is one prepaid Contract per item, kept by the office, every price above zero (Dr Souter's). No Contract prices a second procedure. No contract · RVG, held by AA, is always there; every Contract has AA's own code. A Contract carries none or more specialties (none means every specialty) and at most one funding tag (SXAP, HNZ, ACC); with who it bills, the tag decides where it is offered (Patient, the hospital and tag, or Special), shown live in the editor. The system keeps one Fixed price Contract, marked System, for every place an invoice can go; tagging a hospital's first Contract with a new tag makes its one at once, and only its name, AA code and invoice description change. Tip: find a Contract by typing its name, AA code, holder reference code, a word of its invoice description or its default RVG code ("CAT", "H3"); filter by holder or funding tag.

The money model

Payer → ACCREC → AA account → ACCPAY (full amount) → Anaesthetist
  • ACCREC is money AA is collecting; ACCPAY is the full amount AA owes the anaesthetist, equal to the ACCREC. AA's own fee is never deducted from it (it is invoiced to the anaesthetist separately).
  • Paid into AA and disbursed to the anaesthetist are separate states.
  • A partial payment releases the payable for exactly the amount received; the rest stays outstanding.
  • Both Xero records carry the InvoiceNumber (the ACCPAY's ends -P) and the case Reference.
  • AA's monthly fee: Month end: fee settings + BCTIs paid that month → AA-FEE invoice (ACCREC) → Anaesthetist pays AA's own account; never netted. Sample: $350.00 + $150.00 fixed, $5.00 per BCTI; "Seed a month of BCTIs" then the run gives Dr Rutherford $700.00 before GST ($805.00 with GST).
  • Caveat: the fixed schedule, the paid-only count and BCTI granularity are with AA's accountant; amounts are held ex GST, with GST at the foot of the fee invoice.
  • The apps read the Billing Engine's mirror, never Xero directly.
  • Outstanding balances are flat individual payable rows, not a Booking-level rollup and not amounts the anaesthetist personally owes.

Surgeons, pairings and tiers

  • Surgeon profiles: the HPI CPN is each surgeon's unique index (not the patient's NHI), each surgeon holds one or more specialties from AA's list (required), sits in one surgeons' room with its contact email and phone, and surgeons can belong to groups.
  • Pairing preferences and priority tiers: kept on both the surgeon profile and the anaesthetist record, admin only; a not-preferred pairing warns and never blocks; cover suggestions run in tier order, shuffled within a tier; seeded: Ms A. Reid prefers not to work with Dr Sharma, and Mr J. Whitford prefers to work with Dr Sharma.
  • Sessions and Lists: every active anaesthetist has an AM and a PM session a day from their start date over a configurable horizon (four months); the status is on the session, Free by default, and a List put into it shows in its place; default session times are one setting, sessions can have their own.
  • Find available (Day header): office only; the free anaesthetists for a session in tier order, shuffled within a tier, with not-preferred pairings apart once a surgeon is chosen.
  • Availability statuses (Master data): rename, recolour or add a status and every app follows; rules read the status's ID; it is availability only, never a List's state (Active, Submitted or Authorised, or Draft for a Draft List).
  • Availability and cover on mobile and web: mark days off ahead, repeating days off, change or delete one occurrence (My calendar on the phone, My availability on the web, the same form).
  • Move to a colleague on any of her Bookings: a colleague free that session takes it as a new List of theirs; no warning, and telling the office is optional (catch-up Phase 32a).
  • Out-of-hours sessions: Open out-of-hours session on an anaesthetist's Day row (hover the name or Tab to it) opens an evening session after PM for any day from today, one a day, 18:00 to 21:00 by default; it works like any session: Assign List, a Draft List for the evening, availability and the finders; an empty one closes from its drawer (US-01.1.6).
  • The app never says "slot": it says session, AM, PM or Out-of-hours.

RFP ambiguities and discovery decisions

Where the RFP is open or internally inconsistent, the prototype picks a reading and labels it. Present these as design proposals to confirm with AA, not settled facts.

1 · NHI in Xero (settled)

One section allowed NHI as a searchable Xero cross-reference; Appendix 2 said NHI never leaves the practice system. Confirmed with AA: no NHI or other personal information in Xero, patient names included.

2 · List disappearance trigger (answered)

A List leaves the anaesthetist's main view when the office authorises it (OQ-31), not at submit, not when billed, not on payment. Its invoices show in their balances at once; older work is found by search on patient name, NHI or date, with no calendar archive.

3 · Billing failure isolation

The RFP asks whether one failed Booking blocks the whole List. The prototype isolates the failed Booking and bills the others.

4 · Monitoring location

Admin Web vs a separate Billing Engine screen is left open. The prototype places monitoring in Admin Web.

5 · List reassignment mechanics

Settled by US-01.4.1: the List moves into the colleague's free session with its id, Bookings and history, and nothing about billing is recalculated. Answered (D60, OQ-84): whoever moves a List, the anaesthetist or the office, chooses what the vacated session becomes, Free or Unavailable, on every path; Free is chosen first, and when the move comes from marking the session unavailable, that status is chosen first. Answered (D7, OQ-39): an anaesthetist moves her own List at once, back to the office (a Draft List) or into a colleague's free session; nobody accepts and the office does not confirm. Answered (D15, OQ-65): every such move posts to one shared notification pool in the Admin App, separate from the to-do list. Answered (D58, OQ-79): notices are active or resolved; an admin ticks one (the same tick as a to-do's Clear) and it is hidden for the whole office, Show resolved brings it back with who and when, and nothing expires; an anaesthetist's Alert the office joins the pool (whether an alerted Booking may still be submitted is open; the prototype allows it). Answered (OQ-102's 19 October note, D54): the office's pickers order free colleagues by tier, then preferred pairing; the anaesthetist apps stay by availability alone. Answered (D44, OQ-43): no warning on the anaesthetist's own move; she sees colleagues by availability alone. Answered (D61, OQ-85; D54, OQ-113): she can also move a single Booking, to a colleague free in that session, found by typing a name or from the free list in name order; it always becomes a new List in their session, never added to an existing List (the rule for now), and telling the office is her option. Answered (D38, OQ-80): the payable follows the Booking; a prepaid Booking's payable stays the AA Holding account, the new anaesthetist claims the prepaid amount, and nothing is recalculated or repointed. Whoever submits a List did every procedure on it.

6 · Hospital-closure conflicts (unavailability is never one)

Answered (OQ-09): a soft warning. Nothing blocks the office; the List is accepted and flagged. A conflict is a List at a closed hospital. An anaesthetist's unavailability, short-notice sickness included, is never a conflict (D59, OQ-81 part 3 answered): whoever marks a booked session unavailable, the office included, returns the List to the office or hands it to a colleague there and then. The List changes colour (amber, with its kind's bar and a reason line) and shows on the Conflicts screen across every date. It clears when the clash goes (the closure moved or deleted, or the List's hospital changed) or when the office clears it, with an optional note and "Flag again". The office never assigns or moves a List onto an unavailable session: it is made available first, with the anaesthetist's agreement (US-01.6.3). Not conflicts: a recurring booking landing on an unavailable session becomes a Draft List (Phase 31), and anyone who marks a booked session unavailable is asked first whether the List returns to the office or goes to a colleague (US-01.5.5).

7 · Split-billing invoice count

Grouping text says same-counterparty Procedures share an invoice; split-billing text says two invoices. The prototype makes one invoice per billable party per Booking; a Split on the Booking gives one invoice per share.

8 · Pre-payment

Payment before the procedure is required. AA decided not to block (OQ-57): an unpaid prepayment is a strong before-procedure warning in both apps. The escalation by date and the automatic invoice come later.

Warnings: one routine, a To-do card in Admin, a triangle on Bookings, the warning at the top of the Booking. Never a block, and no confirm step.

9 · Concurrency

Several sources can change a Booking. The prototype is single-user with audited last-write-wins; production concurrency is a discovery topic, shown via the audit trail and live integration updates.

10 · Modifier values

The modifier table is the NZSA RVG 2021 table as printed (30 codes, Admin → Master data → Modifiers); values that are not plain numbers ("2 + Time", "4-6") are entered at capture. Only the age modifier and a modifier the base units include are pre-applied, both removable; nothing else is pre-filled. The age bands are still to confirm with AA (OQ-95). Modifiers stack with no limit; AA's own (Vanessa's list) are not loaded yet, and the office can add one.

11 · Open vs genuinely overdue balances

The billing monitor's "Prior balance" intake flag reads any open (unpaid) prior episode. The RFP asks whether to distinguish open from genuinely overdue; invoices carry a raised date but no due date, so the prototype flags any open balance and holds the distinction as a discovery question.

12 · ACC pre-op flat-fee codes

ACC pre-operative assessment has its own flat-fee code set (CS250, CS260, CS70), separate from the general BTM structure. The prototype captures these as ancillary fixed-amount lines with the code in the description; the code set and rates are a confirmation item with AA billing. ACC itself is not a billing route or a kind of Contract: ACC work bills through a holder's Contract carrying the ACC funding tag, priced like any other.

13 · Specialties and funding tags

The starting specialties are the catalogue's nine plus Neurosurgery, Vascular surgery and Oral and maxillofacial surgery; subspecialties fold in (Colorectal, Breast, Upper GI and HPB under General surgery, Fertility under Gynaecology) until AA's spreadsheet replaces the list. A surgeon may hold more than one specialty (Ben said it is "not a thing"; for AA to confirm), and a Contract is offered when it shares any one. Each funding tag may name its funder. A specialty or tag in use cannot be removed.

Likely evaluator questions

Who actually uses mobile versus web?

The same anaesthetist. Mobile is the in-theatre/on-the-move interface; web is the wider planning and accounts view.

Who creates the shifts?

The system creates AM and PM sessions on the rolling canvas. Recurring bookings supply recurring defaults, painted after the anaesthetist's own calendar. "Apply to canvas now" repopulates the schedule ahead after a change, without touching booked Lists. Office staff reserve and amend Lists and handle exceptions. "Shift" is not the RFP term.

What about cases after six?

The office opens an out-of-hours session after PM for the anaesthetist, even the same evening, and puts a List in it: a new one, or a request already waiting as a Draft List for that evening. It is a session like AM and PM, one a day, 18:00 to 21:00 by default: the anaesthetist can mark it available or not, the finders offer it while it is free, and its Bookings are captured, submitted and billed like any List (S2 Beat 3e).

Can an anaesthetist edit after submission?

Yes, until the office authorises the List. A submitted List stays on the anaesthetist's main view, less prominent, and they can still change it; every change is audited and Review shows "Changed since submitted". The last change stands, and a Booking reopened after submit must be completed again before the office can authorise. Authorised Bookings are immutable.

What happens when the office finds a mistake?

Office staff phone for clarification and make the correction. There is no Returned state; the anaesthetist can also correct it themselves until the office authorises it.

Why is Xero not doing all the billing?

AA has agency-specific billing rules and needs Procedure-level calculation, grouping and anaesthetist-facing views. Xero is the AR and banking add-on.

How do late hospital changes work?

While the List is ACTIVE, mapped hospital messages update the Booking in near real time. Once SUBMITTED or AUTHORISED, an inbound change parks for manual intervention.

What if the patient has no NHI?

The RFP is internally inconsistent (unique identifier vs "where available"). The prototype uses a hidden internal ID as the invariant and allows a provisional NHI-pending patient.

Is the demo using real patient or integration data?

No. All names and data are fictional; hospital, PDF/OCR, NHI, email, Xero and payment events are simulated in-browser.

One-line close. The prototype demonstrates a single audited flow from a changing theatre schedule to captured clinical billing inputs, controlled office authorisation, reliable invoicing, and transparent collection and disbursement.
