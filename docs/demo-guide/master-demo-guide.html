The prototype in one page

A fully interactive React front end over a mock in-browser backend that demonstrates every major feature of the RFP's candidate architecture. It is a demo prototype, not production software: all data is fictional and every external system is simulated.

The 30-second explanation. Anaesthesia Associates coordinates bookings and billing for about 85 independent anaesthetists. The system maintains a rolling AM/PM schedule, keeps patient Bookings current, lets anaesthetists capture BTM billing data, lets the office check and authorise completed Lists, then calculates and issues invoices. Xero handles receivables and banking, while the Billing Engine stays the system that translates financial events back into each anaesthetist's view.

The mental model

Schedule
  → Day
    → Anaesthetist
      → AM List and PM List
        → Booking (one patient appointment)
          → Procedure(s)
            → Billing line(s)
  • Every active anaesthetist has two sessions a day, AM and PM, from their start date, over a rolling horizon (a setting, four months today). Every session starts Free.
  • The anaesthetist's calendar is the session's status; the office puts a List (hospital, surgeon, Bookings) into a session, or a recurring booking does, and the List then shows in its place. The app says session, never "slot".
  • A List is not an employment shift, and a Booking is not a shift application.
  • The anaesthetist completes Bookings and submits the whole List; the office checks and authorises it; authorisation locks the Bookings and hands the List to the Billing Engine.

The five nouns

TermMeaning
ScheduleThe rolling four-month calendar
ListOne anaesthetist's half-day AM or PM operating session; every List has exactly one hospital and one surgeon (AA rooms is a location)
Draft ListA List with no anaesthetist yet, state DRAFT; the office assigns it
BookingOne patient appointment inside a List
ProcedureOne clinical procedure inside a Booking; each resolves its own billing route
BTMBase units + Time units + Modifier units

The lifecycle

ACTIVE → SUBMITTED → AUTHORISED → Billing run completed
  • ACTIVE: anaesthetist, office and integrations may update.
  • SUBMITTED: office-only edits; the anaesthetist sees it completed and unbilled.
  • AUTHORISED: Bookings are locked; the whole List hands to billing.
  • Billing-run completion stamps billedAt and removes the List from the anaesthetist's work view. There is no RETURNED state.
  • A List's state is DRAFT (a Draft List, no anaesthetist yet), ACTIVE, SUBMITTED or AUTHORISED; a session's availability is separate.

Three user-facing apps, two personas. Mobile and web are two contexts for the same anaesthetist role, not two jobs. The Admin app covers several office responsibilities under one persona.

Dr Melanie Souter · anaesthetist (mobile-first)

Primary persona 2

Dr Souter at a desktop · anaesthetist web

A usage context, not a separate role. Wider view of upcoming work, availability and finances, with the same List, Booking and BTM actions.

Primary persona 3

Kirsty W. · scheduling coordinator

Office. Needs the whole day across the roster. Coordinates standing arrangements, ad-hoc bookings, hospital changes, cancellations and short-notice cover.

The one-day dashboard is the control tower: move one Booking for a routine reschedule, reassign a whole List for illness cover, preserving Bookings and audit through every change. Maintains schedule-related master data such as anaesthetists, recurring bookings and hospital holidays (adding, editing and deleting closures), and surgeons, their rooms and groups, the office's private pairing preferences and the anaesthetists' priority tiers.

Primary persona 4

Kirsty W. · billing review and authorisation

May be the same staff member. Reviews all Bookings of a submitted List as a set, corrects office-owned data without returning the List, authorises a clean List, monitors later billing, Xero and integration exceptions, and maintains AA fee settings and runs the monthly fee invoices (AA's own fee to each anaesthetist, never deducted from a payment to them).

Critical rule: there is no RETURNED state. The office phones for clarification and corrects it; the anaesthetist does not reopen the Booking.

The workflows

0 · Create and maintain the schedule canvas

The system ensures every active anaesthetist has two Lists a day across the rolling horizon; the anaesthetist's calendar (days off and repeating days off) sets each session's status directly, nothing reconciled; recurring bookings then project their Lists, empty, after the calendar (a closed session is not painted; Phase 31 makes it a Draft List), and a change reapplies at once through "Apply to canvas now"; hospital holidays flag Lists; conflicts surface without deleting the List and clear when the clash goes or the office clears them. Ready

In Admin, the office also maintains surgeons (each keyed on their HPI CPN), surgeons' rooms, surgeon groups, hospital contact emails, and its private pairing preferences and anaesthetist priority tiers. Anaesthetists never see preferences or tiers, and an anaesthetist handing on their own List gets no warning.

1 · Reserve a List and populate patient Bookings

Triggers: a recurring booking, a surgeon's call, a hospital message, an emailed PDF, or a missing Booking found on the day. Manual fallbacks are first-class: phone advice (office), mobile/web manual Booking, and PDF review-and-ingest (deduped by NHI). A request with no anaesthetist yet becomes a Draft List, which can take Bookings before it is assigned. Every change writes lastModifiedBy/At and append-only audit. Ready

2 · Plan the day and manage late changes

The office reads the status-coloured grid, moves one Booking for a routine reschedule, edits hospital, surgeon or actual times, soft-cancels with a reason (Bookings stay visible, excluded from billing), and adds internal day notes. The Draft Lists rail card and band show unassigned Lists soonest first, assigned from a grouped, tier-ordered picker, and every List block shows its booking count. A recurring booking that lands on a day the anaesthetist has already blocked out becomes a Draft List for the office. Ready

3 · Find cover and reassign a whole List

The anaesthetist offers a Free session or requests cover; the office views free anaesthetists (Find available, or the Reassign list picker: in priority-tier order, with any not-preferred pairing grouped apart and warned, never blocked) and reassigns the whole List: it moves into the colleague's free session with its Bookings and history, keeping its id, and the vacated session returns to Free unless the office marks it Unavailable or Holiday (US-01.4.1; the default is OQ-84, still open). Nothing about billing is recalculated. Ready

4 · Capture BTM and complete a Booking

Per Procedure: select the RVG base code, record start and handover, and the system computes tiered Time units (1/15 min for the first two hours, then 1/10 min), plus ASA and modifiers, at the anaesthetist's own value per unit. A positioning modifier is blocked if the base code already absorbs it; an additional Procedure is time-only. When every active Booking is complete, the List is submittable. Ready

5 · Submit the whole List and office review

The anaesthetist marks the List complete and submits; it becomes SUBMITTED and read-only to her. The office reviews the full set (route, Contract, Insurer, reference, BTM, advisory flags), corrects office-owned data, optionally logs a phone note, and authorises. One list-authorised event hands the whole List to the Billing Engine. Review is a human sanity check, not an automatic gate. Ready

6 · Calculate and issue invoices

The Billing Engine resolves the explicit route per Procedure (hospital/contract holder, billable party, direct-claim insurer), selects the governing Contract and rating method, calculates from the captured snapshot, enforces time-only additional Procedures and conserved two-funder allocations, groups by counterparty, and produces contract-holder or patient invoice layouts. Billing-run completion stamps billedAt and removes the List from the anaesthetist's views. Ready

7 · Collect money and disburse to the anaesthetist

The Billing Engine creates a Xero pair: ACCREC (what the payer owes AA) and a DRAFT ACCPAY (the full amount AA owes the anaesthetist, equal to the ACCREC; AA's own fee is invoiced separately, never deducted). Both carry the InvoiceNumber and Reference. A webhook (with a daily reconciliation poll as safety net) marks payment; the payable authorises for exactly the amount received, including a partial; the payables run disburses. Paid into AA and disbursed to the anaesthetist are separate states. The apps read the Billing Engine's mirror, never Xero directly. Ready

7a · Monthly AA fee invoicing

The office maintains AA fee settings (several fixed items and a charge per BCTI, all ex GST; a change applies from the next run). At month end, Run monthly fee invoices counts each anaesthetist's BCTIs (one per receivable invoice, once, against the anaesthetist who did the procedure, in the month it is paid in full) and raises one AA-FEE invoice to each active anaesthetist, with GST at the foot and a Xero ACCREC against their existing contact (no ACCPAY). The anaesthetist pays it into AA's own account; it is never deducted from a payment to them, and Admin and web Accounts → AA fees show it paid or unpaid. The fixed schedule, the paid-only count and BCTI granularity are with AA's accountant. Ready

8 · Failures, late additions and compliance

Pre-payment: an unpaid prepayment is a clear warning in both apps, never a block (AA's decision, OQ-57). Warnings: one routine; a small triangle on the Booking, the warning at the top of the Booking on opening, the Day view outline, and the office's To-do card with Clear; no confirm step at submit. Post-op: a linked addendum Booking with its own cycle; the original stays immutable. Billing failure: the failed Booking is isolated, others invoice, then resolve-and-retry. Integration failure: a malformed message retries then dead-letters for manual intervention; a duplicate is an idempotent no-op; a message for a SUBMITTED/AUTHORISED target is parked. Compliance: everything is audited, both NHI formats validate, and no NHI or other personal information enters Xero, patient names included (confirmed with AA). Ready

Cheat sheet

The rules most likely to be questioned, and the phrasing that keeps the demo faithful to the RFP.

Fee calculation

Fee = (Base + Time + Modifier units) × anaesthetist's own dollar value per unit
  • The anaesthetist Booking shows no units or fee, only Mark complete; Admin Bookings keep the full billing view.
  • Reset → Confirm reset in the shared top bar restores the pristine data and clock from any app while keeping the current app.
  • Time: 1 unit per 15 min for the first two hours, then 1 unit per 10 min.
  • Each anaesthetist has an independent value per unit.
  • The anaesthetist selects the RVG code; the system does not infer it. Some codes are ranges.
  • Positioning cannot be charged again if the base code already includes it.
  • Modifier codes in the same band (pre-assessment, age, ASA, BMI) are one per procedure, so each band is a segmented control: picking a sibling replaces it, and picking the live segment again clears the band. The codes that genuinely stack (pre-assessment follow-up, emergency, positioning, awake intubation, post-op care) stay as chips under "Also applies".
  • Partial-interval rounding is a prototype assumption (round up per started interval).

Three billing routes

  1. Hospital or other contract holder
  2. Billable Party, usually the patient
  3. Direct-claim Insurer

Explicit per Procedure, not inferred from flags.

Patient insurance distinction

Direct Insurer route: AA invoices the one insurer that accepts direct claims. Insured reimbursement: AA invoices the patient, who claims from their insurer. Not the same workflow.

Split billing

An additional Procedure in the same episode (Add another procedure) is time-only (Brian Holt on Dr Souter's Mon 20 AM List, the S3 split Booking, shows it); Base and Modifiers are not charged again. One Procedure may allocate conserved lines across two funders. Group by counterparty; different funders create separate invoices.

Contracts

Type 1 reference only (standard rate); Type 2 agreed rate/discount; Type 3 fixed price. Every hospital and direct-billing insurer has a protected default Type 1. Surgeon, group and organisation holders do not have that guaranteed fallback in the prototype.

The money model

Payer → ACCREC → AA account → ACCPAY (full amount) → Anaesthetist
  • ACCREC is money AA is collecting; ACCPAY is the full amount AA owes the anaesthetist, equal to the ACCREC. AA's own fee is never deducted from it (it is invoiced to the anaesthetist separately).
  • Paid into AA and disbursed to the anaesthetist are separate states.
  • A partial payment releases the payable for exactly the amount received; the rest stays outstanding.
  • Both Xero records carry the InvoiceNumber (the ACCPAY's ends -P) and the case Reference.
  • AA's monthly fee: Month end: fee settings + BCTIs paid that month → AA-FEE invoice (ACCREC) → Anaesthetist pays AA's own account; never netted. Sample: $350.00 + $150.00 fixed, $5.00 per BCTI; "Seed a month of BCTIs" then the run gives Dr Rutherford $700.00 before GST ($805.00 with GST).
  • Caveat: the fixed schedule, the paid-only count and BCTI granularity are with AA's accountant; amounts are held ex GST, with GST at the foot of the fee invoice.
  • The apps read the Billing Engine's mirror, never Xero directly.
  • Outstanding balances are flat individual payable rows, not a Booking-level rollup and not amounts the anaesthetist personally owes.

Surgeons, pairings and tiers

  • Surgeon profiles: the HPI CPN is each surgeon's unique index (not the patient's NHI), each surgeon sits in one surgeons' room with its contact email and phone, and surgeons can belong to groups.
  • Pairing preferences and priority tiers: kept on both the surgeon profile and the anaesthetist record, admin only; a not-preferred pairing warns and never blocks; cover suggestions run in tier order, shuffled within a tier; seeded: Ms A. Reid prefers not to work with Dr Sharma, and Mr J. Whitford prefers to work with Dr Sharma.
  • Sessions and Lists: every active anaesthetist has an AM and a PM session a day from their start date over a configurable horizon (four months); the status is on the session, Free by default, and a List put into it shows in its place; default session times are one setting, sessions can have their own.
  • Find available (Day header): office only; the free anaesthetists for a session in tier order, shuffled within a tier, with not-preferred pairings apart once a surgeon is chosen.
  • Availability statuses (Master data): rename, recolour or add a status and every app follows; rules read the status's ID; it is availability only, never a List's state (Active, Submitted or Authorised, or Draft for a Draft List).
  • Availability and cover on mobile and web: mark days off ahead, repeating days off, change or delete one occurrence (My calendar on the phone, My availability on the web, the same form).
  • The app never says "slot": it says session, AM or PM.

RFP ambiguities and discovery decisions

Where the RFP is open or internally inconsistent, the prototype picks a reading and labels it. Present these as design proposals to confirm with AA, not settled facts.

1 · NHI in Xero (settled)

One section allowed NHI as a searchable Xero cross-reference; Appendix 2 said NHI never leaves the practice system. Confirmed with AA: no NHI or other personal information in Xero, patient names included.

2 · List disappearance trigger

A table implies disappearance at AUTHORISED; the text says invoice generation is the precise trigger and needs confirming. The prototype uses billing-run completion.

3 · Billing failure isolation

The RFP asks whether one failed Booking blocks the whole List. The prototype isolates the failed Booking and bills the others.

4 · Monitoring location

Admin Web vs a separate Billing Engine screen is left open. The prototype places monitoring in Admin Web.

5 · List reassignment mechanics

Settled by US-01.4.1: the List moves into the colleague's free session with its id, Bookings and history, and nothing about billing is recalculated. Open (OQ-84): the vacated session's default; the prototype uses Free, with Unavailable or Holiday on request. From a session already marked unavailable (a recorded sickness) it keeps that status by default ("Keep as is").

6 · Availability and holiday conflicts

Answered (OQ-09): a soft warning. Nothing blocks the office; the List is accepted and flagged. A conflict is a List at a closed hospital, or a List (and its Bookings) in a session already marked unavailable, including a sickness the office records when an anaesthetist phones in (provisional: short-notice sickness is still open, OQ-81 part 3). The List changes colour (amber, with its kind's bar and a reason line) and shows on the Conflicts screen across every date. It clears when the clash goes (the closure moved or deleted, the session available again, the List reassigned to an available anaesthetist) or when the office clears it, with an optional note and "Flag again". Not conflicts: a recurring booking landing on an unavailable session becomes a Draft List (Phase 31), and an anaesthetist who marks a booked session unavailable returns the List or hands it on (US-01.5.5, Phase 32). Until 32 that path still flags the List, so do not demo it.

7 · Split-billing invoice count

Grouping text says same-counterparty Procedures share an invoice; split-billing text says two invoices. The prototype groups by counterparty, two invoices when funders differ.

8 · Pre-payment

Payment before the procedure is required. AA decided not to block (OQ-57): an unpaid prepayment is a strong before-procedure warning in both apps. The escalation by date and the automatic invoice come later.

Warnings: one routine, a To-do card in Admin, a triangle on Bookings, the warning at the top of the Booking. Never a block, and no confirm step.

9 · Concurrency

Several sources can change a Booking. The prototype is single-user with audited last-write-wins; production concurrency is a discovery topic, shown via the audit trail and live integration updates.

10 · Modifier values and time rounding

The RFP names example ranges, not a certified NZSA table. The prototype values are demo-plausible; partial-interval rounding is an assumption. Neither is authoritative. Codes in the same band (pre-assessment, age, ASA, BMI) are one per procedure, so tapping a sibling replaces it: those per-group ranges only add up if one code applies. The RFP implies that rather than stating it, and a pre-assessment follow-up (PA5) stacks on top, which is a question for AA.

11 · Open vs genuinely overdue balances

The billing monitor's "Prior balance" intake flag reads any open (unpaid) prior episode. The RFP asks whether to distinguish open from genuinely overdue; invoices carry a raised date but no due date, so the prototype flags any open balance and holds the distinction as a discovery question.

12 · ACC pre-op flat-fee codes

ACC pre-operative assessment has its own flat-fee code set (CS250, CS260, CS70), separate from the general BTM structure. The prototype captures these as ancillary fixed-amount lines with the code in the description; the code set and rates are a confirmation item with AA billing.

Likely evaluator questions

Who actually uses mobile versus web?

The same anaesthetist. Mobile is the in-theatre/on-the-move interface; web is the wider planning and accounts view.

Who creates the shifts?

The system creates AM and PM sessions on the rolling canvas. Recurring bookings supply recurring defaults, painted after the anaesthetist's own calendar. "Apply to canvas now" repopulates the schedule ahead after a change, without touching booked Lists. Office staff reserve and amend Lists and handle exceptions. "Shift" is not the RFP term.

Can an anaesthetist edit after submission?

No. Submitted Bookings are read-only to the anaesthetist and editable only by office; authorised Bookings are immutable.

What happens when the office finds a mistake?

Office staff phone for clarification and make the correction. There is no Returned state.

Why is Xero not doing all the billing?

AA has agency-specific billing rules and needs Procedure-level calculation, grouping and anaesthetist-facing views. Xero is the AR and banking add-on.

How do late hospital changes work?

While the List is ACTIVE, mapped hospital messages update the Booking in near real time. Once SUBMITTED or AUTHORISED, an inbound change parks for manual intervention.

What if the patient has no NHI?

The RFP is internally inconsistent (unique identifier vs "where available"). The prototype uses a hidden internal ID as the invariant and allows a provisional NHI-pending patient.

Is the demo using real patient or integration data?
