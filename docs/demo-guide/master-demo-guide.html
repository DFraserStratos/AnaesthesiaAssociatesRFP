The one-sentence product description

Anaesthesia Associates runs the schedule of its anaesthetists' operating Lists, keeps the patient Bookings on those Lists current, captures what billing needs, checks and authorises each completed List, then raises the invoices, keeps the ledger and pays the anaesthetists through Xero.

The mental model

Schedule (four months ahead)
  -> Day
    -> Anaesthetist
      -> Slot: AM, PM, and an out-of-hours one the office opens (on screen: a session)
        -> List
          -> Booking (one patient)
            -> Procedure (RVG code, Contract, Paid by)
              -> Event (pre-op, post-op, billing event, additional invoice, credit note)
  • Slot. A status container for one anaesthetist, one day, one part of the day. Its status comes from the anaesthetist's availability (Free, Unavailable, Public and the rest of the list the office keeps), and a List goes into it. Every anaesthetist has an AM and a PM one every day; the office can open an out-of-hours one after PM, one a day. On screen it is a session, and in the demo you say session, AM or PM.
  • List. One anaesthetist's work in one session at one hospital with one surgeon. Its state is ACTIVE, SUBMITTED or AUTHORISED; a Draft List, with no anaesthetist yet, is DRAFT until the office assigns it.
  • Booking. One patient on a List.
  • Procedure. What is booked inside a Booking. The first is the primary, which carries the base units and modifiers; every Procedure bills its own time. Each has its own Contract, RVG code and Paid by line (the payer on that Procedure).
  • RVG group and code. The RVG groups are the one master list of procedures and base units. The anaesthetist picks the RVG code by typing.
  • Contract and Party. Every pricing arrangement is a Contract held by a Party: a hospital, an insurer, a funder, a surgeon or surgeon group, an anaesthetist, or AA. One Contract per priced item, each with its pricing kind and, for a fixed price, price ranges from dates. The Contract decides who is invoiced: its holder, or the payer on the Procedure.
  • No contract · RVG. The backstop for a patient paying at the standard rate: RVG units at the anaesthetist's own rate, invoiced to the payer. Its partner, No contract · Fixed price, takes a typed amount.
  • Event. Work or money on a Procedure after the fact: a pre-op or post-op event, a billing event, an additional invoice or a credit note. It sits on the Procedure, on no List, goes through one review step and bills in the next run.

The flow over that model:

  • Availability paints first, then the surgeons' recurring bookings fill sessions with Lists. A recurring booking that meets an unavailable session becomes a Draft List.
  • The office imports and matches bookings, sets each Procedure's Contract, and assigns Draft Lists.
  • The anaesthetist completes each Booking and submits the whole List. She can still change it until the office authorises it.
  • The office checks and authorises the whole List. Authorise locks the pricing snapshot, and the Billing Engine raises and sends one invoice per payer from it.
  • Every invoice is a receivable and payable pair in AA's own ledger and in Xero. Anaesthetists are paid in a weekly payment run; AA invoices its own fee monthly.

Status colours

A session shows its availability status, from the list the office keeps in Master data · Availability statuses; a session with a List in it shows the List's kind. Every block carries its label, never colour alone. These are the statuses as seeded.

Sessions
Free Holiday Unavailable Public

Free is the default, drawn with a dashed outline, and open for a List or cover. Unavailable is hatched. Holiday reads "On holiday" in full and Public reads "Public hospital"; a Public session can carry a note, such as CPH.

Lists
Private Pre-op

A List's kind is fixed: Private, or Pre-op ("Pre-op Assessment" in full).

List states
DRAFT ACTIVE SUBMITTED AUTHORISED

DRAFT is only for a Draft List, which has no anaesthetist yet. On her own Lists the anaesthetist reads progress instead: To finish ✓Ready to submit ✓Done · unbilled Authorised Invoiced

Warnings
Mild Strong Cleared

The triangle on a Booking takes the colour of its strongest open warning: amber for mild, red for strong, grey once every warning is cleared. A number beside it counts the open warnings when there is more than one. It is never a status colour and never crimson.

Terminology to correct early

The field user is an anaesthetist, and the unit of work is a List in a session. There is no "SSA" user and no marketplace of open shifts. Anaesthetists keep their availability, see their Lists, and find their own cover by moving a List or a single Booking to a free colleague. Draft Lists are assigned by the office alone. Never show or describe an anaesthetist signing up for a shift.

Persona 1: Dr Melanie Souter, mobile-first anaesthetist

Context

Dr Souter works independently across several hospitals and surgeons. She moves between theatres during the day. Her phone is the fastest way to see her Lists, read each Booking and record what AA needs to bill.

Goals

  • Know which Lists she has in each AM, PM and out-of-hours session.
  • See the hospital, surgeon, patients and times on each List.
  • Deal with late changes without phoning AA for every detail.
  • Record base, time and modifier units with little typing, and see what the price comes to as she records.
  • Find her own cover: move a List, or a single Booking, to a colleague who is free.
  • Keep her calendar: days off, Public sessions, availability weeks ahead.
  • See what she is owed, what she has been paid, her remittances and her monthly GST report.

Core actions

  1. Sign in on the handset with her demo account (m.souter@aa-associates.example, password filled in). The screen carries a "Provisional" pill: how anaesthetists sign in is still to be decided (OQ-83). The framed prototype opens signed in.
  2. Open Lists, her main view from today. Lists from earlier that the office has not authorised sit above it under "Earlier, not yet authorised". An authorised List leaves the main view; search finds it by patient name or NHI, and a picked day shows that day's Lists.
  3. Open a List and read its Bookings in time order. A stub stands in for a row still waiting at intake with no NHI, so she sees the case coming.
  4. Open a Booking. The Procedure section shows the procedure text as it arrived, with when and where it came from. The Billing section below it shows where the invoice goes, the Contract, the RVG code and Paid by. The invoice description starts as the text received; where a Contract has its own invoice description, the invoice prints that.
  5. Record what the Procedure's Contract needs. On an RVG price: the RVG code, found by typing under its body-area heading; Start now and Finish now; and the Modifiers step. On a fixed price: no times or modifiers, and the RVG code is optional. The Price section shows the units, her rate per unit and the price as she goes, and the Booking total sits above Mark complete.
  6. Answer the Modifiers step, even when it is None apply. The age modifier and any modifier already counted in the base units come in applied, and she can remove them. Anything else she claims needs a short explanation, ASA included.
  7. Change the procedure or the Contract while the List is hers to edit: before she submits, and after it until the office authorises the List. She can change the RVG code, add a procedure with Add another procedure, use Make primary on another one, or open the step rail from the Billing section and pick another Contract. A Contract she changes reads "Awaiting office approval" and is flagged at review, where the office approves or corrects it.
  8. Name the payer on each Procedure: Paid by → Change opens a sheet for a parent, guardian, benefactor or family trust, offered to the Booking's other Procedures with the same payer. A payer needs an email or a postal address. Add insurer or funder records who funds the Booking and the insurer's approval number on the day; it never changes who is invoiced.
  9. Type her own price where the Contract allows it: on No contract · RVG (the PRICE $ field and Save price), or the amount on No contract · Fixed price. Her own Contracts, such as her prepaid rhinoplasty at $1,200.00, price at her own fixed price, which the office keeps on her behalf. Anywhere else the price reads "Set by ..." with "To use a different price, change the Contract or alert the office."
  10. Split a Procedure's fee on her own Booking from its Paid by line: each share in $ or %, one party taking the rest, each party invoiced separately, until the office authorises the List.
  11. Mark complete on each Booking, then Mark list completed and Submit to office. Mark complete asks for a Contract and a price, and on an RVG price also the RVG code, both times and the modifiers step. A warning never stops it.
  12. See warnings: a triangle on the List, and the warning at the top of the Booking when she opens it. A patient who owes AA reads "This patient owes AA money. The office is following up", with no amounts.
  13. Move her own List from List options → Move this List: Return to the office or Hand on to a colleague who is free that session. It happens at once, with no acceptance and no approval, and she chooses what her session becomes (Free or Unavailable). The office sees it in its notifications.
  14. Move a single Booking with Move to a colleague: a colleague free that session, found by name or from the free list. The Booking becomes a new List in their session and takes its Contracts with it; whoever does the case is paid for it. Tell the office is her choice. Whoever submits a List did every procedure on it, so she moves a Booking before she submits.
  15. Alert the office from a List or a Booking with a note. It lands in the office's notifications until someone there resolves it.
  16. Keep her calendar on Availability → My calendar: days off weeks ahead, repeating ones included. Marking a booked session Unavailable asks where its List goes: back to the office or on to a colleague.
  17. See an out-of-hours List the office added for an emergency or late case, in an evening session after PM. She captures and submits it like any List, and marks that session available or not like AM and PM. She rings the office to have one opened.
  18. Add a missing Booking with Add a booking, typing the procedure as she was told it (kept exactly as typed). The NHI is checked as she types, and Look up fills the patient from the NHI register, looked up on her behalf under her HPI CPN, only to identify the patient.
  19. Record her own pre-op and post-op care on any of her Procedures, before or days after, from the Booking (main view, search or a past day): Add pre-op or post-op event (a ward review, HDU review, pain consult, the ACC pre-op assessment with its CS250, CS260 or CS70 code, codes still to confirm (OQ-12), or other work, with its own date and a time or a fixed fee) and Add billing event (a free-text charge, or a credit before the Procedure is invoiced). The office reviews each one and it bills in the next run.
  20. On her own invoiced Procedure, Raise additional invoice for a late charge such as a ward review; she can edit or remove it until the office reviews it. Credit note beside it credits the whole invoice (less work than expected, the wrong payer, paid twice), with a new lower invoice if one is due. The office reviews it, and her payment for that invoice is reversed by a negative invoice that nets in a weekly payment run.
  21. On her own prepaid procedure, once the List is authorised, use the Prepaid settlement block: nothing further is raised automatically, so any difference is an additional invoice or a credit note she raises by hand. She can cancel a prepaid Booking on her List; the system credits the prepayment invoice, and refunds it if paid, at once.
  22. Open her Profile on More: rate per unit, phone, email, registration number, HPI CPN and bank account (masked, such as 99-0101-•••••21-00). Change beside Rate per unit sets the dollar value of one RVG unit. The new rate applies to Lists not yet authorised; an authorised List keeps the rate locked at authorise. The other rows show a lock: the office holds them.
  23. Get help from More → Get help: the help line, open 8:30am to 5:00pm on weekdays.

Permissions

  • ACTIVE: edits her Bookings and submits the List once every uncancelled Booking is complete.
  • SUBMITTED: the List shows as "Done · unbilled" and she can still change its Bookings until the office authorises it. Every change is audited and shows at review as Changed.
  • AUTHORISED: the Bookings are locked for everyone.
  • DRAFT belongs to a Draft List, which has no anaesthetist yet. She never sees Draft Lists.
  • Sees only her own Lists and patients. Sees colleagues' availability and phone numbers when she looks for cover, never their patients.
  • Never sees the office's tiers, preferred pairings or who she is Not preferred with.
  • Sets her own rate per unit. Cannot change her contact details, HPI CPN, bank details or any other master data.
  • Cannot authorise a List, assign a Draft List or see the office's notifications.

Pain points the design answers

  • Bookings change right up to the day of the procedure.
  • The hospital's list and the List in theatre can disagree.
  • Base, time and modifier units are fiddly and decide what she is paid.
  • Short-notice illness needs a fast answer, often at six in the morning.
  • Some users are not comfortable with complex software.

Persona 3: Kirsty W., AA scheduling coordinator

Context

Kirsty works in the AA office and sees every anaesthetist. She looks after standing arrangements, bookings by phone, email and PDF, hospital changes, cancellations and short-notice cover.

Goals

  • See the whole day across every anaesthetist.
  • Find a free session fast when a surgeon's office calls (Find available on Day view).
  • Keep hospital, surgeon, times and Bookings right.
  • Get every Draft List to an anaesthetist.
  • Keep a record of every change and who made it.

Core actions

  1. Read Day view: one row per anaesthetist, AM and PM (and an out-of-hours session where one is open), each showing its List or its availability. Draft Lists wait in their own band at the top. The right rail holds To-do, Pre-payments, Needs an invoice destination, Draft Lists and Notifications.
  2. Work Intake: Import hospital file, then on Matching create, apply or reject each row. Nothing reaches the schedule until the office decides, and the procedure text keeps the hospital's wording. On PDF lists, open each rooms' PDF with the address it came from, correct a row beside the original and Send to matching. Every List keeps where it came from.
  3. Set each new Booking's Contract on the step rail: where the invoice goes (Patient, the List's hospital, or Special for an insurer, a funder or a surgeon who is billed), then Standard or Prepaid (or the hospital's funding tag), then the Contract, chosen by typing. No contract · RVG and No contract · Fixed price are always offered. The Contract decides who is invoiced: its holder, or the payer on the Procedure.
  4. Keep each anaesthetist's own price list: the Contracts they hold, such as Dr Souter's prepaid rhinoplasty, blepharoplasty and breast augmentation at her own fixed prices.
  5. Book by phone: Book (phone advice) on a Free session, NHI Look up, the procedure typed as it was told, then the step rail.
  6. Assign Draft Lists. They come from a recurring booking that meets an unavailable session, a List an anaesthetist returns, a sickness, or a request by phone, email or PDF. Only the office assigns one. The picker lists who is free, in the office's priority tiers, then preferred pairings; a Not preferred pairing sits apart with a warning, and she can still go ahead. Tiers and pairings are the office's alone.
  7. Record a sickness with Change status on Day view. A booked session asks where its List goes, back to the Draft Lists or on to a colleague, there and then. Conflicts lists hospital closures.
  8. Open an out-of-hours session for an emergency or late case: Open out-of-hours session on an anaesthetist's row, any day from today, one a day, 18:00 to 21:00 unless changed. Put a List in it, a new one or a Draft List for that evening, and close an empty one from its drawer.
  9. Move a Booking to another List, or reassign a whole List. A move never resets a Contract or a typed price; a Contract that does not fit the new List is flagged at review.
  10. Work the To-do list: each warning with Open; a strong one needs Clear, a mild one has Clear (optional). Warnings cover a missing approval number, a child as the payer, base units outside the RVG guide, a patient who owes AA and an unpaid prepayment. None of them blocks anything.
  11. Work Notifications, the one pool the whole office shares: every List or Booking an anaesthetist moves, and every alert to the office. Nothing in it needs approving. Resolve takes a notice away for everyone, Show resolved brings it back with who and when, and nothing expires.
  12. Save each Booking on purpose with Save changes: each save is one change set with her name on it, shown in History, where View as at shows the Booking at any save.
  13. Draft the update email when a change matters: Draft update email from the Booking, the List drawer after a cover change, or the notice of an anaesthetist's move. She ticks the saved changes to report, the template from Master data → Email templates fills the wording, and the email opens in her own mail client. The system does not send it.
  14. Keep master data by hand after the one-off go-live load: anaesthetists (profile, unit value, HPI CPN and bank details, which every anaesthetist record needs), hospitals and holidays, surgeons, specialties, surgeon groups, recurring bookings, availability statuses, public holidays, Parties, funding tags, Contracts and their price ranges, RVG groups and modifiers, email templates and prepayment letters. Every change is audited.

Surgeon contacts: contacts sit on the surgeon, one personal contact and one or more office contacts (name, email, phone), with the practice name as plain text. The office enters them and the surgeon's HPI CPN, and can save a surgeon without one. Each List keeps its sender, and the update email and the missing-NHI request go to that sender, else to the surgeon's first office contact.

Public sessions: an anaesthetist marks a session Public from her calendar, with a note such as CPH; it shows no List detail, nobody can book into it or cover it, and setting it back to Free reopens it.

Help: Help in the side nav has two tabs, the Help line (Stratos' line for anything in the apps, 8:30am to 5:00pm on weekdays, out of hours only a security breach or the platform being down) and Product support (AA's line to Stratos for incidents, fixes, changes and new features; a public-holiday call-out waits for AA's approval). Whether AA needs cover from 8:00am is open (OQ-141).

Permissions

  • Sees every List and Booking.
  • Edits ACTIVE and SUBMITTED Lists and Bookings, and changes any price until the List is AUTHORISED (or a prepayment invoice is raised).
  • Nobody edits a Booking after AUTHORISED.
  • Assigns Draft Lists, reassigns a List or a Booking, and keeps tiers and preferred pairings.
  • Holds each anaesthetist's profile, bank details and HPI CPN, and can change any anaesthetist's unit value.

Pain points

  • Phone, email and PDF are still the main ways bookings arrive.
  • Each hospital provides its bookings differently. Sample files stand in for each hospital's file while the integration team confirms how each one provides them (OQ-13).
  • Several sources can change the same Booking. Each office save is one named change set.
  • Manual checking is slow and error-prone.
  • Illness and late changes need resolving the same morning.

How the schedule is built

Kirsty does not build each day by hand. The system gives every active anaesthetist an AM and a PM session for every day of the scheduling horizon. Their own availability paints first, then the surgeons' recurring bookings fill the sessions with Lists. Where a recurring booking meets an unavailable session, it becomes a Draft List. Kirsty manages the assignments, the Bookings and the exceptions.

  • Correct what the office owns without sending the List back.
  • Authorise a clean List, knowing it is then locked.
  • Keep the money moving after authorise: invoices, prepayments, events, credits, the weekly payment run and AA's fee.

Core actions

  1. Open Review queue and a submitted List with Review →. Each row shows the procedure text as received, its Contract and RVG code, Paid by, the fee and any flag.
  2. Read the flags: a Contract change (Approve or Correct), a typed price ("Price typed" with the computed price), a $0.00 price (No charge), a Contract this List does not offer, a Contract disabled before the procedure date, a Procedure that needs a price, a Booking not marked completed. The Changed marker shows edits made after submit. Only a Procedure with no price, or a Booking not completed, holds up authorise.
  3. Correct billing setup herself: the Contract, the payer, the price. Authorising approves any Contract change still waiting.
  4. Split a Procedure's fee from its Paid by line when more than one party pays, until authorise.
  5. Authorise for billing. Authorise takes the pricing snapshot: each Procedure's Contract and the price range in force, the units and rate, the price, who is billed and who is paid. The Billing Engine raises one invoice per payer from that record and sends it in the same step, emailed or marked To post. A $0.00 Booking gets a "No charge" invoice with nothing to pay out.
  6. Keep invoices moving: Resend on an invoice, or type an address on one that reads No invoice address and Send.
  7. Approve prepayment invoices on Invoices (Approve and send, with the letter; AA can switch the approval step off), send a reminder with Send prepayment reminder while one is unpaid or part paid, and keep the three letters on Master data → Prepayment letters. On a cancelled prepaid Booking, Rebook with another anaesthetist at that anaesthetist's own price.
  8. Review every event, the anaesthetists' and the office's, on Review → Events. She adds pre-op, post-op and billing events herself, prices an event's time at review, and keeps each Contract's Event fees. An approved event bills in the next billing run.
  9. Raise an additional invoice to any party with Create additional invoice, free-form lines, reviewed and billed in the next run with its own number and its own Xero pair.
  10. Correct an issued invoice without editing it, from Correct this invoice: Credit note, Credit in full and rebill (the new invoice starts from a copy of the original's lines), or Split into additional invoices when the payer wants a combined procedure in pieces. A credit note always covers the whole invoice. Each credit is reviewed on Review → Events and issued in the next run.
  11. Watch the Billing monitor: every List grouped by anaesthetist (invoiced, sent, in Xero, paid in, paid out), Problems only, and the Xero sync queue, where a failed hand-off waits and retries by itself or with Retry now. One Booking's failure holds back only that Booking.

Critical rule

There is no RETURNED state. If the office finds a problem after submit, Kirsty phones the anaesthetist and fixes it herself. The anaesthetist can still edit the List until it is authorised.

Workflow 0: the schedule canvas

Primary actor: the Scheduling engine, set up by the AA office In the demo: S2 Beat 1, S2 Beat 3 and its staged-clash aside, S5's go-live aside Requirements: EP-01, EP-13

Trigger

  • The schedule horizon (Master data → Schedule settings, four months) rolls forward each day, or the office changes it.
  • An anaesthetist is added with a start date, or switched active or inactive.
  • An anaesthetist changes their calendar, or a recurring booking, hospital holiday or public holiday changes.

Steps

  1. Every active anaesthetist has an AM and a PM session for each day from their start date to the end of the horizon. Every new session starts Free.
  2. The anaesthetist's calendar goes on first: days off weeks ahead, repeating days off, and one occurrence changed or deleted. The calendar sets the session's status directly. Seeded public sessions are Public with the note CPH, and Public closes a session as Unavailable does.
  3. Recurring bookings (each surgeon's standing arrangement of hospital, anaesthetist, day and session) go on next. Each one places an empty List in its free sessions ahead. A recurring booking that lands on a session the anaesthetist has closed becomes a Draft List for the office.
  4. A change to a recurring booking reaches the canvas through Apply to canvas now, from tomorrow to the end of the horizon. Empty Lists are added or updated; a List with Bookings or office edits is left as it is. When nothing differs the page reads "The canvas already matches every recurring booking."
  5. A session shows the same thing in every app and every finder: its List when it has one, otherwise its status.
  6. A closed hospital puts every List there that day on Conflicts. The List stays where it is until the clash goes or the office clears it. Unavailability is never a conflict: Workflow 3 covers it.

Handoff

The office gets a schedule already laid out, and works on the exceptions: Draft Lists, conflicts, sickness and new Bookings.

Master data the office keeps

  • Surgeons: one or more specialties, the practice name as plain text, one personal contact and one or more office contacts (practice manager, PA or shared inbox: name, email, phone). Admin staff fill in the HPI CPN. A surgeon can be saved without one and reads "No HPI CPN yet" (OQ-143).
  • Surgeon groups, hospitals and their holidays, public holidays, AA's specialties and its funding tags (SXAP, HNZ, ACC, in the order they are offered).
  • Priority tiers (Tier 1 to Tier 4) on each anaesthetist, and pairing preferences (Preferred, Not preferred) on the surgeon and the anaesthetist. Both are private to the office. Anaesthetists never see them, and an anaesthetist handing on her own List gets no pairing warning.
  • Email templates, one for each kind of change (Change of anaesthetist, Cancellation, Correction of an error, Booking change), and the prepayment letters.

Each List keeps where it came from. The update email and any request for a missing NHI go to that List's sender, otherwise to the surgeon's first office contact with an email, with the others offered. The personal contact is never the default.

Go-live

AA starts clean. Before go-live a developer loads AA's controlled spreadsheets once (Master data → Seed load and Go-live load). Every row goes through the same checks as one added by hand, and a row that fails is listed with its reason and left out. From Solutions Plus the load takes operation names only, never its unit values. After go-live the office keeps every master by hand, and every change is audited. There is no Contract upload or export in the first release.

Demo point

Workflow 1: booking intake by import and matching

Primary actors: the AA office (Kirsty), the surgeon's office, the hospital In the demo: S1 Beat 2 and its asides, S4's unmatched-queue aside, S5 Beat 2 Requirements: EP-02, EP-11, EP-14

Triggers

  • A surgeon's office emails a PDF list. The office uploads it on Intake → PDF lists with the address it came from.
  • A surgeon's office phones or emails AA (Workflow 2's phone-advice path).
  • The anaesthetist finds a patient missing on the day and adds the Booking herself.

Main path

  1. Every row from a hospital file or a rooms' PDF list waits on the matching screen. Nothing reaches a Booking, List or patient until the office decides.
  2. The screen suggests what to do with each row: create a Booking on a List, apply changes to an existing Booking, cancel one, or create a List in a session (or a Draft List) for a row with no List. The office applies, creates or rejects each one.
  3. A row's NHI finds the patient's existing record, so a returning patient keeps one record. A patient who owes AA raises the balance warning when the Booking is created.
  4. The procedure text stays exactly as received. The rooms may send "RTK" and the hospital "right total knee": the hospital's text replaces the rooms' text once the office applies its row, and the rooms' wording stays in the Booking's History. The invoice description starts as the text received; where a Contract has its own invoice description, the invoice prints that instead.
  5. The office sets where the invoice goes and the Contract (Workflow 4's step rail), on the matching screen or later from the Needs an invoice destination list on the Day view.
  6. A later row for a Booking already matched waits as a modification. The office ticks the fields to apply (time, procedure text, note) and approves it. A Booking moved to another day arrives as a cancellation plus a new Booking. A row for a submitted List can still be applied; a row for an authorised List cannot.
  7. The matching screen shows only the fields needed to match. The rest of what the hospital sent is held under restricted access: the office sees it masked with View raw row, giving a reason each time, and every view is audited with the reason and no values ("Raw row viewed"). Anyone else is refused ("Raw row view refused").
  8. Every List records where it came from (a rooms' PDF with its sender, the hospital's list, by hand, or a recurring booking) and keeps it after assignment.

A row with no NHI

  • An NHI is mandatory, so a row without one never becomes a Booking. It waits on Matching and on Admin → Patients → Waiting for NHI.
  • Meanwhile a stub stands on its List: a dashed row reading "Waiting at intake: no NHI · The office is resolving this", with the time, name and wording as received. The List counts it apart ("1 waiting at intake"), and it never blocks submit or authorise. In Admin it carries Resolve on Matching.
  • The office uses Email for the NHI to ask the List's sender, then Add NHI. The Booking takes the stub's place. Rejecting the row removes the stub.
  • How a stub reads is our reading, to confirm with AA.
  • Seeded: Mere Tipene on Dr Souter's Tue 4 Aug PM List.

The rooms' PDF lists

  • On Intake → PDF lists the office opens the list beside its facsimile, corrects a row it can see is wrong (an NHI that fails its check digit, say) and selects Send to matching. The rows land on Matching with the rooms' wording, like a hospital file's.
  • A List made from a PDF reads "From a rooms' PDF · sent from <address>" with Replies go to that address.
  • A re-sent list is matched to the earlier one by surgeon, hospital, date and session (our reading). Unchanged rows are skipped, a changed field waits as a difference, and a new patient arrives as a new row. A patient dropped from the re-sent list is not detected (Future scope).

Other ways in

  • Phone advice: Kirsty opens a free session on the Day view and selects Book (phone advice), or adds a Booking to a List she may edit. The procedure stays exactly as the caller said it, with Told by Phone or Email.
  • The anaesthetist's own Booking: Dr Souter adds a missing patient on her List, on the phone or the web, with the procedure as written on the hospital's list. If she knows it she sets the Contract on the step rail; otherwise the Booking waits on the office's Needs an invoice destination list.
  • NHI lookup: on every Add a booking the NHI is checked in both formats as it is typed, and Look up stays disabled until it is valid. Look up asks the Digital Services Hub (simulated over a synthetic NHI register) and fills in the patient, still editable. The purpose note names who it is looked up for, by HPI CPN. When the Hub is down the details are typed by hand; the NHI is still required and must be valid.
  • Booking from a photo of a paper booking: Future scope, behind a badged demo action on the List.

Handoff

The Booking shows on the assigned anaesthetist's List on mobile and web, with the procedure text as received and where it came from ("Hospital download, St George's").

Points to keep straight

  • Intake is an import that the office decides row by row. Nothing applies by itself.
  • Hospitals and surgeons send bookings; they have no login in the prototype.

Trigger

Steps

Workflow 4: capture a Booking

Primary actor: the anaesthetist (Dr Souter) In the demo: S1 Beats 3, 6 and 7 and their asides, S3's 3-procedure aside Requirements: EP-03, EP-04, EP-05, EP-11

Trigger

The procedure has happened, and the anaesthetist records what is billed.

Setting the Contract

Every Procedure has its own Contract. The office sets it through the step rail, and the anaesthetist can change it until the office authorises the List:

  1. Where does the invoice go? Patient, the List's hospital (with its funding tag) or Special (an insurer, a funder or a surgeon who is billed, then which holder).
  2. Under Patient, Standard or prepaid? Under the List's hospital, its funding tag, filled in by itself when the hospital has only one.
  3. The Contract, found by typing. Two backstops always sit under "No contract": No contract · RVG (priced from RVG units, for an invoice to the patient that is not prepaid) and No contract · Fixed price (a person types the amount).

A "Suggested" mark follows the insurer or funder on the Booking and never hides a path. Start again goes back to step 1. Who is invoiced comes from the Contract: its holder, or the payer on the Procedure (its Paid by line).

Steps

  1. Dr Souter opens her List and the patient's Booking. On a handset she signed in once with her demo account, and the phone keeps her signed in. How anaesthetists sign in for real is still to be decided (OQ-83).
  2. She reads the procedure text as received, with when and where it came from, and the Billing section below it: Invoice to, Contract, Price and Paid by.
  3. What each Procedure asks for follows its Contract's pricing kind. On an RVG price (No contract · RVG, an RVG rate or an RVG discount) the RVG code, the start and finish times and the modifiers step are required, each marked Required until answered. On a fixed or open price there are no times, units or modifiers, and the RVG code is optional.
  4. She finds the RVG code by typing (Choose the RVG code), under its body-area heading. The base units start from the RVG group. On a group with a range she may change the figure, and a value outside the range raises a mild warning for the office.
  5. She records the start and finish (Start now, Finish now, or typed). Time units follow the RVG tiers: 1 unit per 15 minutes for the first two hours, then 1 unit per 10 minutes. The T stepper also lets her set the time units by hand, shown as "Adjusted manually".
  6. The patient's age modifier, and any modifier the procedure's base units include (P1 on Neurosurgery and Spine codes), come in applied, and she can remove them. She claims any other modifier that applies, ASA included, each with a short explanation. Modifiers stack with no limit, and the same modifier is never added twice. An included modifier shows at 0 units. If nothing else applies she taps None apply, which reads "No modifiers claimed · Change". A modifier already applied does not answer the step on its own.
  7. The Price section shows what the Contract comes to as she records ("8 units x $26.50 = $212.00"), and the Booking total sits above Mark complete (the phone's dock, the web's side panel).
  8. She types a price only where the Contract allows it: on an RVG Contract that allows it, such as No contract · RVG (a PRICE $ field and Save price), or the amount on No contract · Fixed price. Elsewhere the price is read only: "Set by {Contract}. To use a different price, change the Contract or alert the office." The office can change any price until the List is authorised, and Review shows a typed price beside the computed one.
  9. She can add an event under the Procedure: Add pre-op or post-op event, or Add billing event (a charge or credit with free text). Workflow 8 covers both.
  10. Mark complete checks three things: every Procedure has a Contract; on an RVG price, the RVG code, both times and the modifiers step, with a reason for each claimed modifier; and a price (a Procedure with none reads Needs a price). An early press jumps to the first thing missing and shows its message. A warning never stops it.
  11. Something only the office can fix goes through Alert the office.
  12. The info sign beside each heading explains the section.

More than one Procedure

  • One Booking can hold several Procedures. One is the primary, listed first with a Primary pill. The first Procedure entered is the primary until someone changes it with Make primary (a sheet on the phone, a dialog on desktop: "Make this the primary procedure?"). Nothing is ever promoted without a person choosing it, and the primary cannot be removed.
  • Who can use Make primary: the anaesthetist on her own List until it is authorised (after Amend on a completed Booking), the office on ACTIVE and SUBMITTED Lists, a hospital feed on an ACTIVE List only.
  • The multi-procedure rule, one rule for the whole system: base units on the primary only; time units on every Procedure from its own times; modifiers recorded once, on the primary. At 4 modifier units or fewer they all stay on the primary. Above 4 they split equally across the RVG-priced Procedures, the remainder to the primary (7 over 3 gives 3, 2 and 2), whichever Contracts they are on.
  • A fixed or open price keeps its own price and takes no share. On a mixed Booking whose primary has a fixed price, no base units bill (provisional, OQ-132).
  • Seeded: Graeme Tullett on Dr Souter's Mon 13 Jul AM List (three Procedures, 7 modifier units, split 3, 2 and 2) and Brian Holt on her Mon 20 Jul AM List (two Procedures, one invoice).

Handoff

When every active Booking is complete, the List can be submitted.

Workflow 6: the billing run and the invoices

Primary actor: the Billing Engine, watched by the office In the demo: S3 Beats 1 and 2 and their asides, S5 Beats 4 and 5 Requirements: EP-08, EP-04, EP-05

Trigger

The List reaches AUTHORISED.

Steps

  1. The Billing Engine reads each active Booking's locked record and nothing else. It never looks a Contract, payer, rate or RVG group up afresh, and never puts another Contract in its place. A Booking with no lock fails on its own.
  2. A Contract edit, a Disable or a new price range after authorise changes nothing here. Regenerate from locked data rebuilds an invoice from the lock and reads "identical".
  3. Each Procedure's locked Contract says who is billed: its holder when the Contract bills its holder (a hospital, an insurer, a surgeon group), otherwise the Procedure's payer: the patient, or whoever the Paid by sheet names (a parent, a guardian, a benefactor, a family trust). A surgeon's fixed price, No contract · RVG, No contract · Fixed price, an anaesthetist's own price list and every prepaid Contract bill the payer. The insurer or funder on the Booking never decides it.
  4. Procedures that share a payer share one invoice. Payers are never matched on name or email, so a payer typed on two Procedures gets an invoice for each.
  5. A Procedure's fee can be split (the Split button on its Paid by line, set by the office or the anaesthetist before authorise: each share in $ or %, one party taking the rest). Each party gets an invoice for its share.
  6. The price comes from the lock: units at the rate, less any discount (printed as its own line), or the fixed price from the range in force on the procedure date. The multi-procedure rule applies to RVG-priced Procedures. A typed price of $0.00 still gets an invoice, marked "No charge".
  7. Each invoice is in the name of the anaesthetist locked at authorise, as supplier with her GST number, with AA as her agent (the agency wording is provisional, OQ-29). The invoice's Locked record card shows what it was raised from.
  8. The layout follows the Contract: the holder's layout when it bills its holder (its invoice descriptions, the B, T and M breakdown, the procedure date and surgeon, and the Contract's own code as "Your reference"), otherwise the patient's. Prices are held without GST, and GST goes on at the foot.
  9. The run sends each invoice by the Party's delivery: by email, or To post for a payer with only a postal address (provisional, OQ-122). How each Party's invoices are delivered is still open (OQ-114). A payer with neither reads No invoice address until the office adds one and sends. The office can Resend from the invoice; there is no separate email step.

Handoff

The Billing Engine sends each invoice to Xero as a matched pair. Each ACCPAY is the buyer-created tax invoice for one receivable invoice, at the same value, naming the anaesthetist as supplier and AA as agent (provisional, OQ-29; one BCTI per invoice is provisional, OQ-29 and OQ-60).

How many invoices

One invoice per payer per Booking. A Booking with several Procedures and one payer is one invoice, and one BCTI. A split fee gives one invoice per share.

Workflow 7: collection, the ledger and paying the anaesthetist

Primary actors: Xero, the Billing Engine, the office and AA's accountant In the demo: S3 Beats 3, 4, 5 and 7, S4 Beat 6 and its asides Requirements: EP-08, EP-09, EP-10, EP-12

Trigger

An invoice is raised.

Steps

  1. Every invoice is a pair in AA's own ledger, created with the invoice: a receivable from the payer and a payable to the anaesthetist, the same amount. AA's own fee is invoiced separately and never deducted here.
  2. The Billing Engine creates the matching pair in Xero:
    • ACCREC: what the hospital, insurer or patient owes AA;
    • ACCPAY: what AA owes the anaesthetist, equal to the ACCREC, starting as a Draft bill. Both carry the invoice number (the ACCPAY's ends -P) and the case reference.
  3. The payer pays into AA's account. A Xero webhook tells the Billing Engine, and a daily poll catches anything missed.
  4. The matching payable is authorised for exactly the amount received, part payments included. The rest stays outstanding on the invoice.
  5. The released payable waits for the week's payment run on the Billing monitor's Payment cycle panel:
    • the office closes the week (planned for Friday), which reconciles it to the bank download;
    • the Monday checks list anything odd, and a problem not sorted in time is held out of this run and rolls into next week;
    • the office approves the week's BCTIs (Approve week N's BCTIs), and nothing is paid without it;
    • on Tuesday the payment schedule goes to the accountant, who pays it in Xero.
  6. A bill-paid webhook, or the daily poll, tells the Billing Engine, which records the payment. The app never pays anyone itself.
  7. Each run is named by its ISO week and kept as a record: its page, its payment schedule and a remittance advice for each anaesthetist, which she reads under Accounts → Remittances on the web. Every payment shows the week it was paid in. A negative invoice nets against her payments in the run. The weekly cycle is proposed and being confirmed with AA's accountant (OQ-47).
  8. A prepayment's payable sits in the AA Holding account and no run pays it. The anaesthetist is paid only after the transfer at authorise (Workflow 8).
  9. The anaesthetist sees her position from the Billing Engine's own ledger, never from Xero: owed to you, collected, paid out to you; one flat list of unpaid invoices, oldest first, each reading "Paid in, due to you" once the money is in; her payment history; and the monthly GST report of what AA paid her, on a cash basis.
  10. The office checks Admin → Ledger: receipts held, less payables due, the AA Holding account and credits held for payers, plus negative invoices to net, come to $0.00 ("In balance · receipts held equal payables due"). A receipt that matches no invoice puts the ledger out of balance, with the anaesthetist it names on the out-of-balance list, until the office allocates or refunds it.
  11. Patient and payer contacts in Xero carry only a hidden internal ID: no name, NHI or other personal information. Once fully paid and inactive for a set number of days, the nightly job archives them (Master data → Xero & archiving).

Xero going wrong

  1. Every call to Xero (a hand-off, a payment, a disbursement, the poll) is written to the Xero sync queue first. If Xero does not answer, the call waits and retries with growing gaps. The Billing monitor shows each waiting call, and Retry now sends one straight away.
  2. Authorising and invoicing carry on during an outage. The invoice and its ledger pair stand, and the List reads Waiting for Xero until its hand-off lands.
  3. When Xero is back, the queue sends what waited in order, once each: no duplicate pair, contact, receipt or disbursement.
  4. An invoice voided or edited straight in Xero is found at the daily poll and flagged on the Billing monitor (Changed directly in Xero). The ledger keeps what AA issued, and the office records a resolution, which moves no money. The policy for each mismatch is provisional, to be agreed with AA.
  5. A hospital that pays many invoices with one bank payment is matched in Xero: its remittance matches each line by invoice number, and the engine learns each payment through the normal webhook. A line Xero cannot match waits in Xero's bank reconciliation for the office (Demo actions → Simulate bulk hospital remittance, then Match in Xero).
  6. The Day view's Reconciliation card says whether the ledger and Xero agree (Books square) or not (Out of balance), with each item to resolve and how long it has waited.

Handoff

The financial episode closes when the money is in and the anaesthetist has been paid.

S3 Beat 7 closes on the ledger: in balance, then a $120.00 receipt that matches no invoice puts it out of balance until the office allocates it, and the Reconciliation card reads Books square again.

Workflow 8: exceptions and compliance

Primary actors: the office, the anaesthetist, the Billing Engine In the demo: S4 throughout, S2 Beat 2, S5 Requirements: EP-06, EP-08, EP-10, EP-11, EP-12, EP-13, EP-15

Prepayment

  • A Procedure on a prepaid Contract is paid in full before the procedure. The office picks the prepaid Contract (Patient, then Prepaid), often one of the anaesthetist's own price list, which the office keeps. A prepaid Contract always bills the Procedure's payer, awaiting AA's confirmation (OQ-73).
  • One prepayment invoice per payer, each payer locked once theirs is raised. The amount is the Procedure's price when the invoice is raised, read only after; on No contract · Fixed price, an amount the office types. That is the prepaid amount. The anaesthetist sees it and cannot change it ("Set by the office").
  • The office approves and sends it from Admin → Invoices (Approve and send). The approval step is on by default and AA can switch it off, in which case the invoice goes by itself. It goes with the prepayment letter from Master data → Prepayment letters (the Standard prepayment letter by default), which names the anaesthetist (provisional, OQ-120). The letter says the anaesthetist may send a further invoice or a credit if the procedure turns out very different.
  • The invoice's pair credits the AA Holding account, never the anaesthetist. While it is unpaid or part paid, the office can Send prepayment reminder from the Booking for what is still to pay; the panel and the Day view rail show when it was sent.
  • The AA Holding account is a payables creditor, kept like an anaesthetist's account. It is not a bank account and not a Xero ledger account, and no payment run pays it. Money leaves it only by a transfer or a refund the system raises.
  • Two steps, and the anaesthetist is paid only after the second:
    1. Collected into the AA Holding account. The payer's money lands, the payable to the AA Holding account is authorised for what was received, and the Billing monitor's AA Holding account card shows a Received entry.
    2. Transferred at authorise. When the office authorises the List, the system writes a transfer from the AA Holding account to the anaesthetist who did the List, with no office step: a Transferred entry on the card, and a manual journal in the Xero simulation. No invoice or payment is raised and GST does not move. The next weekly payment run pays the anaesthetist. The release at authorise is provisional (OQ-125), and the card says so.
  • Money paid after the transfer goes straight to the anaesthetist's payable as it arrives. A part-paid prepayment moves in full at authorise and is released as money arrives.
  • An unpaid prepayment never blocks. It is a warning that turns strong as the date nears, so the anaesthetist sees it before surgery starts.
  • After the procedure the prepaid amount is the price. The Procedure stays off the final invoice, and nothing more is invoiced or credited by itself; the Prepaid settlement block shows the recorded units for reference only. A real difference is raised by hand, by the anaesthetist or the office: a credit note, always for the whole prepayment invoice, and anything still owed as a new additional invoice. Both go through review and the next run. Before authorise, only the office credits it.
  • A move changes nothing in the books: the patient is not billed again, nothing is repointed, and the transfer pays whoever did the List.
  • A cancel settles it on the spot, whoever cancels: credited if unpaid, credited and refunded in full from the AA Holding account if paid, and a payment that arrives after the credit is refunded straight back. A Contract changed off prepaid after sending is settled the same way. Rebook with another anaesthetist starts a fresh prepayment at the new anaesthetist's own price.

Warnings and the to-do list

  • One routine checks each Booking and raises warnings, each with its own text, a kind (before or after the procedure) and a strength (mild or strong). A Booking can carry several.
  • A warning never blocks. Saving, completing, submitting and authorising all go through, with no confirm step.
  • The anaesthetist sees a triangle on the Booking's row and the warning at the top of the Booking when she opens it. The office sees the same triangle, the Day view outline, and the To-do card on the Day view.
  • The office clears a warning once it has dealt with it (Clear, or Clear (optional) on a mild one). The clearance is audited, and the Booking shows who cleared it and when.
  • The rules: an unpaid prepayment (strong as the date nears); base units outside the RVG guide; a child as the payer on a Procedure; an insurer named with no approval number (an insurer only, never ACC or Health NZ); a split that does not fit the fee; and a patient who owes AA (mild under the threshold, strong over it: Master data → Patient balance warning, 90 days).
  • The to-do list holds warnings that need action. Moves and alerts go to the notification pool.

The payer and the insurer or funder

  • Every Procedure names a payer: the patient, filled in from the patient record. The office and the anaesthetist change it in Paid by (a sheet on the phone, a dialog on the web) until the List is authorised. The change is offered to the Booking's other Procedures with the same payer, and a Procedure added later copies the payer the others share.
  • A payer needs an email or an NZ Post postal address. The Paid by sheet asks for one, and Review flags Email or postal address needed; nothing blocks.
  • The Booking's Insurer or funder row (reading "None" when empty) holds the insurer or funder, a Party, and the approval number. It comes from the hospital's row where the file carries it, or the office records it; the anaesthetist sees the number and can enter it on the day. The funder marks one path "Suggested" on the step rail. It never narrows the choice and never decides who is invoiced.

The anaesthetist's profile

  • Each profile holds the rate per unit (the dollar value of one RVG unit), phone, email, registration number, HPI CPN and bank account.
  • The anaesthetist sees it on the phone's More screen and on the web Profile page. She edits only her own rate per unit (Change on the phone, the field and Save changes on the web). The new rate applies to Bookings on Lists not yet authorised; authorised Lists keep their locked rate.
  • Contact details, the HPI CPN and the bank details are held by the office and read only for her. The bank number is always masked (Dr Souter's 99-0101-•••••21-00).
  • The office keeps every profile on the Admin anaesthetist record (Master data → Anaesthetists, open a row, Edit details), with the GST number in the table. The HPI CPN and bank details are required. Every change is audited, with the bank number masked in the audit too.
  • The payment itself is made in Xero, never from the app.

Events: pre-op, post-op and billing events

  • An event sits on a Procedure, never on a List: its date is its own. The original Booking, if authorised, stays locked.
  • The anaesthetist records her own from the Booking, reached from the main view or search: Add pre-op or post-op event, a type (Post-op ward review, HDU review, Pain consult, ACC pre-op assessment, Other pre-op, Other post-op), its date and start time, and a time recorded or a fixed fee. Invoice this event keeps it billable. It is billed to the same party as the procedure unless she names another. She sees no calculation; the office prices the time at review.
  • A billing event (Add billing event) is a charge or a credit with free text and an amount without GST. A credit is a negative line, offered only before the Procedure is invoiced; after that it is a credit note.
  • The office can add any of these too.
  • One review step (Review queue → Events) approves each one, and the next billing run bills it. It joins an invoice to the same party in that run where there is one; otherwise it gets its own invoice, linked to the Procedure's.
  • A Contract can carry an event fee that replaces the time (St George's ACC STGACC-PAIN, $120.00 for a pain consult). On a fixed-price Procedure the office sets a price.
  • ACC pre-op assessment: a pre-op event dated on or before the procedure. The sheet offers three codes, CS250, CS260 and CS70, and the code prints on its invoice line. The codes and their fee are to confirm with AA's accountant (OQ-12).

Additional invoices

  • A later ward review or pain consult never reopens the Booking. The office, or the anaesthetist on her own invoiced Procedure (found through search), raises an additional invoice with free-form lines (description, quantity, amount) to whoever should pay (Create additional invoice, or Raise additional invoice for the anaesthetist).
  • It goes through the same review step and bills in the next run, with its own number and Xero pair, linked both ways to the original.
  • After a prepaid or fixed-price procedure nothing is billed by itself; any extra is an additional invoice raised by hand.

Correction after invoicing

  • An issued invoice is never edited. A wrong amount or billable party, an invoice paid twice, or less work than a fixed price assumed, is corrected by crediting the invoice in full and rebilling.
  • The office does it from the invoice's Correct this invoice card (Credit note, or Credit in full and rebill, whose draft starts from a copy of the original's lines). The anaesthetist does it on her own invoiced Procedure (Credit note, beside Raise additional invoice).
  • The credit is an event on the Procedure, through the same review step, and is issued in the next billing run with its credit note number (CN-2026-nnnn). Any new invoice raised with it gets its own number and is linked to the original.
  • The anaesthetist's payable is always reversed by a negative invoice: offset against the payable while it is unpaid, or netted in her next weekly payment run once she has been paid. A negative invoice with no later payment to net against is settled with the anaesthetist outside the system. Both sides move together in the ledger and in Xero.
  • A credit note is not a refund. Nothing is credited without a person deciding. A disputed invoice with no AA error stays outstanding.

Splitting a combined procedure

  • When the billable party wants a combined procedure's invoice in pieces, the office uses Split into additional invoices: the invoice is credited in full and one invoice is raised per piece.
  • The office divides the amount however it needs, but the pieces must add up to what was credited. Left to allocate must reach $0.00 before it can be saved.
  • It goes through the same review step and run as any credit. Before an invoice is sent, the office edits the Booking instead.

Billing failure

  • The run prices only from the lock, so a Contract edit cannot make it fail. What can still fail is outside the lock, such as the hand-off to Xero.
  • Failure is per Booking: the failed Booking is held back (a split Booking's invoices with it) while the rest of the List bills.
  • A failed hand-off waits in the Xero sync queue, which retries it by itself, and Retry now on its row sends it at once. A failure in the run itself shows its reason and Retry on the Booking's row, which rebuilds from the lock. Problems only on the Billing monitor narrows the list to these.
  • The fix never edits a Contract or any other master. A wrong price after invoicing is corrected by a credit note and rebill.

Patients

  • An NHI is required for every Booking, checked in both the current and the new format as it is typed.
  • The patient record (Admin → Patients) holds what the patient owes under each anaesthetist, what other payers owe, their invoices and Bookings, and a follow-up log (call, note, reminder), with Resend on an unpaid invoice and Clear on the balance warning. The anaesthetist sees only that the patient owes AA.
  • The office refreshes a patient from the NHI register on demand (Refresh from NHI register, marked Provisional): each field that differs is Keep or Use register, the change reaches the patient's Bookings, the record reads "Last refreshed", and the refresh is audited. Nothing goes to Xero.
  • On Patients → Waiting for NHI, the Add NHI sheet looks up a valid NHI and shows the register's name and date of birth, with a mild line if they differ from the row's. It never blocks the add.

Compliance

  • Every change is audited, including those the system makes, with who made it, their role, where it came from, and the value before and after.
  • An NHI is validated before any lookup, its use is limited to its purpose (Health Information Privacy Code 2020), and practitioners are referenced by their HPI CPN.
  • No NHI or other personal information goes to Xero, patient names included. The Data inspector's leak scan checks the Xero records, the diagnostic log and every failure reason.
  • Sign-in, sign-out and failed attempts are recorded against the account.

The 30-second explanation

Anaesthesia Associates books and bills for about 85 independent anaesthetists. The system keeps a rolling schedule of AM and PM sessions, keeps each patient's Booking current, and lets the anaesthetist record what the bill needs. The office checks and authorises each finished List, and the Billing Engine raises the invoices. Xero handles receivables and banking. The Billing Engine's own ledger is what each anaesthetist sees.

The five nouns to remember

Term Meaning
Session An anaesthetist's AM or PM (or an evening Out-of-hours session). Every session exists, Free by default, and holds a status or a List.

Contracts and pricing

The plain words below follow the contract model (AR-58) and its ERD (AR-61), as the app builds them.

Parties, specialties and funding tags

  • Every Contract is held by a Party. Parties live in Master data → Parties, each with its billing name and how its invoices are delivered (email or post).
  • A Contract carries none or more specialties (none means every specialty) and at most one funding tag: SXAP, HNZ or ACC. Together with who it bills, the tag decides where the step rail offers it.
  • ACC is a funding tag. St George's Contract "ACC elective services via St George's" carries it.
  • The office keeps the specialties and funding tags in Master data. Every change is audited.

One Contract per priced item

  • Each priced item is its own Contract, with AA's own code (for example MER-001), an invoice description and an optional default RVG code.
  • A combination such as Merivale's Facelift plus 1 add on is its own Contract. Fee extras (a GA add-on fee, per 15 minutes over 120, per area) are billing events.
  • A fixed price has price ranges. The range in force on the procedure date gives the price. A new range can start in the past: everything not yet authorised, prepaid or priced by hand re-prices at once. An authorised Booking keeps its price.
  • Every other change is an edit in place, audited with the values before and after. A change that must start on a date is a new Contract, and the office disables the old one. Nothing is deleted.
  • No contract · RVG and one Fixed price Contract for every place an invoice can go are made by the system and marked System. They are always there.
  • On Master data → Contracts, find a Contract by typing its name, its code, a word of its invoice description or its default RVG code.

The step rail

The office sets the Contract when it sets up the Booking, in three steps:

  1. Where does the invoice go? Patient, the List's hospital, or Special, each with its count.
  2. Then the payment type or tag. Under Patient, "Standard or prepaid?" is always asked. Under the hospital, its funding tag. Under Special, "Which holder?".
  3. The Contract, typed to search. The list opens on the No contract heading, with No contract · RVG and No contract · Fixed price first, then the holder's own Contracts.
  • Patient › Standard has one Contract, No contract · RVG, with RVG code and Fixed price beside it.
  • A patient paying for themselves is on No contract · RVG. A patient insured with nib is billed to nib only under nib's own Contract.
  • "Suggested" marks the path that fits the insurer or funder on the Booking. With none named, it marks Patient and Standard. It never hides a path.
  • An RVG-priced Contract leaves the RVG code to the anaesthetist.
  • The anaesthetist sees the same rail and can change the Contract until the office authorises the List. Each change reads "Awaiting office approval" and is flagged at review. She never picks Prepaid.

Who is invoiced

  • Each Contract says whether it bills its holder or the payer on the Procedure. A prepaid Contract always bills the payer.
  • The payer is on each Procedure, in its Paid by line, filled from the patient. Change names someone else: a parent, a guardian, a benefactor or a family trust. A change is offered to the Booking's other Procedures with the same payer.
  • A payer needs an email or a postal address. A postal address alone is enough, and the invoice is then marked To post (provisional, OQ-122).
  • The insurer or funder sits on the Booking with its approval number. It never changes who is invoiced.
  • One invoice per billable party per Booking. Procedures with the same payer share one. A Split gives one invoice per share.

Units and the RVG code

  • Base comes from the RVG group, on the primary Procedure only.
  • Time comes from start and finish: 1 unit per 15 minutes for the first two hours, then 1 per 10 minutes. A part interval rounds up. The office keeps the tiers in Master data → RVG groups.
  • Modifiers are a step the anaesthetist answers, even with None apply. The age modifier, and P1 where the base units include it, come in applied and can be removed. Anything else she claims needs a short reason, ASA included.
  • Base units outside the RVG guide raise a warning. It never blocks.

The price rules

  • Five pricing kinds: RVG at the anaesthetist's rate, RVG at the Contract's rate, RVG less a discount, a fixed price from its range, and an amount typed on the Procedure.
  • A price follows its Contract until a typed price, a prepayment invoice or authorise holds it. If nothing gives a price, the Procedure reads Needs a price.
  • The anaesthetist sees what the price comes to as she records ("4 units x $26.50 = $106.00"). She can type her own price only where an RVG Contract allows it, such as No contract · RVG, and she types the amount on No contract · Fixed price.
  • Elsewhere the price is read only: "Set by Forte Health RVG less 10%. To use a different price, change the Contract or alert the office."
  • The office can change any price until the List is authorised, or until a prepayment invoice is raised. Nobody gives a reason.
  • A discount prints as its own line: $159.00 less 10% ($15.90) is $143.10.
  • A price typed at $0.00 keeps the RVG detail and raises a $0.00 "No charge" invoice, with nothing to pay or pay out.

What a Procedure asks for, and Mark complete

  • On an RVG price the form asks for the RVG code, start and finish, and the modifiers step. On a fixed price it asks for none of them, and the RVG code is "for reference".
  • Mark complete checks three things: a Contract; on an RVG price, the RVG code, both times and the modifiers step, with a reason for each claimed modifier; and a price. A warning never stops it.
  • Submit to office needs every Booking complete. The confirm reads "Submit this list to the office?", with Submit to office and Not yet.

The Split

  • Split sits on each Procedure's Paid by line. The office, or the anaesthetist on her own Booking, uses it until the List is authorised.
  • Each share is typed in $ or %, one party takes the rest, and a share can go to another person. Each party gets its own invoice. The Contract has no split setting.
  • Alan Prentice: nib $132.50, Alan $79.50 (the rest) of $212.00.

The multi-procedure rule

  • One primary per Booking, listed first. Make primary changes it, until the List is authorised.
  • Base units on the primary. Time on every Procedure. Modifiers on the primary up to 4 units; above 4 they split equally across the RVG-priced Procedures, the remainder to the primary. Graeme Tullett's 7 modifier units split 3 · 2 · 2, $636.00; making the umbilical hernia primary gives $583.00.
  • One rule for every Booking, with no switch.
  • A fixed price keeps its price and takes no share. When the primary has a fixed price, no base units bill (provisional, OQ-132).

The pricing snapshot at authorise

  • Authorising locks each Procedure's pricing onto it: the Contract's settings, the price range in force, the units, the rate, the price, who is billed and who is paid.
  • The invoice is raised from that lock. A later Contract edit, a new price range or a rate change never reaches it.
  • An ACTIVE Booking still follows its Contract.
  • Demo actions → Regenerate from locked data rebuilds the invoice from the lock and reads "identical".
  • An invoiced Booking changes only by a credit note and a new invoice.

The descriptions, kept apart

  • The procedure text is the latest plain text received, exactly as sent. The hospital's text replaces the rooms' text, and the rooms' words stay in the Booking's history. A Booking added by hand keeps what was typed or told.
  • The Contract and the RVG code sit in the Billing section below it.
  • The invoice description starts as the text received. Where the Contract has its own invoice description, the invoice prints that. It is not edited on a Booking screen.
  • The procedure wording in the demo data is illustrative (AR-35), not AA's.

Warnings, never blocks

  • Six warning rules: an unpaid prepayment, base units outside the RVG guide, a child as the payer, an insurer with no approval number, a split that no longer fits the fee, and a patient who owes AA.
  • Mild ones can wait. Strong ones need action before the procedure.
  • They land on the office's To-do list. A triangle marks the Booking in every app, and the warning shows when the Booking opens.
  • Nothing stops completing, submitting or authorising.
  • A patient who owes AA: mild within the threshold, strong over it (90 days from the invoice date, set in Master data → Patient balance warning). The anaesthetist sees "This patient owes AA money. The office is following up.", with no amounts.
  • At review, flags point at what needs a look: "Changed by Dr X: was" the old Contract (Approve or Correct), "Price typed: $X (computed $Y)", "Contract not offered on this List", "Contract disabled on" a day, "Email or postal address needed", Needs a price.
  • Two things hold up authorising: a Procedure with no price, and a Booking reopened and not completed again. Authorise for billing names it, with Open.

The notification pool and the to-do list

  • Admin Day's right rail holds the To-do list and, beside it, Notifications: one pool the whole office shares.
  • Every move by an anaesthetist posts a notice. So does Alert the office, with her note.
  • A notice is active or resolved. When anyone ticks it resolved, it goes for everyone. Show resolved brings it back with who and when. Nothing expires.
  • Nothing in the pool needs approving. The cover-change email is drafted from the notice.

Preferences and tiers, admin only

  • A surgeon can prefer, or prefer not, to work with an anaesthetist. The office sees it on both records.
  • Priority tiers order who the office's pickers offer: tier, then preferred pairing. Not preferred sits apart with a warning, and the office can still go ahead.
  • Anaesthetists never see tiers, pairings or Draft Lists. Their own moves show colleagues by availability alone.
  • Seeded: Ms A. Reid prefers not to work with Dr Sharma; Mr J. Whitford prefers Dr Sharma.

The money model

Payer -> ACCREC -> AA's bank account -> ACCPAY (the same amount) -> Anaesthetist
  • Every invoice is a pair in AA's own ledger: a receivable from whoever pays, and a payable to the anaesthetist for the same amount. AA passes on everything.
  • Each invoice is issued in the anaesthetist's name, with her GST number, and AA as her agent (provisional, OQ-29).
  • Prices are held without GST. GST, at 15 per cent, goes on at the foot of the invoice.
  • Each Party's invoices go by email or by post (OQ-114). The billing run sends them as it raises them.
  • The ACCPAY is the buyer-created tax invoice, one per invoice. The count is provisional (OQ-29, OQ-60).
  • Xero holds no NHI and no patient name. It knows each patient or payer by a hidden ID ("Patient PT0005").
  • Every call to Xero waits in the Xero sync queue and retries by itself. An outage never stops authorising.
  • A part payment releases the anaesthetist's payable for exactly the amount received.
  • Billing fails per Booking. The rest of the List bills.

The weekly payment run

  • Each run is named by its ISO week. The week closes on Friday and is checked against the bank download. Monday is for sorting out anything odd: fix it, or Hold out of this run and it rolls into next week.
  • The office approves the week's BCTIs. Nothing is paid without that. On Tuesday the payment schedule goes to the accountant, who pays it in Xero.
  • The Billing Engine records each payment when Xero reports it. Every payment carries its week, and every run leaves a remittance advice per anaesthetist.
  • A negative invoice from a credit nets against the anaesthetist's next run.
  • The weekly cycle is proposed (OQ-47).

AA's monthly fee

  • AA invoices each anaesthetist once a month: fixed charges plus a charge per BCTI paid that month. The sample: $350.00 + $150.00, and $5.00 per BCTI. Dr Rutherford with 40 BCTIs: $700.00 before GST, $805.00 with it.
  • It is always a separate invoice, paid into AA's own account, and never taken out of a payment to the anaesthetist.
  • Only paid BCTIs count, so a moved Booking is never charged twice. The count is provisional (OQ-29, OQ-60). The demo raises it at month end (OQ-112).

The ledger balances

Receipts held = payables due + the AA Holding account + credits held for payers - negative invoices to net
  • On a fresh reset: Receivables outstanding $12,630.50, Receipts held $1,640.00, Payables due $0.00, Disbursed $27,760.00.
  • A receipt nobody can match puts it out of balance, with a name on it. The office allocates it to an invoice or marks it refunded.
  • The anaesthetist sees her position on web Accounts, the web Dashboard and mobile Balances, from the same ledger.

Credit notes

  • An issued invoice is never edited. A credit note always covers the whole invoice.
  • Credit in full and rebill starts the new invoice from a copy of the original's lines. Split into additional invoices must add up to what was credited.
  • If the anaesthetist was already paid, a negative invoice reverses her payable and nets in her next run.
  • Money paid on a credited invoice is held for the payer. The credit sheet says "A credit note is not a refund."

Prepayment

  • The office sets Patient › Prepaid and picks the anaesthetist's own prepaid Contract, from the price list the office keeps for her. The prepaid amount is her fixed price, in full.
  • A prepaid Contract bills only a person paying, never an organisation (awaiting AA's confirmation, OQ-73). Each payer gets their own prepayment invoice.
  • The invoice waits for an admin to approve it. AA can switch that step off. Approving sends it with a letter.
  • The money is held in the AA Holding account. When the office authorises the List, a transfer moves it to whoever did the List: no invoice and no GST (provisional, OQ-125).
  • The prepaid Procedure stays off the final invoice. Nothing is deducted, and nothing more is invoiced or credited automatically. A difference is an additional invoice or a credit note, raised by hand.
  • An unpaid prepayment is a warning that turns strong two days before the procedure. It never blocks.
  • Cancelling settles it at once: an unpaid invoice is credited, so nobody chases the patient; a paid one is credited and refunded from the AA Holding account. Moving the Procedure off a prepaid Contract after sending is settled the same way.
  • A move changes nothing. Whoever does the procedure is paid the prepaid amount.
  • Rebook with another anaesthetist starts a fresh prepayment at the new anaesthetist's own price ($1,350.00 for Dr Beaumont), so the patient may see two amounts.

Events

  • Events sit on the Procedure, on no List: pre-op and post-op events (a time or a fixed fee, with their own date), billing events (a free-text charge or credit), additional invoices and credit notes.
  • The office or the anaesthetist raises them. Each goes through one review step, Review queue → Events, and bills in the next billing run.
  • An event joins an invoice to the same party in that run where it can, otherwise it gets its own.
  • ACC pre-op assessments are preconfigured pre-op events with codes CS250, CS260 and CS70 (to confirm, OQ-12).

Likely evaluator questions

"What if the office never confirms an anaesthetist's move?"

It doesn't need to. The move happens at once, and nobody accepts it. The office sees a notice in Notifications and resolves it when it has done what it needs, such as the cover-change email.

"Who finds out when an anaesthetist moves a List?"

The whole office, through the shared notification pool on Admin. A colleague who receives the List sees it on their schedule straight away. The office drafts the cover-change email to the hospital from the notice.

"Can I move just one Booking?"

Yes. The anaesthetist picks a colleague free in that session, by name or from the list. The Booking becomes a new List in their session, with its Contracts, and the colleague is paid for it. Telling the office is her choice.

"Can a warning stop me?"

No. Warnings sit on the To-do list and on the Booking. Completing, submitting and authorising all go through. Only a Procedure with no price, or a Booking reopened and not completed again, holds up authorising.

"Can I copy a Booking?"

No. Each Booking is added on its own: from a hospital file, from the rooms' PDF, or by hand with Add Booking.

"Who decides which Contract applies?"

The office, when it sets up the Booking, through the step rail. The anaesthetist can change it until the office authorises the List. Her change is flagged at review, and the office approves or corrects it.

"Can the anaesthetist change the price?"

Only on an RVG Contract that allows it, such as No contract · RVG, or by typing the amount on No contract · Fixed price. A hospital's or funder's fixed price is read only. To change it she changes the Contract or alerts the office.

"What if the procedure turns out bigger than the prepaid one?"

Nothing happens automatically. The prepaid amount is the price. If more is owed, the anaesthetist or the office raises an additional invoice by hand.

"Does the system notice when a prepaid Booking moves?"

There is nothing to notice. A move changes nothing in the ledger. When the office authorises the List, the transfer pays whoever did it.

"Can an anaesthetist see who they are not preferred with?"

No. Pairing preferences and tiers are admin only.

"Who uses mobile and who uses web?"

The same anaesthetist. Mobile is for the day in theatre. Web gives a wider view for planning and accounts.

"Who sets up the sessions?"

The system. Every anaesthetist has an AM and a PM session each day, four months ahead. Her own availability goes on first, then recurring bookings fill the sessions. A recurring booking that lands on an unavailable session becomes a Draft List.

"What about cases after six?"

The office opens an Out-of-hours session after PM, even the same evening, and puts a List in it. It works like any session.

"What about public work?"

Public is a status on the session, with an optional note such as CPH. Nobody books into it or covers it, and it is never billed. Setting it back to Free reopens it.

"Can an anaesthetist edit after submitting?"

Yes, until the office authorises the List. Every change is audited, the last change stands, and Review shows "Changed since submitted".

"What happens when the office finds a mistake?"

The office phones the anaesthetist and corrects it on Review. There is no returned state.

"Why isn't Xero doing all the billing?"

AA's rules need pricing per Procedure, invoices per payer and views for each anaesthetist. The Billing Engine does that. Xero holds receivables and banking.

"Is the anaesthetist paid before the procedure?"

No. The prepaid amount waits in the AA Holding account until the office authorises the List. A transfer then moves it to the anaesthetist who did it, and the next weekly run pays her (the trigger is provisional, OQ-125).

"How do late hospital changes work?"

A later row from the hospital lands on Matching, matched to its Booking by date, surgeon, hospital and session. It waits for the office to approve it, field by field, up to AUTHORISED. Nothing applies itself, and the hospital never sets the Contract.

"What if the patient has no NHI?"

The NHI is mandatory. A row with no NHI waits on Matching, and a stub holds its place on the List so the anaesthetist still sees the case. When the office adds the NHI, the Booking takes the stub's place.

"Who do the update emails go to?"

To the List's sender when it came from a rooms' PDF, else the surgeon's first office contact with an email. A cover change goes to the hospital. The office drafts the email on demand and sends it from its own mail client.

"Is this real patient or integration data?"

No. Point at the Synthetic data only badge. The hospital files and PDF lists are samples. Xero, email, the NHI Hub and payments are simulated in the browser.

"Can it handle 85 anaesthetists and 28,000 invoices a year?"

Show it: Admin → Day view → Demo actions → Load full-scale data → Confirm. It builds 85 anaesthetists, 21,080 sessions and 28,279 invoices in the browser. The banner's Timings shows each screen's render time. These are timings on one laptop, with no server; formal performance testing is a Testing-milestone task. Reset returns to the demo data.

"How will people who are not comfortable with new systems cope?"

One clear action on each screen, and each procedure shown the same way in every app. The info sign beside each heading explains the section. Training runs per user group before go-live.

"How do anaesthetists sign in?"

With an account on the phone app. The screen is a stand-in, marked Provisional: how they sign in is still to be decided (OQ-83). Every sign-in, refusal and sign-out is in the audit.

"What if something is not working?"

Help in any app: the help line's number, its hours and a request form, each request tracked to its respond-by time. AA also has Product support for incidents, fixes, changes and new features. Both open 8:30am to 5:00pm, Monday to Friday, outside New Zealand public holidays. Whether AA needs cover from 8:00am is open (OQ-141). After a Reset the clock reads 8:00am, so press +1 hour to show it open.
