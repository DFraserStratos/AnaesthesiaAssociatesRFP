Greg described the generation run: write an AM and a PM Slot as free for every active anaesthetist, then paint the anaesthetist's calendar (holidays, unavailable), then the surgeons' recurring bookings, "or vice versa, depending on your priority of who wins". "If the anaesthetist is away... you probably want to put the surgeon's booking in and flag it as a conflict." Not settled: (1) the order; (2) whether a recurring booking that lands on an unavailable Slot is flagged as a conflict on the anaesthetist's List, or goes to the office as a Draft List, as happens when the anaesthetist marks a booked Slot unavailable (OQ-64); (3) whether a short-notice sickness marked by the anaesthetist takes that same return-or-assign path, or is recorded by the office as a conflict with the Bookings left in place, as the 2026-09-29 meeting said.

Recommendation: paint the anaesthetist's calendar first; a recurring booking that lands on an unavailable Slot is created and flagged as a conflict for the office to resolve; a sickness marked by the anaesthetist takes the same return-or-assign path as any other unavailability.

## Meeting update

Discussed with Greg in the 2026-10-02 afternoon review but not answered on the board, and part 3 is not settled, so still Open. Part 1, the order: when the day rolls over the system creates the blank Slots, then applies the anaesthetist's availability (the block-outs) first, then the surgeons' recurring bookings. Greg: "Yes." Part 2: a recurring booking that lands on an unavailable Slot becomes a [Draft List](US-01.6.1) for the office to resolve, not a List flagged as a conflict on the anaesthetist. Donald: "the system should automatically create that list as a draft rather than assigning it to an anaesthetist"; Greg: "So do I... And it should blank it." This reverses the recommendation above and Greg's morning view. Part 3, a short-notice sickness marked by the anaesthetist, was not discussed.
