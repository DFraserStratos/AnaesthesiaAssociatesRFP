Contract prices carry an [effective-from date](US-04.2.10), and hospitals and funders usually give an effective date, sometimes with a few weeks' notice. When a Procedure is priced, which date picks the price: the date of the procedure, the date the Booking was made, or the date the invoice is raised?

Recommendation: the date of the procedure, because that is when the service was given; the Contract version used is then locked at AUTHORISED as already agreed (US-04.3.5).

Meeting 2026-10-01: Greg: "I'd go with the procedure" date. A price change between booking and procedure then only affects prepayments. To propose to AA.

## Answer

The date of the procedure. At the 7 October client meeting, Donald: "the price used for the invoice is the price effective on the date the procedure actually takes place, correct?" The room: "Correct." Donald: "Okay, so that's question 48." Greg, the same morning: "that's settled. It's the date of the procedure", the same idea as the patient's age on the day.

Greg and Donald also agreed that Contract pricing must carry an effective date; whether the Contract is dated and its lines move with it (Greg's view) or the lines carry their own dates is left to Greg's model. The guide has a price review create a new dated version of the contract, with bookings priced under the old version keeping the old price; the draft technical design has the resolver use the procedure date and reject a contract not valid on it.
