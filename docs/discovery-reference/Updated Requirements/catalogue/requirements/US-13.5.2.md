Admins can see who, or what, made every change in the system, and when:

- creates
- updates
- [reassignments](US-01.4.1)
- [authorisations](US-07.3.1)
- invoices
- payments
- disbursements

This gives a complete audit trail across both manual actions and automated ones, so any change can be traced back to its source.

## Technical discussion

Proposed in our RFP response: the change history uses Azure SQL system-versioned (temporal) tables at the data layer, recording who changed what, and when, on key scheduling and billing data. This gives a full history rather than a single last-modified stamp.

- Because the database records history automatically, no write path can skip or bypass it.
- Point-in-time reconstruction (what a Booking looked like at a given instant) is a direct query rather than a log replay, supporting [invoice reproducibility](US-08.4.4) across roughly 28,000 invoices a year.
- Alternatives considered: trigger-based audit tables were rejected as more bespoke and weaker at point-in-time reconstruction. A domain-event log may still be used where the reason for a change matters, but it is not the primary history mechanism.
