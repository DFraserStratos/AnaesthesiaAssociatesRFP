Admins see every availability conflict across the schedule in one coloured view, instead of running a report and printing, annotating and reprinting it.

A List whose anaesthetist has said they are unavailable does not stay with them as a conflict, so it is definitely resolved. It goes back to the office as a [Draft List](FT-01.6), worked from the [Draft List view](US-01.6.2):

- when the anaesthetist marks a booked Slot unavailable and returns the List to the office, rather than assigning it to a colleague ([return or assign](US-01.5.5))
- when a surgeon's recurring booking lands on a Slot already marked unavailable ([conflict flagging](US-01.5.2))

The dashboard shows the conflicts that remain: a booked List whose hospital is closed, or a Booking landing on a List whose anaesthetist is unavailable. Each conflict is coloured and shows the reason, the anaesthetist and the hospital.

From a conflict, an admin can reassign the List to cover it ([reassign a List](US-01.4.1)). The conflict clears once the List is covered or the clash is removed.

This is because the office has to run a report today to find which surgeon needs covering and which anaesthetist is away, and wants to see it at a glance ("a dashboard with colour").

## Acceptance criteria

- Given a booked List whose hospital is closed, or a Booking landing on a Slot its anaesthetist has marked unavailable, when an admin opens the dashboard, then the List appears as a conflict and its Bookings are still in place.
- Given a conflict, when the List is reassigned to an available anaesthetist, then the conflict clears.
- Given a recurring booking that lands on a Slot marked unavailable, then it shows as a Draft List, not as a conflict on the anaesthetist.

## Notes

Whether a short-notice sickness shows here as a conflict, or, when the anaesthetist marks it, follows the return-or-assign path to a Draft List, is not settled yet (OQ-81).
