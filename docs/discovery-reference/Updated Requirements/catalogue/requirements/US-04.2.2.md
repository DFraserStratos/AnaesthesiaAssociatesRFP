Admins need to be able to create and manage Contracts. Each Contract is made up of a few settings that together define how a Procedure using it gets priced.

A Contract sets its pricing basis: RVG units multiplied by [the anaesthetist's own unit value](US-12.1.1) by default, RVG units multiplied by an agreed contract rate or discount percentage, [a fixed price taken from a fee schedule](US-04.2.4), or [a Contract defined rate](US-05.2.6), a unit rate the Contract specifies.

Base units live in a [default RVG Contract](US-04.4.2) of the procedure. Any other Contract may override them for a procedure, RVG code or group, for the cases where it departs from the standard NZSA value.

The Contract also sets whether the anaesthetist [may adjust the price afterwards](US-05.4.1), as a percentage discount or a fixed final price. Where this is not permitted, the app does not offer the field at all.

This is so that every Procedure that selects a given Contract is billed the same way.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Pricing basis.** The Contract sets exactly one of: RVG units x the anaesthetist's own unit value (default); RVG units x a contract rate per unit, or a percentage discount; a fixed fee schedule (lines per US-04.2.4); or a Contract defined unit rate.
- **Base units.** Base units come from a [default RVG Contract](US-04.4.2) of the procedure. Any other Contract may override them for a procedure, RVG code or group where it departs from the NZSA value.
- **Rate.** For the contract-rate basis, the Contract holds either the $ per unit or the discount percentage.
- **Fixed price.** For the fixed fee schedule basis, the price comes from the schedule line, independent of standard rates.
- **Anaesthetist adjustment.** The Contract sets whether the anaesthetist may apply a percentage discount or a fixed final price. When not permitted, the app does not offer the field.

## Technical discussion

Proposed in our RFP response: insurer terms tend to be schedule-based (a maximum payable per procedure) rather than a discount applied to a counterparty, so the question is the shape of the rate lookup. Stratos leans toward extending how the Contract model resolves rates rather than building a parallel structure alongside it. AA to advise how likely direct insurer billing is to grow (see OQ-20).
