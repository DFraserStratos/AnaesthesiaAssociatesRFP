AA runs a rolling four-month schedule. Every active anaesthetist has an AM Slot and a PM Slot on every day of that window, whether or not anything is booked. A Slot is the place a List can exist, and it can be empty. Every new Slot starts as free, and a Slot becomes a List only when something is put in it.

A List is assigned to an anaesthetist, sits in one of their Slots and holds Bookings. Once a List is assigned, it belongs to exactly one surgeon and one hospital. A Draft List is a List created with no anaesthetist: its surgeon, hospital, day and session are known, only the anaesthetist is missing.

A day holds Slots or Lists, a List holds Bookings, a Booking holds Procedures, each Procedure has a Contract, and the Contract may be split.

Each Slot carries its own availability. This keeps the calendar complete and gapless, whatever is or isn't booked yet.

The nesting above and a Slot becoming a List are working readings: Greg accepts the model "in principle, not in detail", and the logical model of days, Slots and Lists is still to be made clear (OQ-64).

## Technical discussion

Proposed in our RFP response: adopt AA's candidate architecture (the schedule canvas grid, the List/Booking hierarchy, half-day availability and permanent-list templates) as the starting point, with the Schedule Management candidate architecture verified during discovery.

- Reference data by identity: hospitals, surgeons, statuses, rates and the like are referenced into the schedule, not copied into it, so a change is made once and reflected everywhere.
- The single proposed exception sits in the billing model, where reference data is [frozen at authorisation](US-07.3.1); exactly which fields is determined in discovery.
