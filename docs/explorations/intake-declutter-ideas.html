Intake, with the noise taken out

The Arrivals screen works, but it says everything at once. Every row and every header carries three or four pills, the sources strip repeats the Sources tab, the workbench is grey on grey, and on a new List the button that finishes the job sits two screens down, under a list of every free anaesthetist in the practice.

Below are five ways to fix it, each drawn at 1440 × 900 with the same morning's data. All five drop the sources strip, swap the filter pills for drop-downs, put the List's name at the top of the workbench and show no sender addresses. They differ in how the feed reads and where the decision lives.

What needs fixing

  • Sources strip"Southern Cross, receiving" and friends at top right. The Sources tab already says it. Remove.
  • Filter pillsSeven pill buttons in three rows. Replace with ordinary drop-downs.
  • Pills on every row"Rooms PDF", "Needs NHI 1", "Values to check 1", the kind. Keep the kind if it earns it, drop the rest.
  • Workbench headerTwo pills and a received time above the name, a third pill below, then a sender address. Name first, details quietly after.
  • Grey on greyA grey workbench holding grey panels. The working surface should be white.
  • Below the foldPlace this List lists every free anaesthetist, so Place and Reject end up two screens down.

1Tidy in place

Keep the screen you have and take things away. The feed rows become two plain lines, the kind is a coloured word, the workbench turns white, and the choice of where a List goes shrinks to four options with drop-downs inside them. A bar pinned to the bottom of the workbench always holds Reject and Place.

Smallest changeSame layout, same benches

What changes

  • Three drop-downs under the search: what to show (To decide, Needs an NHI, Decided), which source, which kind.
  • Rows: the List on line one, then "New List · 4 patients · 1 has no NHI". Only something that needs the office is coloured.
  • "Place this List" offers the named anaesthetist, another anaesthetist (a drop-down of who is free), an existing List (a drop-down) or a Draft List. No more 25-row radio list.
  • Place and Reject pin to the bottom of the workbench, so they never scroll away.

What it gives up

  • The PDF stays small beside the values (about the width of a phone) unless you open it full size.
  • The drop-down hides who else is free until you open it. Fine when the suggestion is usually right.

2Decision rail

Three columns: the feed, the List in the middle, and a rail on the right that holds only the decision. The feed groups by kind, so the kind is said once in a heading rather than on every row. The rail's button sits at its foot and never moves.

Medium changeNew rail per bench

What changes

  • The feed is grouped under New Lists, Updates, Cancellations, Can't be read and Waiting for an NHI. Rows lose every pill.
  • The middle column is the List: patients first, the PDF underneath at a readable size.
  • The rail lists the suggestion, then who is free that afternoon, then Lists that could take it, with a search box over all of them. It scrolls on its own; the button doesn't.
  • Every kind gets the same rail: an update's rail says Apply changes, a cancellation's says Cancel the Booking.

What it gives up

  • Three columns are tight at 1440. The PDF and the patients stack rather than sit side by side.
  • Every bench needs splitting into "what came" and "what to do", which is most of the build.

3Inbox, then open

Stop splitting the screen. Arrivals is a full-width table, like a mail inbox, with drop-downs above its columns. Opening one gives the whole width to that List: the PDF large on the left, the patients and the decision on the right, and a top bar with Place, Reject and next and previous.

Larger changeTwo screens instead of one

The inbox. Sortable columns say the kind and what needs attention without a pill in sight.

One arrival, opened. J and K still step through; Esc goes back to the inbox with your place kept.

What changes

  • The table has room for real columns: received, the List, kind, from, patients, and a "Needs" column that is blank when nothing does.
  • Opened, the PDF is close to A4 size beside the patients, which is the comparison the office is actually making.
  • Place and Reject live in the top bar with next and previous, so deciding and moving on is one row of controls.

What it gives up

  • You lose sight of the feed while deciding. Next and previous and the count ("3 of 18") stand in for it.
  • It walks away from the feed-and-workbench idea agreed in the last round.

4Action in the header

The workbench header becomes the control bar: the List's name on the left, the main action on the right as a split button whose menu holds every other place the List could go. Below it, tabs separate the patients from the PDF so neither has to shrink.

Medium changeHeader and tabs, benches slimmed

What changes

  • One filter drop-down in the feed holds show, source and kind together, with counts. The kind on a row is a thin coloured edge plus the word.
  • "Place in Dr Delaney's PM" is one click when the suggestion is right. The arrow opens the rest: free anaesthetists, Lists, a Draft List, and Reject at the foot.
  • Patients and PDF are tabs; the Patients tab keeps a small page beside the table so a click on a value still shows it on the page.

What it gives up

  • Choosing someone other than the suggestion is hidden in a menu. Quick for the common case, one step slower for the rest.
  • A split button is a new pattern for the shared Button; it needs a design source before it ships.

5Check, then place

Treat a new List as the two jobs it is. First check what was read against the page, then place it. Each step gets the whole workbench, and the footer always says what happens next. The feed sorts by what needs the office rather than by kind, with one-line rows.

Medium changeNew List and Update benches

Step one, check. The PDF at a readable size beside the values; anything unsure is highlighted on both.

Step two, place. The choices get the whole width, laid out in columns, so 25 free anaesthetists fit without scrolling.

What changes

  • Feed groups: Something to check, Ready to apply, Waiting for an NHI. One line per arrival; a dot marks the ones that need a look.
  • Step one shows the PDF large and the values beside it. Step two shows every place it could go in three columns.
  • An exact match or a plain cancellation skips straight to a one-step bench.

What it gives up

  • An extra click on every new List, even when nothing needs checking. "Place now" on step one could cover that.
  • One-line rows show less: the patient count and the change summary move to the workbench.

Recommendation: start with 1, borrow the header from 4

Idea 1 fixes every complaint on the list with the least rework: the pills go, the workbench turns white, the name comes first, and the pinned bar ends the scrolling. Add idea 4's tabs (Patients and PDF) to it and the PDF gets its full size without leaving the bench, which is idea 1's one weak point.

Keep 2 in mind if the drop-down for "another anaesthetist" turns out to be used often; the rail is the version that shows those choices without a click. I'd set 3 aside: it reads well but gives up the feed beside the work, which was the reason Idea 1 won last time.
