Intake as the front door Every List and every change comes in here

Intake today is five tabs that were added at different times: a table of hospital rows, a copy of some of them as "Waiting for NHI", a PDF inbox that sits in front of Matching, and two tabs (Data quality, Validators) that no requirement asks for. It reads as a pile of tools, not as the place the office trusts to tell them what came in and what it did to the schedule.

This page sets out what the requirements, AA's own words and the code say, then gives three ideas for one screen: a feed of every arrival on the left, and on the right a workbench where each one is either placed as a new List or merged into a List we already hold. All three assume the integrations simply arrive: no import button, nothing ever written back.

1What we found

Four readers went through the catalogue (about 45 stories, AR-21, 15 questions), every AA transcript that mentions lists, PDFs or hospitals, and the Intake code. The short version, in three voices:

The catalogue says

One matching screen for every source, nothing applied without a person

  • Hospital rows and rooms' PDFs "each land in the same matching screen" EP-02 · OQ-108
  • Match on surgeon, hospital, day and session, then patient NHI; show field differences; approve or reject AR-21 · US-02.1.2 · US-02.1.3
  • No match: existing List, new List in a session, Draft List, or reject US-02.1.2

The app does today

Rows, not Lists, behind a modal

  • Matching is a table of single rows; deciding opens a modal 440px drawer that hides the queue
  • "Waiting for NHI" is the same rows as Matching's Unmatched filter, with a second UI and a second Add NHI path
  • PDF lists is a staging inbox in front of Matching; the anaesthetist the PDF names is never used to place it
  • A hospital update to a List that came from a PDF matches, but nothing shows it is the same List's next version
  • "Import hospital file" and "Upload PDF list" are the only ways anything arrives
  • Data quality and Validators have no story behind them
1The thing that arrives is a List, not a row

Rooms send whole Lists. Hospitals send tomorrow's theatre Lists. The office thinks "Mr Okafor's Wednesday". The screen should group by List and decide a List at a time, with per-Booking overrides.

2One List has a life across sources

Rooms PDF weeks ahead, re-sent as it changes, then the hospital the day before with fuller wording, the Contract and often the missing NHI. Showing that lifeline is what builds trust.

3Confidence comes from showing the why

No story defines confidence. Rather than a score, show each match key ticked, what will change, what stays, and where it lands. "Nothing changes until you apply" stays true.

4Waiting for an NHI is a state, not a place

It is an arrival that cannot finish yet. It belongs in the same feed with a filter, and the hospital's day-before list resolving it should be a visible, satisfying moment.

5Arrivals should just arrive

No import or upload button. A mailbox reader and the hospital feeds bring things in; a quiet strip says each source is healthy. Demo actions stand in for the outside world.

6Not every PDF reads cleanly

5 of the 19 real room PDFs are image scans with no text. Fields the reader is unsure of must be marked, and an unreadable PDF must land in the feed, not vanish.

4Principles every idea keeps

Decide a List, override a Booking

The default action applies the whole arrival. Each Booking has its own tick, so one bad row never blocks the rest.

Show the why next to the what

Match keys ticked or not, the List's earlier versions, and the session it lands in, on screen before the button.

Nothing changes until you apply

First release rule. "Matches exactly" Lists are the one thing worth confirming in bulk, because they change nothing.

Never lose anything

Unreadable PDFs, rows with no NHI and feed messages that failed all land in the feed with a reason. Nothing is silently dropped.

Docked, not modal

The decision lives in the page beside the feed, like the Day view's rail, so the office keeps their place and moves to the next one with a key press.

Inbound only, and say so

AA reads from hospitals and rooms and never writes back. The Sources strip states it. Emails to the rooms stay drafts the office sends.

For a new PDF it shows the page beside what was read from it, and where to place it.

Why it works

  • Closest to AR-21 as drawn: rows wait, match proposed, differences reviewed, applied.
  • One place for every kind of arrival; the source is a mark, not a tab.
  • The basis strip and lifeline answer "why does it think that?" without a score.
  • Docked workbench keeps the queue in view; J and K move through it.

Watch out for

  • The workbench has to handle six shapes (update, new, cancel, needs NHI, can't read, matches exactly). Each needs a designed state.
  • Grouping by arrival can bury a single urgent List inside a big hospital file; the "To decide" filter flattens it.
  • Less sense of the wider schedule than Idea 2.

Catalogue: fits US-02.1.2, 02.1.3, 02.2.1, 02.2.4, 02.5.7. US-11.1.5's "own tab on Intake" becomes "a filter on Intake".

Needs a ruling: no import or upload button means OQ-109 ("no auto syncing on the first release") is re-answered and US-02.1.5 leaves Future Work; the mailbox reader (US-02.2.1 Notes) becomes the story; US-02.2.3 removal detection comes into the first release; using the PDF's named anaesthetist to suggest the session is new.

Schedule lens

Intake drawn like the Day view. A narrow feed on the left; in the middle the anaesthetist grid for the day of the selected arrival, with a 14-day strip above it whose dots show what is waiting on each day. Every waiting arrival is drawn on the session it would land in: a dashed green outline for a new List, an amber ring for an update, a red ring for a cancellation. Clicking one opens the decision in a docked panel on the right. The office sees, before they decide, exactly whose day it changes and whether it clashes.

Why it works

  • The "custom view like the Day view" you described: the office already reads this grid every morning.
  • Placing a new List is pointing at a session, and clashes (unavailable, already booked) are visible before deciding.
  • The day strip answers "how much is coming at me this fortnight?".

Watch out for

  • Three panes are tight below 1600px; the grid has to shrink to just the anaesthetists touched.
  • Arrivals with no session to sit on (can't read, needs NHI with no List) have nowhere on the grid.
  • Risk of a second Day view drifting from the first. It must reuse DayGrid, not copy it.

Catalogue: same fit and rulings as Idea 1. The grid also serves US-01.3.3 (assign a List to a free session) and US-01.6.1 (Draft List) at the moment of intake, which settles US-01.6.1's open note on Draft Lists from intake.

List threads

Treat each List like an email conversation. The left side lists threads, one per List, newest activity first, each with its versions as small marks (PDF, PDF, SG) and the waiting one ringed. Every value carries a small tag saying which version it came from, so "where did 10:45 come from?" never needs the audit log.

Why it works

  • Answers Ben's "which version is the latest?" directly; the List, not the email, is the source of truth.
  • Removals are natural: a Booking in the last version and not this one shows as missing.
  • The strongest sense of confidence and history; the change history is on the page, not behind a button.

Watch out for

  • A version only makes sense once the system knows two arrivals are the same List; the first arrival of a hospital file has to split into Lists first.
  • Provenance tags on every cell are dense; they may want to show on hover.
  • Single-Booking feed messages (an HL7 cancel) fit less naturally as a "version".

Catalogue: builds on US-02.2.4 (a List keeps where it came from) and US-02.5.8 (change history). New: a List's versions as a first-class idea, and showing the source of each value. Also needs the same rulings as Idea 1.

Recommendation: build Idea 1, borrow the best of 2 and 3

Idea 1's feed and docked workbench is the right shape for a screen the office works through every morning. Take two things from the others:

  1. From Idea 3, the lifeline. The small track of earlier versions in the workbench header, and a removed Booking shown struck through. It is the cheapest way to show "same List, next version" and to make Vanessa's scenario visible.
  2. From Idea 2, "where it lands". A small session card in the workbench and, for a new List, the session picker reading the anaesthetists free that day. The full grid lens can come later if the office wants it.
  3. Group the feed by List, show the source as a mark. A hospital file is a header over its Lists; a PDF is one List. Filters, not tabs, for Needs NHI, Rooms PDFs, each hospital, Decided.
