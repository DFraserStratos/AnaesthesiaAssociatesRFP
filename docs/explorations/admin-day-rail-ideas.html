Admin Web App, Day view. Brainstorm for the office, 25 November 2026.

Nine cards on the rail.
Four would do.

The Day view's right rail stacks nine cards under the calendar. Most of them answer one question, "what does the office need to do?", each in its own shape and its own words. Below are eight ways to calm it down, from a quick tidy to a second dashboard, and what each would change in the catalogue.

The side nav already repeats most of these as badges: Draft Lists, Conflicts, Notifications, Review queue, Billing monitor, Ledger. So the office sees the same counts in two places that don't line up with each other.

Decided: Draft Lists and Awaiting review come off the rail in every idea below. Each has its own page in the nav, and the day's Draft Lists already sit in the band above the grid, where they get sorted. That leaves seven cards, and the ideas below work on those.

The pattern underneath

Strip away the headings and there are only four kinds of thing on the rail.

This day

Belongs to the date on screen: calendar, day notes, the day's Draft Lists band.

Needs action

Somebody has to do something before it goes away: warnings, missing Contract, pre-payments to approve. (Draft Lists and Lists to review also need action, but they live on their own pages.)

Worth knowing

Something changed. No action needed, but someone ticks it once they've seen it: the notification pool.

Are we OK?

A health reading, not a list: books square, pre-payment money outstanding.

The catalogue already leans this way. US-13.7.2 says the to-do list is there "so the office has one place to see what needs doing", and Greg called the dashboard "effectively the to-do list". The one hard line is that notices are kept off the to-do list (FT-13.7 note; US-13.8.1 "Not a to-do"). They can sit next to it, in a tab, but not be mixed into it.

One set of words, whatever the layout

Most of this works with any of the eight ideas. The aim: one verb per kind, headings named after the job rather than the data, and the same row shape everywhere (what, then who · when, then one action and maybe a tick).

Eight ideas

Mockups are rough sketches of the Day view: side nav, day grid, rail. The numbers are made up. Tags: fits as written story wording edit needs AA to agree.

1

One work card with tabs

Merge To-do, No Contract yet and Notices into a single card with two tabs, To-do and Notices. Missing-Contract rows join the To-do tab as a row type. Reconciliation and pre-payments shrink to two status tiles above it. The rail goes from nine cards to four: calendar, tiles, work card, day notes.

Recommended coreEffort: Mstory wording edit

Why it works

  • Exactly what you described: the "things to deal with" live in one place, each tab with its count, so it's obvious where to look.
  • Notices stay on their own tab, so the "Not a to-do" rule holds.
  • Every tab uses the same row shape and the same tick.

Watch out for

  • A tab hides its contents. Both tabs show their counts, so an active notice is still visible from the To-do tab.
  • Pre-payments awaiting approval need a home: a To-do row, or the tile's link to Invoices.

Catalogue: US-13.8.1 says the pool is a panel "beside the to-do list". A tab next to it counts, but reword to "alongside". US-01.6.2 is met by the band above the grid (see the recommendation). US-13.2.4 "at a glance": the tile covers it.

2

Do / Know / Watch

Keep separate cards but put them under three labelled groups, so the rail reads as three things, not nine. Do is one merged list with a small chip for the kind. Know is the notices. Watch is two health tiles. Day notes move up under the calendar, with the other things that belong to the day.

Effort: Mfits as written

Why it works

  • Teaches the four kinds without saying so. A new office hire learns the screen in a minute.
  • Do and Know stay physically apart, so the catalogue's split is easy to see.
  • Nothing is hidden behind a tab.

Watch out for

  • Still a tall rail when every list is busy. Needs a row cap per group (5 rows, then See all).
  • A merged Do list mixes warning rows (tick) with work rows (no tick), so the row shape has to make that obvious.

Catalogue: fits as written. The pool is still a panel beside the to-do list, and reconciliation is still a status.

3

One feed with filter chips

The boldest merge. Everything that wants the office's attention, notices included, goes in one list sorted by when it matters (the Booking's date, or newest for notices). Filter chips across the top narrow it down. It works like an email inbox: one place, one tick.

Effort: Mneeds AA to agree

Why it works

  • The simplest idea to explain: "this is your inbox".
  • Greg's "I need a way to make them go away, whether they're to-dos or notifications" fits it well: one tick for everything.
  • Sorting by when it matters puts tomorrow's problem above next month's.

Watch out for

  • It directly undoes a settled ruling: notices were split off the to-do list on purpose ("this is more like a notification").
  • Notices have no Booking date, so the sort order is a compromise.

Catalogue: FT-13.7's note and US-13.8.1's "Not a to-do" criterion would need rewriting, and that's AA's call, not ours. The default filter could be "Needs action", with notices one chip away, to soften it.

4

Status ribbon above the grid, notices behind a bell

Take the counts and health readings out of the rail and put them in a slim row of chips across the top of the day grid: Books square · 3 pre-payments · 2 no Contract. Each chip opens its list in a side panel. Notices move to a bell in the top bar, like every other app. The rail keeps only the calendar, the To-do and day notes.

Effort: Mstory wording edit

Why it works

  • The whole picture fits in one line you can scan in a second, and it's always above the fold.
  • The bell is a pattern everyone already knows for "things that happened".
  • Gives the To-do the full height of the rail, which it will need at the scale of AA's whole practice.

Watch out for

  • Another strip above the grid pushes the grid down. It has to be a single line.
  • A notice behind a bell is easier to ignore, and the catalogue says notices should stay in front of the office until someone has dealt with them.

Catalogue: US-13.8.1 says the pool is "a panel on the dashboard". A bell panel is arguable, but needs a reword and probably a word with Greg. Everything else fits.

5

This day vs all dates

The rail sits next to a single day, so let it default to that day. A switch at the top flips between This day (warnings and missing Contracts on that day's Bookings, and its day notes) and All dates (the full backlog). Change the date and the rail changes with it.

Effort: S to Mstory wording edit

Why it works

  • Fixes the mismatch where the rail looks like it's about the day on screen but mostly isn't.
  • "This day" lists are short, so the rail stops scrolling.
  • Makes the rail useful for planning ahead: jump to next Tuesday and see what's wrong with next Tuesday.

Watch out for

  • On "This day", a problem tomorrow is out of sight. The All dates count must stay visible on the switch.
  • Notices and Books square aren't tied to a day, so they sit outside the switch.

Catalogue: US-13.7.2 asks for "every open warning, all dates". That still holds under All dates, but if This day is the default, say so in the story.

A second screen: the Office desk

A full-width dashboard that becomes the admin landing page, with room for everything: Bookings, Money and Notices, one column each. The Day view goes back to being about the day: calendar, today's To-do, day notes, and one dark card that says "Office desk · 9 to do" and takes you there.

Effort: Lstory wording edit

Why it works

  • Room to breathe. Reconciliation can show its checks properly, and pre-payments can show amounts and reminders.
  • Morning routine: open the desk, clear it, then plan in the Day view.
  • The side-nav badges could fold into the desk, which shortens the nav too.

Watch out for

  • One more place to check. The Day view loses its "you can't miss it" quality.
  • The most work, and with Draft Lists and reviews on their own pages there is less left for it to gather. It may not earn a screen of its own.

Catalogue: "the admin dashboard" in US-13.7.2, US-13.2.4 and US-13.8.1 would mean the Office desk, and US-13.1.1 stays the Day view.

Fold and hide: the quick tidy

Change nothing about what the cards are. Each one folds to a single line with its count. Only one is open at a time (the most urgent, by default), and a card with nothing in it disappears. Books square becomes a thin green line. A quiet day shows three lines, not nine cards.

Effort: Sfits as written

Why it works

  • A day's work, no catalogue edits, no story changes, and you can try it in the next workshop.
  • Hiding empty cards alone cuts most of the noise on a normal day.
  • Works on top of any of the other ideas.

Watch out for

  • Doesn't fix the words or the "nine kinds of the same thing" problem. It just hides it better.
  • A card that vanishes when empty can make people wonder where it went. Keep an "All clear" line for To-do.

Catalogue: fits as written. Each story's panel is still on the dashboard. It's just folded.

8

The Booking journey

Arrange the work by where it sits in a Booking's life: Plan (conflicts), Set up (no Contract, pre-payments), Bill (billing warnings), Reconcile (books square). Four stages with a count each. Pick a stage and its list opens below. Notices sit apart.

Effort: Mfits as written

Why it works

  • Tells the workshop the system's story in one glance: this is where every Booking goes, and this is where it's stuck.
  • Shows bottlenecks: a pile-up in Set up means Contracts are behind.
  • The words come from the process, so they're easy to guess.

Watch out for

  • Warnings don't fit one stage neatly: a pre-payment warning is Set up, base units is Bill. Each rule needs a stage.
  • Prettier than it is faster. The office clears things by urgency, not by stage.

Catalogue: fits as written. Each warning rule (domain/warnings/rules/) would get a stage, which is a design choice rather than a requirement.

What I'd do

Build idea 1 as the core, with two borrowed pieces, and keep 6 in reserve.

  1. Draft Lists and Awaiting review come off the rail. They live on their own pages, and the band above the grid keeps the day's Draft Lists in view.
  2. One tabbed work card: To-do (warnings plus No Contract rows) and Notices. This is the unification you asked for, and it keeps notices apart, as the catalogue requires.
  3. Two status tiles above it: Books square and pre-payments due. Reconciliation's detail moves to a click-through.
  4. From idea 7: hide what's empty, and fold Day notes to one line until it has something in it.
  5. From idea 5, later: a This day / All dates switch on the work card once the whole-practice data makes the lists long.
  6. Apply the word changes above, in the rail and in the side nav, so the counts read the same in both places.

If the rail still feels crowded after a workshop with real volume, that's the cue for idea 6 (the Office desk). Idea 1's tabs would carry straight across to it.

Your calls before I build

Notices in a tab, or kept as their own card? A tab is cleaner. Its own card is closer to "a panel beside the to-do list".

Catalogue tidy-up for Draft Lists: US-01.6.2 gives "space reserved beside the day view" only as an example. The band above the grid and the Draft Lists page meet its criteria. I'd drop the example from the story so it doesn't read as a rail card.

Rename Notifications to Notices everywhere, nav and page included?

Pre-payments awaiting approval: To-do rows, or only behind the tile?

If any of this changes a story's wording, I'd record it with /update-requirements first, then build it as a routine app change.
