Admin Web App, Contracts. Brainstorm for the office, 25 November 2026.

Every Procedure is priced by a Contract. The office finds them under Master data.

A Contract decides whether a choice shows on the step rail, where the invoice goes, what the Procedure costs until it is held, whether it needs a prepayment, and what prints on the invoice. At go-live there will be about 340 of them. Today they sit third in the Billing group of the Master data sub-nav, edited in a side panel over a 1,180px table.

Below: what hangs off a Contract, what the office has today, four ways to give Contracts a page of their own, and what I would build first.

What the office has today

Built across catch-up phases 18 to 25a and 39b (ContractsView.tsx, ContractEditSheet.tsx). The parts work; what is missing is a place for them to live, and a view of the Contract in use.

Works today

The listOne search over name, holder reference and AA code. Active, Disabled or All. Holder and funding tag filters. Rows under holder headings, No contract · RVG first.
The editorA wide side panel with Details and History: codes, holder and who it bills, where it is offered, pricing, price ranges, event fees, and Replaces.
Change warningsReplacing or disabling lists the Procedures still on the old Contract; a backdated range lists the authorised ones that kept their price (D79).
System ContractsNo contract · RVG and the fixed price backstops, read-only except name, AA code and wording.

Missing

A page and a linkA Contract opens in a modal panel over the list, so it can't be linked from a Procedure, a review flag or the audit, and the rest of the app is blocked while it is open.
The Contract in useNowhere shows which Bookings sit on a Contract, which are locked, or what re-prices when a range starts.
Where it appearsThe office can't see which step rail path a Contract lands under, or why it isn't offered on a given List.
Replace from a dateA dated change is three steps by hand: create, warn, then disable the old one. The S5 script already promises one step (backlog).
What's comingPrice ranges that start next week are visible only by opening each Contract.
Room to readCapped at 1,180px beside a 190px sub-nav; nine columns wrap (layout audit).

Four ideas

Each puts Contracts in the side nav. They differ in what the office lands on and how it gets around. The mocks are drawn at the app's real sizes (216px nav, 28px inset, 412px rail) with seed data; scroll them sideways on a small screen.

1

List and record

The plain promotion. Contracts gets a full-width list page in the layout audit's fit mode (sticky header, pager in the card footer), and every Contract gets its own routed record page, /admin/contracts/DOYLE-001, in place of the side panel. The record page shows the settings, a price timeline, and a rail with the Bookings on it and its history. Parties and Funding tags move with it as routed tabs, because they only exist to shape Contracts.

What it fixes

  • One click to Contracts, and a URL for every Contract: review flags, the Booking's Billing card and the Audit can link straight to it.
  • The page isn't modal, so the office can keep a Contract open while working a Booking.
  • "In use" answers the question the office asks before every change: who does this touch?
  • Full width ends the nine-column wrap.

What it costs

  • The panel's sections move into cards; the store calls, warnings and History stay as they are.
  • A new selector, Procedures by Contract. The 25a warning lists already compute most of it.
  • Master data's sub-nav loses three entries and gains a link across.
  • Recipes and demo guide need a sweep afterwards.

Catalogue: fits US-04.1.2 to US-04.1.5, US-04.2.15, US-13.4.1 ("in one place" still holds if Master data links across).

Design: Admin Review's record layout; the layout audit's fit list and fluid record with a 412px rail.

New: the "In use" list and the price timeline. Both read existing data.

2

Holder schedules

The page is organised the way contracts arrive at AA: as one holder's schedule. Holders sit in a left column, grouped by kind. Picking one shows its Party details (billing name, delivery) above its Contracts, grouped by funding tag, the same shape as the CES SXAP schedule of 1 Apr 2026. Because a schedule changes all at once, the office can select many Contracts and add the same dated price range to every one.

What it fixes

  • Matches how the office thinks of a contract: "the CES SXAP schedule", not 56 separate items.
  • Holder details and their Contracts on one screen, so the Parties page is no longer a separate trip.
  • An annual schedule increase becomes one action, not 56 panel edits.

What it costs

  • A third column makes Special holders with one Contract feel heavy; a cross-holder search is still needed.
  • The bulk range needs its own warnings: kept prices across 56 Contracts, and one audit change set.
  • Close to the export and re-import that D34 parked as Future Work.

Catalogue: the holder view fits US-04.1.5 and US-04.2.1.

New story needed: "Add one price range to many Contracts". It sits next to US-04.2.13 (export and re-import, Future Work), and Greg's second-person check on large imports is still open there.

Ask AA: how often does a whole schedule move on one date? CES's 1 Apr and Merivale's 1 Jun suggest yearly.

3

Through the step rail

The catalogue arranged the way it is used: the step rail's three steps as columns. Where does the invoice go, then the funding tag or payment type, then the Contracts. A "Try a List" bar sets a hospital, surgeon, anaesthetist and date, and the columns show exactly what the rail would offer on that List, with a list of what it leaves out and why. This answers the office's commonest Contract question, "why can't Dr Ngata see it?". Click through the columns below; they are live.

What it fixes

  • Shows the catalogue as anaesthetists and the office meet it, so gaps show up: a hospital and tag with only backstops under it.
  • "Why isn't it offered?" gets an answer in words, without opening six Contracts.
  • Also useful for training: a new office person learns the three steps here.

What it costs

  • A reasons list for each filter the rail applies (link, holder, specialty, date, status). The rail filters already exist; they would need to say why.
  • As a whole page it's weak for editing. It should sit beside a list (idea 1) or a desk (idea 4).

Catalogue: reads US-04.3.2, US-04.3.8 and US-04.3.9 without changing them.

Mind: the catalogue and the domain model disagree on the backstops. US-04.3.9 gives each hospital and tag an RVG backstop; the model lists only the fixed price one. This view would make whichever answer AA picks visible, which is a reason to settle it first.

4

Contracts desk

Land on the work, not the catalogue. The first tab shows what is about to change or has gone wrong: price ranges starting soon, Bookings still on a Disabled Contract, fixed prices with no range in force, and Contracts that anaesthetists have changed. The catalogue is the second tab. It comes with one guided flow the S5 script already promises: replace a Contract from a date. That flow copies the Contract, changes the setting, lists the Bookings to move, and disables the old one, all in one sheet.

What it fixes

  • The things that go wrong with Contracts (a price that starts next week, a Booking left on a Disabled one) come to the office instead of waiting for review day.
  • The commonest dated change is one sheet, with nothing to forget.
  • The nav badge is earned: it counts Bookings that need a Contract fixed.

What it costs

  • Another badge in the side nav, and an overlap with the Day rail's To-do and the Review queue's flags, so they need one source.
  • The replace flow moves Bookings when the office ticks them. That is office action, not "moving by itself" (D79), but it should be said out loud.
  • "Disable on a date" puts a date on a Contract, which D77 avoided.

Catalogue: desk cards read US-04.1.2, US-04.2.15, US-07.2.2 and the OQ-135 review flag.

Edit: US-04.1.2 gains "replace from a date" as one action.

Ruling: a scheduled disable, or keep "disable now" only.

Backlog: closes "Build what S5 promises: a new Contract disabling the old one".

What I'd do

Start with idea 1. It is the base every other idea needs (a side nav entry, a URL per Contract, room to read), and it needs no new requirement. Then add the best parts of the others as tabs, in order of value:

  1. Contracts in the side nav, the list at full width, and a record page with the price timeline and "On this Contract". Holders and Funding tags move across as tabs; Master data keeps a link.
  2. "Replace from a date" from idea 4, using "disable now" only, so it needs no ruling. It closes the S5 backlog item.
  3. "Where invoices go" with Try a List from idea 3, as a tab. It is cheap because the rules already exist, and it is the best demo moment of the four.
  4. The desk from idea 4, once the warning sources are one list shared with the Day rail.
  5. Bulk price ranges from idea 2: only if AA say whole schedules move on one date. Ask at the next workshop; it needs a story first.

Questions for you before building

  • Do Parties and Funding tags move under Contracts, or stay in Master data with a link both ways?
  • Where should Contracts sit in the nav: after Ledger as drawn, or beside Invoices?
  • Should the record page's "Edit" open the sections in place, or keep the panel for editing and use the page for reading?
  • Is a scheduled disable worth taking to AA, or is "disable now" enough?
  • Should the anaesthetist get a read-only view of their own price list on the same pattern? It's in the backlog for mobile.
