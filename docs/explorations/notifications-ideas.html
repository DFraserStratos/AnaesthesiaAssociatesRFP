Why the Notifications page feels noisy

The page holds a short, simple thing: what anaesthetists moved or asked the office to check this morning. It reads harder than the Lists and Intake tables beside it because each row packs everything into one bold sentence, then adds three styles of action around it. This page draws three refreshes on this morning's seeded notices so you can compare them a screen at a time.

One bold sentence holds six facts

Who, what they did, the date and session, the hospital, the surgeon and who it went to are all in one bold line, so nothing stands out. On a wide screen the line runs about 140 characters.

The same thing said twice

"By" repeats the name the sentence opens with. "When" repeats "Tue 24 Nov" on every row. The returned List says "Open Draft List" twice in one row.

Three kinds of action per row

An outlined Resolve button, a text link to open, and a big outlined email button inside the text. Each row is a different height and its actions sit in different places.

Nothing says what kind it is

A check request needs the office to do something. A move is mostly worth knowing. Both look the same until you read the whole sentence.

What the other admin tables do

Facts in columns

Draft Lists and Intake put session, hospital and surgeon in their own columns: bold hospital, slate surgeon. Rows are one or two lines.

Status as a tinted pill

"Unassigned" and "Hospital closed" are pills, amber when something needs doing. You can scan the column without reading the rows.

Days as bands

Draft Lists and Intake group rows under a day band ("Wed 25 Nov · 5 Draft Lists"), so each row only needs a time.

Text actions on the right

Draft Lists ends each row with Assign, Change date, Remove as plain teal words. Payments ends with Review. Outlined buttons are kept for page-level actions.

The three ideas

AColumns, like Draft Lists

Break the sentence into the columns the Lists tabs already use: what kind, which List, from whom to whom, and the message. Rows sit under a day band. Actions are words on the right, with a round tick to resolve.

For

  • Reads like the Draft Lists and Intake tables beside it: same columns, pills and day bands.
  • The kind pill tells a check request from a move at a glance; only check requests are amber.
  • Every row is one or two lines high, and every action sits in the same place.
  • Active and Resolved become pills, like Draft Lists' Waiting and History, instead of a checkbox.

Against

  • A long message is cut at two lines; you open the List to read the rest.
  • "Dr Karaka to Dr Zhou" loses the sentence's "when marking the session not available". It moves into the pill's wording ("Handed on, unwell").

What changes

  • The Day rail keeps the notice sentence; the page shows the notice's parts in columns instead.
  • "Draft cover-change email" becomes the text action "Email".
  • The returned List's hint line goes; "Assign" opens the Draft List.
Build size

BA quiet feed

Keep the sentence, but short and in regular weight, with the List's details on a second line and the message as a quote beneath. Resolved notices move to a rail on the right, like To authorise's "Recently authorised".

For

  • The closest to today's page: the same content, much easier to read.
  • Messages show in full. A check request's note gets an amber edge.
  • Who resolved what is always in view, without a filter.

Against

  • Fewer notices fit on a screen: rows with a message run three or four lines.
  • A feed is a new pattern in the admin app; every other list is a table.
  • The rail shows only today's resolved notices; older ones need a link.
Build size

CRows and a detail pane

One line per notice, with nothing to click in the row. Selecting a row opens it in a pane on the right, with the message, the List as it is now, and the actions: Resolve as the main button, the email and Open beside it.

For

  • The calmest table: every row is one line, so the whole morning fits on a screen.
  • The pane has room to say where the List is now (it may have moved again since).
  • Resolve is one clear button, after you have read the notice.

Against

  • Two clicks to resolve instead of one.
  • No other admin list has a pane like this; Lists opens a drawer or a page.
  • The most new code of the three.
Build size

Decide

My pick: A, columns like Draft Lists

The page's trouble is that it doesn't look like its neighbours. A uses the patterns the office already reads every day on Lists and Intake: columns, a kind pill, day bands and text actions. It keeps Resolve one click away.

From B, I'd borrow the amber edge on a check request's note if two lines turn out to be too few.
