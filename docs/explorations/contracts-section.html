Admin Web App, Contracts. Design brief, 25 November 2026. First pass: four ideas

Contracts gets its own section. A catalogue you can group, an address book of Parties, and a page for each Contract.

The section takes idea 1's side nav entry and a page for every Contract, idea 2's grouping (by holder, kind, funding tag or surgeon) and idea 4's single-step "Replace from a date". Parties and Funding tags become tabs. This version drops the desk and the step rail test screen, explains how Contracts, Parties and funding tags fit together, and shows price ranges as a table that will still read well after ten years of changes. It is the design brief for build phases 45 to 49.

Contracts and Parties

Yes: the address book idea from the 23 and 28 Oct sessions with Greg still stands. The Parties are that address book, and every Contract picks one of them as its holder. The model has tightened since then in a few places, set out in the table below. In short, a Party is who: a name and where their invoices go. A Contract is what and how much: one priced item, and whether it bills its Party or the patient's payer.

The terms are drawn in AR-44 (Billable Party Terms); the rules are in the domain model under "Patient, payer and billable party".

Funding tags and the step rail

A funding tag is a short label from a list AA keeps (SXAP, HNZ, ACC) saying who funds the work a Contract covers. It exists for one situation: a hospital that does the same operation for several funders, at a different price for each, and is billed for all of them. The tag sorts that hospital's Contracts into one pile per funder, so the step rail can ask "which funder?" before it shows a long list of prices.

Where a Contract lands on the step rail

Nobody chooses a Contract's place on the step rail. It is worked out from two of its settings, who it bills and its funding tag, plus what kind of Party holds it. The tag only counts in the middle case.

Things that may catch people out

  • A hospital's untagged Contracts sit under Special, not under the hospital. St George's plain RVG Contract is under Special › St George's. Under the rules this is right, but staff may look for it under St George's first. It's worth asking Ben.
  • An ACC tag on a surgeon group does not put it under ACC. Canterbury Orthopaedic Surgeons' ACC Contract bills the group, so it sits under Special › Canterbury Orthopaedic Surgeons.
  • Backstops disagree in the catalogue. US-04.3.9 gives each hospital and tag an RVG backstop as well as the Fixed price one; the domain model has only the Fixed price one.
  • Not yet confirmed. Tags come from Donald's decisions of 5 Nov (D4, D7), still at Verify. AA and Greg haven't confirmed them.

The groupings

  • Holder: idea 2's view. Sub-headed by funding tag.
  • Kind: Hospitals, Insurers, Funders, Surgeon groups, Surgeons, Anaesthetists. Sub-headed by holder.
  • Funding tag: SXAP, HNZ, ACC, No tag. Sub-headed by holder.

How it behaves

  • The grouping and the chosen group live in the URL, so a review flag can link to "Funding tag › ACC". The grouping is remembered for each person.
  • Search runs across every group, and the index counts update to show where the matches are.
  • A row opens the Contract's page. No checkboxes and no bulk actions: every change is made on one Contract.
  • A "next price" chip shows when a later range is already set.

Catalogue: US-04.1.2 already asks for an Active filter and a Holder filter. "Group by" extends that story; the Holder filter becomes one grouping.

Seed: 32 Contracts drawn from the seed here; the real list is about 340, which is why the index pane earns its space.

Not doing: adding one price range to many Contracts. A schedule's yearly rise is entered Contract by Contract.

Parties moves from Master data

A Party is a holder, insurer or funder. Its details (billing name, where invoices go, code prefix) shape every invoice billed under its Contracts, so it belongs next to them. The list is grouped by kind, like idea 2's holder column. Parties with no Contracts still show, because insurers and funders are also chosen as a Booking's funder. A Party's page shows its details, edited in place, and its Contracts counted by funding tag, each count linking to the grouped catalogue.

The price ranges table

  • One row per range: from, to, price, the change on the one before, and how many Procedures it priced. Ten years of yearly changes is ten rows, most of them folded.
  • "To" is worked out, never typed: the day before the next range starts.
  • "Correct" fixes a range's price or start date in place, with the same warning as adding one. The last range can't be removed.
  • Only a fixed price has ranges. An RVG Contract shows its rate or discount in this card instead, undated.

What changed since the last version

  • The timeline is gone. A table holds any number of ranges and is plain to build.
  • "Who it bills" is its own card, showing what comes from the Party, so the office sees where the name and email live.
  • Each card still has its own Edit and saves as one audited change.

Catalogue: US-04.1.2, US-04.1.3 (audit), US-04.2.1, US-04.2.15, US-04.2.18. No change to what a Contract is.

Mock data: the ranges before 1 Apr 2026, the patient names and the counts are made up to show scale. At go-live only current prices are loaded, so the table starts with one or two rows.

Updating a Contract

Four ways to change a Contract, matched to the flat model's rules. A setting with no date (where it is offered, codes and wording) is edited in place. A fixed price changes by adding a price range. Anything else that must start on a date is a new Contract, via Replace from a date. Stopping a Contract is Disable. Every one of them shows who it touches before it saves (D79), and nothing moves on its own.

Which change is which

  • Edit in place: where it is offered, invoice and codes, the anaesthetist's override, who it bills. No date, so it applies now.
  • Add a price range: a new row at the top of the price ranges table, from a date, backdating included. Correcting a range edits its row the same way.
  • Replace from a date: a rate, a discount, the holder, the pricing kind, prepaid.
  • Disable: it stops being offered. Make active undoes it.

Changed since the first pass

  • The replace sheet drops "disable on a date", so no ruling is needed. "Leave it active" is the default; a To-do on the Day rail reminds the office on the day.
  • The catch: a rate, a discount or a setting change has no date, so the new Contract is offered from the day it is saved. The sheet says so. A scheduled disable would close the gap, which makes it a real question for AA.
  • The sheet can change any setting, not just the price, because that is what a dated change means in the flat model.
  • Disable is a red-outlined button in a dialog. It is the only change that takes a Contract off the rail.

Catalogue: US-04.1.2 gains "Replace from a date" as one action. Moving the ticked Bookings is the office's own act, which D79 ("nothing moves by itself") allows; the story should say so.

Backlog: closes "Build what S5 promises: a new Contract disabling the old one".

Open: whether ranges may be backdated is still disputed (Greg, 23 Oct). The form allows it, as the build does today.
