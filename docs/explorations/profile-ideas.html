Profile page · option CThe header bar, 27 November 2026
← → or 1 to 6 to switch

1Cells share the width

The bar keeps its full width, and every cell stretches to an equal share, so the last one ends at the bar's edge. "Active since" gets its own cell, so the Status cell drops back to one line.

For

  • The smallest change: one rule, and nothing hangs off an edge.
  • Lines up as a row of equal columns, like a summary table.

Against

  • Short values ("Tier 2") sit in wide cells with a lot of space after them.
  • The anaesthetist bar has six cells and the surgeon bar five, so the two pages' columns don't match.
  • A long practice name gets squeezed into an equal share.

2The bar hugs its content

The bar is only as wide as its facts, so its last divider is its own edge. "Make inactive" (or "Retire" for a surgeon) sits at the far end of the same row, filling the space with something useful. "Since 9 Jun 2014" goes on the Status line in smaller type.

For

  • Every cell is as wide as its value; nothing is padded out.
  • The status action sits right beside the status, where the office looks for it.
  • One line high on both pages.

Against

  • The bar's width changes from person to person (a long practice name makes it wider).
  • The status action moves out of the Status card; the card goes, or keeps only the history of last days.

3Contact on the right

The record facts sit on the left. Phone and email move to the right end as contact links (a phone and a mail icon), with no divider before them, so they read as the bar's own right-hand end rather than another cell. "Make inactive" (or "Retire") sits at the right of the name row, level with the name and above the contact links. "Since" goes on the Status line.

For

  • Both ends of the bar are anchored, and the bar keeps its full width.
  • The phone number is the thing the office does something with: it now looks like a link, not a fact.
  • On a surgeon, the office contact is the one emails go to first (US-13.6.5), so it belongs there.
  • The status action has the name row's empty right end to itself, the usual place for a page's main action, and the Status card below can go.

Against

  • A tel: or mailto: link from a desktop app may do nothing useful; a copy button may suit better.
  • The Contact card below repeats the phone and email.

4No box

Drop the bar's border. The facts sit as plain label and value pairs in the header row, to the right of the name, so there's no edge to hang off. "Active since 9 Jun 2014" becomes the Status value's own words.

For

  • The calmest header: one row instead of two, so the usual week moves up about 70px.
  • Nothing to align, so nothing can look unfinished.

Against

  • The facts compete with the name for the eye.
  • Five facts beside a long name won't fit on a laptop; they wrap under it.

5One header card

The name, avatar and status action sit in one card, and the facts run along its foot as a tinted strip. The strip ends with a divider after the last fact, and the rest is just the card's footer, so it reads as finished. "Active since" gets its own cell, so the strip stays one line.

For

  • The empty space belongs to a card, so it reads as layout, not a gap.
  • The header becomes one object, like a contact card: who, how they stand, the action.
  • Cells stay as wide as their values; the two pages look alike however many facts each has.

Against

  • One more card on a page of cards; the header carries a bit more weight than today.
  • A little taller than idea 2.

Decide

My pick: 5, one header card

Idea 5 solves both problems without trade-offs. The empty space at the end of the strip sits inside a card's footer, so nothing hangs. "Active since" gets its own short cell, so the strip stays one line. And the Make inactive / Retire action gets a natural home in the card's top corner.

Idea 2 is close behind, and is the one I'd take if the header should stay lighter.
